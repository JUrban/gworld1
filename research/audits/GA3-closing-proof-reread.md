## The scale and collapse

The proof treats an arbitrary finite set of words by passing to a
finitely generated **nonabelian** subgroup K containing every syllable
and two noncommuting elements. Neither G nor its ordered length group
is assumed finitely generated. Scalar extension to the divisible hull
preserves positive translation lengths and hence freeness.

For a finite symmetric generating set S, the maximum L of the lengths
of S and S squared is positive. The disjoint-axis product formula bounds
each bridge between generator axes by L/2. Finite Helly applied to their
closed L-neighbourhoods gives a basepoint with all generator displacements
at most 3L. Thus the orbit hull has distances in the principal convex
subgroup A generated by L. This step avoids assuming that the original
ordered group has a largest proper convex subgroup.

The infinitesimals relative to L form the largest proper convex subgroup
I of A. The quotient A/I is Archimedean and divisible, so its real image
is dense. An element of S or S squared whose translation length is L
has an axis intersecting the orbit hull: its axis projection lies between
its forward and backward translates of the basepoint. It survives the
collapse as a hyperbolic element. The collapsed action therefore does
not acquire a global fixed point merely from the choice of scale.

Guirardel's remark after Fact 5.1 expressly extends the arc/tripod
stabilizer conclusion to **every** ordered abelian length group and
**every** convex subgroup. Filling segments of the dense Archimedean
tree introduces no branch points; every new nondegenerate fixed arc or
tripod contains an old one. The real action has abelian arc fixators
and trivial tripod fixators. Closed-axis/projection and elliptic versus
hyperbolic facts are used as supplied by the cited tree preliminaries;
metric completeness is not silently imposed.

CSA and torsion-freeness exclude a nontrivial abelian normal subgroup
of nonabelian K. An invariant line would have abelian kernel; after
excluding that kernel, torsion-freeness makes its isometry image a group
of translations. A fixed end likewise gives the Busemann homomorphism:
zero-displacement elements fix terminal rays and commute pairwise on a
common ray, so its kernel is abelian. Either case would make K abelian.
These implications justify the non-elementary boundary action used next.

## The finite separator

The supplement replaces the earlier endpoint-density import. Two
independent hyperbolics exist: otherwise the pairwise-intersecting family
of translates of one endpoint pair forces an invariant end, pair, or
three-point set; a power of a hyperbolic cannot fix three ends.

A nonidentity element fixes at most two ends, since three would give a
fixed tripod. For the finite syllable set C, one of the 2|C|+1 distinct
ends a^k b-positive is therefore fixed by none of C. A sufficiently small
half-tree V about that end satisfies cV disjoint from V for every c in C.
Conjugating a second independent hyperbolic far along its axis places
both of its ends inside V. Axis projection shows that this works for
both ends at once; it is not an unproved compact-boundary assertion.

Two disjoint half-trees along the resulting axis supply attracting and
repelling sets. A sufficiently large translation power sends the full
complement of one into the other and preserves each relevant half-tree.
The same power bound works for all syllables and all the finitely many
words. Boundary ping-pong detects every cyclically reduced alternating
core; single-factor words and conjugating prefixes are handled separately.
Finally the identity/inner-conjugation factor maps extend from K to all
of G by their explicit formulas.
