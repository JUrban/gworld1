## Leading factors and exact periods

- Exact determinant-one pair moves preserve the group commutator,
  so dependent equal-weight leading terms can be reduced until their
  bracket is nonzero. A nontrivial target prevents termination with a
  trivial factor. All resulting weights satisfy p+q equal to the target's
  first nonzero degree.
- For unequal weights, the bracket has no zero divisors even after
  algebraic closure. The centre of its projective linear projection
  misses the Segre variety. Any positive-dimensional projective fibre
  would meet the centre hyperplane in the fibre's ambient linear space;
  hence fibres are finite. Normalizing one first-factor coordinate gives
  zero-dimensional affine charts. The rational-point algorithm retains
  all signed integral scale divisors, not just a primitive first factor.
- At equal weights, associative block splitting makes the exterior
  bracket map injective. Saturating the two-dimensional support and
  enumerating oriented Hermite lattices retains all index allocations.
- In each correction-kernel use of the inner-solution theorem, the
  coefficients are placed in an actual homogeneous free basis of the
  specified graded tail algebra. Independence modulo its derived algebra
  and positive-degree induction justify that inclusion. The theorem is
  not applied to arbitrary ambient coefficients.
- Formal universal substitutions fix the full BCH logarithm of the
  commutator. Clearing denominators for both positive and negative powers
  produces reversible integral pair substitutions. Their first increments
  span a finite-index sublattice of the integral kernel; every residue
  remains. They need not be automorphisms of the ambient nilpotent group.

## The first exceptional block

The quadratic coefficient must survive after **all** later columns have
been allowed, not merely after the last homogeneous correction image.
The full-block argument was checked with that stronger requirement.

Before offset 2t, two still-variable increments cannot interact, so the
joint solution set is an affine integer lattice. Its first-exception
coordinate is either fixed or ranges over k_0+bT with the actual nonzero
integer step b. After the fixed rational prefix normalization, lower
first-factor components vanish. This normalization is only a proof device;
the algorithm continues solving the original integral coordinate systems.

The projection algebra contains all of the first varying homogeneous
component, including particular solutions. Weight bounds make every
projected first-factor component through offset 2t have at most one E
letter. The largest E-letter component of D yields a nonzero restricted
quadratic term by the positional lemma; its scalar E_0 exception is
excluded by q>p+t.

The coefficients of T in every earlier satisfied block equation give
the triangular convolution. Starting from the zero top E-letter
component of the leading D, it annihilates the relevant fixed Z terms
below t. Every other parameter begins later than t and can pair only
with those lower fixed terms, or with the leading C term, whose delta
image vanishes under the restriction. Thus the same functional kills
every later column while remaining nonzero on the T-squared coefficient.
Terms with two Y occurrences may be nonzero, but their parameter-dependent
parts start too late; the proof subtracts their fixed parts.

Solving the resulting scalar quadratic retains at most two integer
first-parameter values. Restarting after fixing that prefix can forget
later necessary restrictions without losing a solution: later equations
are checked again, and a positive output must satisfy the full original
commutator. Each restart fixes a strictly greater offset. If the remaining
class ends before 2t, the whole remaining system is linear. These steps
give the stated finite recursion rather than an unbounded parameter search.
