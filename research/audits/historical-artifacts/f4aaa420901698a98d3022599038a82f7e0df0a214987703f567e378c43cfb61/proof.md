# N5: deciding direct decomposability with torsion

Candidate argument, 28 September 2026. First route recorded approximately 12:18–12:20 UTC; proof and implementation audit completed through 12:43 UTC. This covers the whole entry, including groups with torsion. It is a candidate awaiting independent review; novelty is provisional.

## Statement and conventions

**Proposed theorem.** There is a uniform algorithm which, given a finite presentation of a finitely generated nilpotent group G, decides whether G=A x B with A,B nontrivial, and produces finite generating sets for such factors if they exist.

The input is promised to define a nilpotent group. The theorem does not assert recognition of nilpotency among arbitrary finite presentations. The trivial group has no decomposition with two nontrivial factors. Group commutators are x^-1 y^-1 x y. Central abelian groups are written additively when solving linear systems.

The original [N5](https://shpilrain.ccny.cuny.edu/gworld/problems/probnil.html) asks for decidability for finitely generated nilpotent groups without a torsion-free restriction. The full archived HTML, exact fragment, and rendered N5 statement were inspected; evidence is in `research/statement-audits/N5/`.

## 1. Established effective and structural ingredients

We use the usual effective calculus for finitely generated nilpotent groups: conversion to consistent polycyclic presentations; finite presentations and generators for subgroups, kernels and intersections; membership; centre and finite torsion subgroup; quotient operations; and equality of finitely generated subgroups. These follow from standard polycyclic-group algorithms. Only promised nilpotent inputs are used.

For a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group Q, its rational Malcev completion R and the corresponding finite-dimensional rational nilpotent Lie algebra can be computed. Rational direct decomposition into indecomposable factors is effective. Intersections of Q with specified rational subgroups, and the induced kernels, are effective by rational Malcev collection. These are established ingredients, not new claims here.

The structural ingredient needed is the following form of rational decomposition uniqueness. If

    R=R_1 x ... x R_m

is a decomposition into nontrivial rationally indecomposable factors, every decomposition R=A^* x B^* matches a partition I,J of {1,...,m} modulo Z(R):

    A^* Z(R)=R_I Z(R),   B^* Z(R)=R_J Z(R).

Empty parts are allowed. For a direct primary source, [Fisher–Gray–Hydon, arXiv:1303.3376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3376), Theorem 3.3, matches indecomposable Lie summands N_i,M_i with projection onto M_i surjective and N_i contained in M_i plus the centre of the complementary summand. Hence N_i+Z(L)=M_i+Z(L); regrouping gives precisely the displayed assertion. The Section 3 proof is linear-algebraic and applies over Q, despite the real low-dimensional classification in the title. Its argument was checked separately here.

The rational group formulation is also Proposition 18 and Corollary 19 of [Baumslag–Miller–Ostheimer, arXiv:1510.05632](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.05632). Their rational decomposition algorithm is described around Proposition 13, citing de Graaf, *Lie algebras: theory and algorithms*, Section 1.15. We use these ingredients, rather than their later arbitrary-lift criterion. Their paper supplies the prior torsion-free decidability result and explicitly asks about torsion.

## 2. A finite list of possible decompositions modulo the centre

Compute T=T(G), Z=Z(G), Q=G/T and its rational completion R. Let

    K=G/Z,   S=T(K),   kappa:K -> R/Z(R).

The homomorphism kappa is induced by G -> Q -> R. We first prove ker(kappa)=S.

The centre of Q is the intersection Q intersect Z(R). Indeed, an element commuting with Q commutes with its rational completion, since roots are unique. Let C be its preimage in G. Thus ker(kappa)=C/Z.

There is a characteristic torsion-free subgroup U=G^e of finite index, where G^e denotes the subgroup generated by all e-th powers. To obtain one effectively, use residual finiteness of G to find a finite quotient injective on its finite torsion subgroup T. Its kernel H is torsion-free, normal and of finite index. Take e to be the exponent of the finite quotient. Then G^e is contained in H, is characteristic, and is torsion-free. Its index is finite because the finitely generated nilpotent quotient G/G^e has bounded exponent and hence is finite.

If c is in C, then [c^e,g] belongs both to T and to the normal subgroup U for every g in G. It is therefore trivial. Thus c^e is central, and C/Z is torsion. Conversely, if c^k is central, the image of c in the torsion-free group Q has a central power. Unique roots in Q imply that this image is central. Hence C/Z is precisely T(K), as asserted; it is finite. The proof also supplies a computable bound, although the algorithm may simply compute T(K) directly.

Compute an indecomposable rational decomposition of R. For each ordered partition I,J, form

    K_1=kappa^-1(R_I Z(R)/Z(R)),
    K_2=kappa^-1(R_J Z(R)/Z(R)).

These are effective finitely generated subgroups of K: equivalently, take the preimages in G of Q intersect R_I Z(R) and Q intersect R_J Z(R), then divide by Z. The rational-subgroup intersection operation of Section 1 applies. Each K_i contains S.

Suppose G=A x B is an actual direct decomposition matching the partition. Its images

    Y_1=AZ/Z,   Y_2=BZ/Z

give K=Y_1 x Y_2. Moreover K_i=Y_i S. To check the latter assertion, write an element of G as ab. If its rational image belongs to A^* Z(R), the rational image of b is central in B^*, and hence central in R; the image of b in K is therefore in S. The converse inclusion is immediate. Consequently

    [K_i:Y_i] <= |S|.

Every finitely generated group has only finitely many subgroups of index at most a specified integer. These can be enumerated effectively from a finite presentation: enumerate all generator assignments to each finite symmetric group of degree at most the bound, test relators and transitivity, and recover point stabilizers by Schreier rewriting. Apply this to K_1 and K_2 with bound |S|. For every pair retain it only if it satisfies

    K=Y_1 Y_2,   [Y_1,Y_2]=1,   Y_1 intersect Y_2=1.

These are decidable subgroup tests. Thus we have a finite, complete list of possible direct decompositions of K induced by decompositions of G. The list may contain decompositions which do not lift, and may contain duplicates.

This step includes finite groups: when Q=1, R=1 and K=S is finite, so the same bound includes every subgroup of K. It also includes central factors: a central factor has trivial image Y_i, so trivial Y_i and empty partitions must be retained.

## 3. Translating a proposed lift into abelian splitting constraints

For a retained pair K=Y_1 x Y_2, let X_i be the full preimage of Y_i in G. Then

    X_1 X_2=G,   X_1 intersect X_2=Z.

If [X_1,X_2] is nontrivial, discard the pair. This test uses finitely many generators. It is necessary because any desired factors have X_1=AZ and X_2=BZ, and central changes cannot affect commutators across the two groups.

We may therefore assume that X_1 and X_2 commute. Choose finite presentations

    Y_i=<y_i1,...,y_is | r_i1,...,r_im>

and lifts x_ij in X_i. Each relator evaluates to a central element v_il in Z. Let E_i be its integer matrix of exponent sums. If the generator lifts are changed from x_ij to x_ij z_j, the defect vector becomes

    v_i + E_i z.

This equality is exact, since every z_j is central.

Fix a central splitting Z=Z_1 direct-sum Z_2. There exists A_i<=X_i with A_i Z=X_i and A_i intersect Z=Z_i precisely when the central extension

    1 -> Z/Z_i -> X_i/Z_i -> Y_i -> 1

has a section. Sections are exactly the choices of modified lifts whose relator defects vanish modulo Z_i. Therefore the required condition is solvability of E_i z=-v_i over Z/Z_i.

Take an integer Smith decomposition U E_i V=D_i, and apply the row transformation U to v_i. A zero row gives a condition v in Z_i. A positive diagonal entry d gives

    v in Z_i+dZ.

Unimodular row and column operations are valid over every abelian group, including finite ones. Hence the equivalence holds with torsion and with finite-order quotient generators. Diagonal entries 1 impose no restriction.

We are left with a finite list of constraints of the following form:

    find Z=Z_1 direct-sum Z_2 such that v in Z_i+dZ,
    with each d a nonnegative integer, and d=0 meaning v in Z_i.

Nontriviality of the resulting group factor requires Y_i!=1 or Z_i!=0. Section 4 decides these abelian constraints with these additional requirements.

Once a central splitting is found, the Smith calculation solves for modified generator lifts explicitly. A_i is generated by these modified lifts and Z_i. Its image modulo Z_i is a section, so A_i intersect Z=Z_i. The groups A_1,A_2 commute, intersect trivially and generate G; hence they give the required direct decomposition. These statements follow directly from the corresponding facts for X_i and for Z_1,Z_2.

## 4. Finite solution of the abelian splitting constraints

We prove the algorithm for an arbitrary finitely generated abelian group Z=F direct-sum T_0, where F is free of rank n and T_0 finite.

### 4.1 Separating finite torsion and free coordinates

Enumerate every direct decomposition T_0=T_1 direct-sum T_2. This is a finite operation, for example by enumerating its idempotent endomorphisms. Enumerate also every homomorphism beta:F->T_0, again a finite set. It defines a shear

    s_beta(f,t)=(f,t+beta(f)).

Every ordered direct decomposition Z=Z_1 direct-sum Z_2 is obtained by applying one of these shears to a product decomposition

    Z_1=A direct-sum T_1,   Z_2=B direct-sum T_2,
    F=A direct-sum B.

To prove completeness, take T_i=Z_i intersect T_0. The images of Z_i in F are complementary direct factors A,B. The free summands of Z_i are graphs over A,B with torsion values, modulo T_i; choose representative homomorphisms into the complementary torsion factor. Their sum extends to beta on A direct-sum B=F. This gives the asserted form.

For each torsion splitting and shear, apply s_beta^-1 to each constraint vector. A condition v in Z_i+dZ separates into its finite torsion condition t in T_i+dT_0 and its free condition f in A_i+dF. The finite condition is decidable by enumeration or Smith arithmetic. We now solve the free conditions.

### 4.2 Exact free containments

Let V_i be the lattice generated by the free vectors in the exact constraints for side i, and W_i its saturation in F. Since A and B must be direct summands, they must contain W_1 and W_2 respectively. Necessary conditions are

    W_1 intersect W_2=0,   W_1+W_2 is primitive in F.

They are also sufficient for the exact constraints. Compute these tests and a complement by integer Smith normal form. If they hold, fix

    F=W_1 direct-sum W_2 direct-sum C.

All free splittings containing W_i are obtained from the finitely many standard splittings

    A_k=W_1 direct-sum <c_1,...,c_k>,
    B_k=W_2 direct-sum <c_(k+1),...,c_d>,   0<=k<=d=rank C,

by automorphisms fixing W_1+W_2 pointwise. Indeed, for any such splitting, W_i is primitive in its assigned factor, so choose complements C_1,C_2 there and map the standard complement bases to bases of C_1,C_2. This is an automorphism fixing W_1 and W_2 pointwise.

### 4.3 The finite congruence search

In the displayed basis, the group Gamma of these automorphisms consists of matrices

    [ I 0 U ]
    [ 0 I V ]
    [ 0 0 W ],   W in GL_d(Z).

It has an explicit finite generating set: elementary shears from complement columns into W_1 and W_2, and signed permutations and elementary matrices on C. If d=0 it is trivial.

Let M be the least common multiple of all positive moduli in the remaining constraints, or 1 if there are none. Their truth depends only on Gamma modulo M. Enumerate this finite matrix-group image by closure under the generators, retaining a word and hence an integral lift for each image.

For each image and each k, form the associated projection P onto the transported A_k along B_k, modulo M. The constraint f in A+dF is equivalent to

    (1-P)f = 0 mod d,

and the side-two condition to Pf=0 mod d. Thus every condition is a finite congruence test. If one succeeds, its stored integral lift supplies the actual free splitting. If none succeeds, Section 4.2 proves that none exists. Required nontrivial central factors are checked by the free rank and torsion-factor size. All possible free ranks and all torsion splittings are included, so this filter cannot discard an alternative of the same congruence type which would be nontrivial.

Together with Section 4.1 this proves a terminating, constructive algorithm for the abelian splitting problem. There is no appeal to an unrestricted Diophantine decision procedure.

## 5. Termination, completeness and status

Section 2 gives a finite complete list, with the explicit index bound |T(G/Z(G))|. Sections 3 and 4 decide exactly which of these decompositions lift with two nontrivial factors. Every loop is finite or invokes one of the established effective nilpotent-group operations stated in Section 1. Positive answers construct the factors; if all branches fail, every possible group decomposition has been excluded.

The argument therefore proposes a positive answer to the whole of N5. An end-to-end implementation of the general rational decomposition and bounded-index subgroup stages is not claimed. The theorem invokes the established algorithms for those operations. The new lifting algorithm and complete examples with finite central quotient were tested as follows.

## 6. Exact checks, independent evaluations and limits

`scripts/n5_central_constraints.py` implements the centre-splitting decision, Smith translation of relator defects, and construction of corrected lifts. Its 120 deterministic cases, seed 9282613, include 60 comparisons with a separate finite subgroup-set enumeration, 45 comparisons with a separate finite-field projector enumeration, exact lattice obstructions, torsion shears, and split/nonsplit central extension controls. They passed in 3.88 seconds. GAP independently verified all 54 positive centre projections in 1.88 seconds; its log contains harmless top-level variable-scope syntax warnings, with no failed identity or membership check.

The complete quotient-branch and lifting pipeline was then applied to all 82 nilpotent groups of orders 4,6,8,9,12,16,27,32 in GAP's Small Groups library. Decisions agreed with GAP's separate `DirectFactorsOfGroup` routine. The lifting stage took 1.02 seconds, and GAP independently reconstructed and verified its 32 nontrivial direct decompositions in 1.83 seconds.

Seven infinite examples with finite central quotient also passed: G_1,G_2,G_3,G_4, G_2 x Z, G_2 x C_2, and G_3 x C_2, where G_m=<a,b,t | [a,b]=t, t^m=1, [a,t]=[b,t]=1>. The independent proof that G_m is indecomposable for m>=2 is in `audit.md`. The complete lifting decisions took 0.87 seconds; GAP reconstructed and verified all four positive decompositions in 1.88 seconds. These cases exercise a centre with both free and finite coordinates, including the noncoprime modulus four.

Three further products with nonabelian rational completion and noncentral finite torsion checked the support-preimage formula and finite index bound directly in GAP. Their six support indices were 4,1,4,1,4,4, against kernel sizes 4,4,4,4,16,16 respectively. The support subgroups were computed as kernels of projections to the other torsion-free quotient modulo its centre, then compared with the predicted Y_i T(K). This verifies concrete instances of the bound beyond the virtually abelian examples, not a general rational-decomposition implementation.
