## F34(a) and F38(a): the shared language construction

The following interfaces were checked against Diekert--Elder,
*Solutions to twisted word equations and equations in virtually free
groups*, [arXiv:1701.03297](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03297), archived
v6, Section 3.4, Definition 4.1, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4.

| Needed property | Source and application check |
| --- | --- |
| All solutions, not merely some witnesses | Theorem 4.3 identifies the full tuple solution relation with the accepted morphism images. Cancellation triangles preserve the projected full relation, including the generator images. |
| Counts in distinct components | The source provides one designated letter for each output variable. Applying the same incidence matrix separately to each seed retains those counts. Taking a single total Parikh vector would not. |
| Original alphabet and constraints | Accepted tuples are terminal words in the original alphabet. The finite recognizing monoid is part of the input; trivial twisting is allowed. Reducedness, cyclic reducedness and positivity are regular constraints. |
| Finite polynomial test | The lift contains all monomials through the required degree, including the constant. The statewise reachable span has finite dimension. Reversing control paths reconciles morphism composition with matrix application. |
| Actual witnesses | Spans are generated by actual accepted-path vectors. A nonzero functional on the span is nonzero on one retained path vector. No arbitrary linear combination is reported as a word. |

The determinant condition is sufficient for injectivity, rather than
equivalent to it: the image free subgroup has rank at most the number
of source generators and at least the nonzero abelianization rank;
Hopficity then applies. This condition also includes every automorphism.
For F38(a), KLSS Corollary 1.4, reread in the archived primary text,
is what permits equality under all injections to follow from equality
under all automorphisms. The determinant-times-length polynomial has
exactly the required universal-zero semantics. This is not a decision
for existence of a polynomial zero or for determinant one.

For F34(a), the subgroup graph belongs to the image of an injection.
Orienting each edge by its positive ambient label and collapsing a
spanning tree sends a positive closed path to a positive word in a free
basis of that image. Pullback along the injection is an isomorphism.
The argument permits infinite-index subgroups and a basepoint stem;
neither finite index nor a free-factor hypothesis is being inserted.

The full equation-to-language construction remains **unimplemented**.
The existing polynomial and graph tests, and the restricted Lean span
proof, do not verify that imported construction. No successful suite was
rerun for this audit. The old proofs' dated statements that F38(c) was
unresolved, and their historical partial-entry counting instructions,
remain historical: the current scope is in `reports/CURRENT_RESULTS.md`.

## F38(c): the graded-shortening application

Reread Sela, *Diophantine geometry over groups I*, Sections 9--10
through Lemma 10.4 and its local proof, with the quotient order and
constant-sequence conventions from Section 5 and the tripod argument
in Lemma 1.3. The publisher's [bibliographic record](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_2001__93__31_0/)
was reopened. No new edition or complete audit of the underlying
JSJ/Rips machinery is claimed.

1. For a filling word u in A, the pair G=A*<z>, P=<u,z> is relatively
   freely indecomposable. The intersection-of-free-factors argument
   also proves that every pointwise P-fixer preserves A. Thus minimizing
   over Aut(A/u) really gives shortness under the graded modular group.
2. In the nontrivial-JSJ case, a constant shortest embedding gives a
   canonical shortening quotient map that is an isomorphism. If eta is
   any other quotient map, eta composed with that isomorphism's inverse
   gives the required domination map. This proves uniqueness of the
   maximal class, not merely abstract isomorphism with some quotient.
   The trivial-JSJ case is rigid. The coefficient-free setting occurs
   explicitly in the source's examples.
3. Injectivity excludes a nontrivial relative **quotient** free-product
   factorization. An embedding in a larger free product with an unused
   factor would not be the quotient factorization in this definition.
   This convention is stated in the candidate rather than left implicit.
4. Failure of the linear bound gives shortest maps with
   M_m > 2^m(1+||f_m(u)||). Including u among the generators does not
   change shortness in this range. The source-ball inequality follows
   since the ball contains the basis. Printed page 90 confirms the
   exponential 2^m, which the text extraction flattens.
5. The based limit is not assumed faithful merely because the maps are
   injections. The displacement lower bound M_m/(|u|+1) excludes a global
   fixed point. The three displayed orbit points form a limiting tripod;
   Lemma 1.3(iii) then forces every action-kernel element eventually into
   the kernels of the injections. The quotient is therefore G itself,
   contradicting Lemma 10.4(ii).
6. The reduction from a proper free factor retains the ambient
   automorphism quantifier. The rank-two primitive case uses preservation
   of the commutator class to bound the two axes' distance; it does not
   extend its assertion to all injections. Lee's boundary example remains
   a relevant control.
7. The finite Whitehead/mod-three orbit procedure decides the stabilizer
   criterion. It does not compute the shortening constant. Its finite
   tests therefore verify a different component from the deep sufficiency
   implication.
