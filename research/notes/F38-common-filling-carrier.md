## The property that is decided

Given nonidentity u,v in a finite-rank free group F, decide whether there
is a finitely generated subgroup H in which conjugates of both u and v
are filling. The conjugations may be independent. In rank1, every nonzero
element of the infinite cyclic group counts as filling for this test.

A positive answer implies bounded translation equivalence in the original
ambient group. It in fact implies the stronger comparison under every
injective homomorphism from F to any finite-rank free group, with one
constant independent of that homomorphism. A negative answer to the
carrier search says only that this sufficient condition fails.

To justify the positive implication, first conjugate H so that it contains
u, and write t*v*t^-1 in H for a suitable t. The prior Kapovich--Lustig
filling theorem gives a uniform ratio bound for these two H-elements over
all free tree actions of H. Restrict any free action of F to the minimal
H-invariant subtree. Translation lengths of elements of H are unchanged,
and conjugation does not change the length of v. This proves the desired
bound. Pulling back the unit-edge free action under an injection proves
the stronger assertion as well. For cyclic H the ratio is just the ratio
of the absolute exponents. No assertion that the general converse holds
is used, and the program does not compute the comparison constant.

## Why the subgroup search is finite and complete for this property

Normalize u to a cyclically reduced conjugate. Its cyclic subgroup has a
finite labeled core graph: the cycle spelling this word. If u fills H,
then <u> is contained in no proper free factor of H. Otherwise the
associated free splitting of H would make u elliptic. Thus <u> <= H is
an algebraic extension, in the free-group sense.

Miasnikov--Ventura--Weil, *Algebraic extensions in free groups*,
[arXiv:math/0610880](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610880), Proposition3.7(i),
places every such extension in the finite principal fringe of <u>.
Section2.2 describes its effective enumeration by congruences on the
vertices of the core graph. These are prior versions of Takahasi's theorem.
We enumerate all folded quotient graphs, without needing to recognize
which are algebraic before the filling test.

The implementation starts with the cycle and successively identifies
pairs of vertices, folds both positive and inverse labeled edges, and
retains a canonical rooted graph only once. Each nontrivial identification
decreases the vertex count. Any congruence is generated by finitely many
pair identifications, and the intermediate folding closure stays within
that congruence. Thus this search is finite and reaches every required
quotient; it has no numerical cutoff.

For each graph, a spanning tree gives an explicit free basis for H and
coordinates for u. A cyclically reduced v has a conjugate in H exactly
when it labels a closed path at some vertex of this core graph. Examine
every vertex, not only the basepoint. If its tree path from the basepoint
is t, the resulting basis word represents t*v*t^-1. Test both basis words
for filling and return the complete graph/basis/conjugator certificate
on success. If every graph and closed lift fails, no common filling
carrier exists. Independence of the two initial conjugations is therefore
included in the decision, not silently omitted.

Single-word filling recognition reuses Gupta--Kapovich's finite outer
stabilizer criterion and the previously implemented exact orbit algorithm;
the detailed source credits and proof dependencies are in
`F38-filling-recognition.md`. Missing basis letters, or a basis letter
occurring exactly once, give an immediate non-filling certificate in rank
at least2: the word lies in a proper free factor, or is primitive.
All other cases use the full filling procedure. Carrier rank is computed
from its graph and is not artificially restricted to the ambient rank.

## Integration and an explicit newly recognized family

`f38_common_filling.py` implements this complete carrier search.
`f38_filling_recognition.bounded_comparison` now tries it after the
existing negative stabilizer test has passed and ambient filling has
failed. Its possible outcomes remain distinct:

- a negative automorphism witness from the earlier stabilizer obstruction;
- a positive result from ambient filling;
- a positive result from a common filling carrier;
- an unresolved pair with commensurable stabilizers and no such carrier.

For a concrete family, write A=a, B=b*a*b^-1, and H=<A,B> in F(a,b),
or in F(a,b,c). Take

    u=A^2 B^2 A^-1 B^-1,
    v=A^2 B^-2 A^-1 B.

The two H-words are filling. For example the Whitehead graph of the first
is the complete graph on the four oriented letters, each edge with weight1;
the second is its signed-basis variant. Their computed finite outer
stabilizers give the implemented certificates. H is free of rank2 and is
not an ambient free factor: its abelianization image has rank1. The
infinite-order outer automorphisms fixing a and sending b to b*a^n fix H
pointwise. Hence these words are not filling in the ambient group.
The new carrier branch recognizes them despite that ambient failure.
This is a consequence and implementation of prior filling machinery,
not a newly established solution of F38(c).

The essential opposite control is Lee's prior rank-two bounded pair

    a, a^2*b*a^-1*b^-1.

Since a is primitive in every subgroup containing it, a filling carrier
containing a would have to be cyclic; the second word is not conjugate
to a power of a. The carrier search therefore fails. Its integration
correctly leaves the original boundedness question unresolved, although
Lee's theorem says this pair is positive. Including the same pair in
rank3 gives the earlier explicit negative automorphism witness. Neither
the carrier criterion nor the stronger injection quantifier is substituted
for the original question.
