The question asks whether some word outside the commutator subgroup of a
finite-rank free group has infinitely many pairwise nonconjugate normal
roots. Normal-root membership means u lies in the normal closure of r.

## Elementary constraints on proposed constructions

Let ab:F_n->Z^n be abelianization. If u is a product of conjugates of
r and r^-1, then

    ab(u) = k ab(r),   k an integer.

Since ab(u) is nonzero, k is nonzero and divides every coordinate of
ab(u). Thus only finitely many abelianization vectors can occur among
its normal roots. This does **not** imply finitely many conjugacy
classes: abelianization has infinite fibers.

There is also a useful free-factor reduction. Suppose 1!=u belongs to
a standard free factor H. Cyclically reduce a proposed root r. If r
contains a basis letter outside H, the Freiheitssatz embeds the subgroup
generated by all the other basis letters in F_n/<<r>>. It therefore
embeds H, contradicting u=1 in that quotient. Consequently every root
is conjugate into H. Once r lies in H, the free-product decomposition

    F_n/<<r>> = (H/<<r>>_H) * K,   F_n=H*K,

shows that its root property already holds inside H. An automorphism
carries the same argument to any free factor. This is a routine
consequence of the classical Freiheitssatz, not a novel claim.

For example, if u=a^m in a cyclic free factor, the roots are conjugates
of a^d with nonzero d dividing m. Adding unused free generators does
not create a counterexample.

## Attempted stabilizer route

If an automorphism fixes u and moves one normal root through infinitely
many conjugacy classes, it would answer F27 positively. The elementary
attempts considered here did not supply such a root. The free-factor
observation rules out moving it into unused free directions. The usual
commutator examples violate ab(u)!=0. Trying nonorientable quadratic
words suggests equations in a free quotient, but no suitable root,
infinite orbit, or complete impossibility proof was obtained.

No exhaustive root enumeration was run and no finite search is being
used to infer finiteness. This lead is parked with the missing step
explicit rather than counted as progress on the mathematical assertion.
