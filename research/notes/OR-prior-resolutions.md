## OR7 and OR8: all three subparts have prior negative answers

Ke Wang and Qiang Zhang, *Commutator relators of one-relator groups do not
force Hopficity, residual finiteness, or automaticity*,
[arXiv:2607.21493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21493), submitted 23 July 2026,
explicitly cites these numbered questions. Read Theorem 1.3, Corollary 1.4
and the proofs in Sections 2–5 of the archived primary PDF.

Their convention is `[x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y`. For

    G_m = <a,t | [t,a[a,t]^-m]>,  m >= 1,

putting `c=[a,t]` gives the presentation

    <a,c,t | t^-1 a t = ac, t^-1 c^m t = c^(m+1)>.

The subgroup generated by c,t is a retract isomorphic to BS(m,m+1): the
retraction sends a to c^m. For m=2, the map a->ac^2, c->c^2, t->t is a
surjective endomorphism killing the nontrivial Britton-reduced word
`[t^-1 c t,c]`. Thus G_2 is non-Hopfian and not residually finite. For m=1,
the BS(1,2) retract gives a superpolynomial Dehn-function lower bound,
excluding automaticity. The relators are single commutators, so these
examples cover both OR8 and its broader counterpart OR7.

OR7(b) already has an older negative answer credited to Olshanskii on the
original website. The 2026 authors also credit earlier negative results for
OR7(b,c). The inspected new preprint is sufficient to exclude all six
subparts from this experiment's discovery count; this audit is not an
independent peer review of their paper. Its AI disclosure credits ChatGPT
with assistance in constructing the examples.

## OR11: prior positive theorem

Ashot Minasyan and Pavel Zalesskii, *One-relator groups with torsion are
conjugacy separable*, [arXiv:1211.0488](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0488),
Theorem 1.1: every such group is **hereditarily** conjugacy separable.
The exact theorem and relevant proof were read in the archived primary PDF.
It implies the positive answer to OR11 without extra hypotheses. Its
introduction also states Wise's residual-finiteness theorem, and Section 2
explains hyperbolicity from Newman's spelling theorem.

## OR2: prior positive consequence

François Dahmani and Vincent Guirardel,
[arXiv:1002.2590](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.2590), Theorem 1, gives an
algorithm taking two finite presentations of hyperbolic groups and deciding
isomorphism, allowing torsion. One-relator groups with torsion are hyperbolic
by Newman's theorem (also explained in Section 2 of the preceding primary
paper). This settles OR2 positively. The general theorem is prior work;
this specialization is not a new algorithm.

## OR6: prior positive consequence, including every integer k

Write H=F/<<r>> and G=F/<<r^k>>. If r=1 or k=0, then G is free and is
residually finite. For k=1 or -1, G=H, so the stated hypothesis suffices.
For |k|>=2 and r nontrivial, r^k is a proper power and G is a one-relator
group with torsion; Wise's residual-finiteness theorem applies regardless
of the status of H. Thus the answer is positive for every integer k.
This is a direct consequence of the known answer to OR1.

## OR12: prior positive consequence with the transfer step explicit

Use the following published facts.

1. An infinite freely indecomposable one-relator group with torsion is
   one-ended. This is the Fischer–Karrass–Solitar ends theorem, stated and
   used in Section 3 of Alan D. Logan,
   [*The JSJ-decompositions of one-relator groups with torsion*](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/121498/19/121498.pdf).
   Read the section preceding Lemma 3.2 in the archived primary paper.
2. Larsen Louder and Henry Wilton,
   [*Uniform negative immersions and the coherence of one-relator groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08911),
   Theorem 5.1, supplies a torsion-free finite-index subgroup G0 of G
   which is the fundamental group of a finite 2-complex with uniform
   negative immersions. Theorem 6.5(i) states that every finitely generated
   one-ended subgroup of G0 is co-Hopfian. Read both exact theorems and
   their proofs. Only this qualified assertion of 6.5(i) is used; the
   printed subsequent “In particular” clause is not needed.

Here is the elementary finite-index transfer, to avoid assuming that
co-Hopficity is generally a commensurability invariant. Put d=[G:G0] and
let K be the intersection of **all** subgroups of G of index at most d.
There are finitely many such subgroups because G is finitely generated.
Hence K has finite index, lies in G0, and is finitely generated and
one-ended. By fact 2 it is co-Hopfian.

For any endomorphism phi of G and any subgroup L of index at most d,
the subgroup phi^-1(L) also has index at most d. Thus K is contained in
phi^-1(L) for every such L, proving phi(K)<=K. If phi is injective,
co-Hopficity of K gives phi(K)=K. Injectivity then gives phi^-1(K)=K,
so [phi(G):K]=[G:K]. Since K<=phi(G)<=G, this forces phi(G)=G.

Finite G is co-Hopfian immediately. These cases prove the positive answer
to OR12. This is recorded as a routine consequence of published structural
theorems, with their exact scope checked, not as a novel result.
