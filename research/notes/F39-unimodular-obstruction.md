# F39: why even all nilpotent quotients do not repair the injection shortcut

29 September 2026, approximately 22:39–22:47 UTC. A proof-strategy
obstruction and a deduction from credited blocking results, **not**
a solution of F39 or an additional partial candidate. No novelty is
asserted for the construction. The earlier determinant-four example
in [the shared audit](F34-F38-shared-foundation-audit.md) is retained.

## The failed replacement

For F39(a), replacing an automorphism by an injective endomorphism
with nonzero abelianization determinant gives false positives. The
following construction shows that requiring determinant one does not
fix this. Even requiring an automorphism on **every free nilpotent
quotient** is insufficient.

For each finite rank r>=2 we construct an injective endomorphism phi
of F_r, inducing the identity on abelianization, whose image H
contains no ambient primitive element. Thus phi(x_1) is in H, but
no automorphism sends x_1 into H. A stronger version makes H avoid
every automorphic orbit having a representative of bounded cyclic
length, while retaining the same nilpotent-quotient property.

This says nothing negative about existence of the requested decision
algorithm. The primitive-input case F39(b) is already decidable, and
all of (a) in rank two is prior work.

## An explicit primitive-free image in every rank

Use the basis x_1,...,x_r and put

    K = x_1^2 x_2^2 ... x_r^2 x_1,
    W = K x_2 K^-1 x_2^-1,
    t_i = x_(i+1) for i<r,     t_r=x_1,
    U_i = x_i^2 W x_i^-1 t_i x_i^-1 t_i^-1 x_i.

Define phi(x_i)=U_i and H=<U_1,...,U_r>.

These displayed words are freely reduced. K starts and ends in x_1;
W starts in x_1 and ends in x_2^-1. In U_i the junctions with W have
letters of the same sign, and t_i differs from x_i. Thus U_i starts
and ends in x_i, and

    |K|=2r+1,       |W|=4r+4,       |U_i|=4r+11.

For signed generators, U_i^epsilon starts and ends in x_i^epsilon.
Hence two such words cancel at their junction exactly when their
source letters are inverse. A nonempty reduced source word therefore
has a nonempty, freely reduced image. This proves injectivity directly.
A cyclically reduced source word likewise has a cyclically reduced
image, containing at least one whole U_i or U_i^-1.

Use the Whitehead graph convention assigning the unoriented edge
{a,b^-1} to a consecutive pair ab. The **internal** edges of K,
without any external closing edge, form the spanning cycle

    {x_i,x_i^-1}                 (1<=i<=r),
    {x_i,x_(i+1)^-1}             (1<=i<r),
    {x_r,x_1^-1}.

It has all 2r vertices, is connected and has no cut vertex. Adding
edges preserves those two properties. Reversing and inverting a
word gives the same unoriented internal edges. Thus a cyclic word
containing K or K^-1 cannot be primitive, by the classical Whitehead
cut-vertex lemma.

Every nonidentity h in H is phi(z) for some nonidentity z. Cyclically
reduce z by conjugation inside F_r. Its image is then a cyclically
reduced representative of the conjugacy class of h and contains an
entire U_i or inverse, hence K or inverse. Primitivity is invariant
under conjugacy, so **no nonidentity element of H is primitive**.
In particular H is proper. This argument concerns all words in H,
not a bounded enumeration of its elements.

Since W is a commutator word, its exponent-sum vector is zero. Direct
addition in the formula for U_i gives the exponent vector of x_i.
Consequently the abelianization matrix of phi is exactly I_r, not
merely a matrix of nonzero determinant.

## Every nilpotent quotient still sees the whole group

Let N=F_r/gamma_(c+1)(F_r). The induced endomorphism phi_c has image
J with J gamma_2(N)=N. In a nilpotent group, a subgroup generating
the abelianization generates the whole group. One proof is to use
successively that gamma_k(N)/gamma_(k+1)(N) is generated by simple
commutators of weight k in a generating set; replacing the entries
by representatives in J modulo gamma_2 does not change those
commutators modulo gamma_(k+1). Induction yields

    N = J gamma_k(N)             for all k,

and hence N=J at k=c+1. The finitely generated nilpotent group N is
Hopfian, so phi_c is an automorphism.

It follows that H maps onto **every nilpotent quotient of F_r**,
including finite ones: every such quotient factors through an N of
finite class. This does not assert that phi descends to every chosen
quotient; the surjectivity assertion concerns the image of H under
the quotient map. Nor is the conclusion asserted for arbitrary
finite or solvable quotients.

For example, the rank-two construction has a small finite separator.
Map x_1 and x_2, respectively, to

    a=(3,5,4),              b=(1,5)(2,4)       in A_5.

GAP computes that these generate A_5 and that U_1,U_2 map to

    u=(1,5,3,4,2),         v=(1,4)(3,5).

Their subgroup has order 10, whereas a has order 3; thus a is not
in the image of H. This gives a separate exact finite check of
properness. Its ambient group is nonnilpotent, as required by the
preceding universal argument.

## Avoiding all short automorphic orbits

There is a useful stronger version using the **prior** orbit-blocking
theorem of Koch-Hyde, O'Connor, Olive and Shpilrain, Theorem 6.1 of
arXiv:2505.00477v2. It implies that K^(L+1) is forbidden in cyclically
reduced automorphic images of every word of cyclic length at most L.
The inverse block is forbidden too: otherwise inversion gives the
positive block in an image of the inverse input, of the same length.

For L>=1 replace K by B=K^(L+1) in the definition of W, keeping
the formula for U_i unchanged. All junction, injectivity and
abelianization arguments still hold, and now

    |U_i| = 2(2r+1)(L+1)+9.

Every nonidentity element of H has a cyclically reduced representative
containing B or B^-1. Therefore, for every nonidentity h in H and
every alpha in Aut(F_r),

    ||alpha(h)|| > L.

Indeed, an alpha making that length at most L would put h in an
orbit forbidden by its displayed block. H still maps onto all
nilpotent quotients. For any fixed nonidentity input g of cyclic
length at most L, phi(g) lies in H although its automorphic orbit
misses H entirely.

This deduction does not extend the prior orbit-blocking theorem's
scope or solve F40 again. Its role here is to exclude the proposed
nilpotent-approximation repair of the F39 injection reduction. It
also does not disprove the separate primitive-*length* reflection
question in the earlier F37 note.
