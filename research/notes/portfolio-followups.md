## N5: direct decomposability with torsion

The archived Baumslag–Miller–Ostheimer paper proves the torsion-free case and explicitly asks whether its algorithm extends to groups with torsion. A possible route is to choose a characteristic verbal subgroup U=G^m that is torsion-free and has finite index. Every direct decomposition of G restricts to one of U; finite factors disappear and must be handled separately.

What would be needed: an effective finite list of Aut(U)-orbits of direct decompositions, control of which automorphisms of U extend to G, and a finite/decidable lifting test. Finiteness of isomorphism types is not by itself an effective enumeration or a stopping rule. A claim that the restriction of Aut(G) has finite index in Aut(U) also needs proof; do not assume it. Central mixing in the BMO construction looks relevant. Finite direct factors lie in the finite torsion subgroup, but deciding complements still needs an explicit cited algorithm or argument.

An elementary embedding is available for orientation: after choosing torsion-free finite-index normal U, G embeds into (G/T) x (G/U), where T is the finite torsion subgroup, as a fiber product over G/TU. This does not itself give a decomposition algorithm.

## N9(a): fixed ambient group

The website explicitly leaves open whether some **fixed** finitely generated nilpotent group has an undecidable retract problem. The known construction of a group with relation [x,y]=w(c) varies the ambient group with c and is insufficient.

A naive attempt to fix the ambient group by a nilpotent coproduct of G0 with two free generators and to take H_c=<G0,[x,y]w(c)^-1> fails: the new central generator would have to map to a commutator lying in G0', so such a subgroup is never a retract. Recording this prevents mistaking the construction for a fixed-group reduction. More sophisticated variable embeddings would be needed.

## B9: special braid counts

Dehornoy's *The braid shelf*, Question 3.19, is a useful primary starting point. The term “special braid” is also used for a different family in the unprovability paper; do not conflate them. Bounds extracted as “2n” need visual inspection of the original typography.

Possible bounded experiment: enumerate one-generator self-distributive terms by depth, evaluate their braids by a faithful representation/normal form, and test their minimum strand number. This can give lower bounds or explicit counterexamples to proposed bounds but cannot establish an exhaustive count without a proof controlling term depth. No such search has run yet.

## F28: index-two virtual endomorphisms

The question asks for a nontrivial invariant subgroup, not necessarily normal or finitely generated. The largest candidate is the subgroup of elements whose positive iterates remain in the domain. Faithful self-similarity only controls normal invariant subgroups and is therefore insufficient.

A length-preserving map arising from an isomorphism between the two-vertex covers for the parity-of-a and parity-of-b subgroups has an evident invariant subgroup generated by a^2,b^2. Thus the simplest cover-swapping construction cannot disprove F28. No general conclusion is drawn.

## N8 beyond class four

The outer-slot map is injective in the bounded rank-two checks for degrees 3 through 7. This does not prove injectivity in all ranks/degrees. If a general injectivity theorem were available, a nonzero Lie element could have at most two directions of a degree-one commutator factor, because such a direction must divide a nonzero quadratic obtained by contracting the two outer slots.

In class five, the nonzero degree-two case has free correction parameters at the next stage; some correspond to simultaneous conjugation by the commutator, which preserves the target. A finite normalization would need an integral argument, not merely a rational dimension count. Other leading-weight cases introduce further bilinear terms. Preserve the completed class-three/four candidates while investigating these gaps.
