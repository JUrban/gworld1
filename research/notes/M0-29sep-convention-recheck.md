This is a check of its stated Lemma 1 and conventions, not a new proof of
Bachmuth's theorem or an independent specialist review.

The paper states the full square-Jacobian basis criterion, without the
smaller-tuple restrictions appearing in its other results. It defines
right derivatives by D_R(uv)=bar(v)D_R(u)+D_R(v). Our proof uses left
derivative columns, D_L(uv)=D_L(u)+bar(u)D_L(v). The exact conversion is

    D_R,i(w) = bar(w) X_i^(-1) iota(D_L,i(w)),
    iota(X_i)=X_i^(-1).

It holds on generators and their inverses and is preserved under products
by the two displayed product rules. For generator-image words w_j, the
source's row matrix and our column matrix therefore satisfy

    J_R = diag(bar(w_j)) iota(J_L)^transpose diag(X_i^(-1)).

All diagonal entries are Laurent monomial units and iota is a ring
involution. Thus invertibility of the two square matrices is equivalent.
This makes the existing convention conversion explicit. It does not
substitute a converse primitivity test for a single Jacobian column.

The remaining potentially sensitive points survive this reading:

- A primitive column cannot vanish at a field character because it extends
  to an invertible basis Jacobian. This is used only as a necessary condition.
- Similarity after a basis change uses the IA normalization. Its source
  and target characters agree; a general unnormalized map would not give
  that same evaluated similarity formula.
- Each constructed elementary transvection fixes the chosen character,
  so evaluated Jacobians of their compositions multiply without a character
  twist. Its appended word omits the changed generator, giving an actual
  free-group automorphism and an explicit inverse.
- The finite residue field is generated by the character values. After
  the integer character basis change, it is F_p(t), even when t does not
  generate the entire multiplicative group of that field. Polynomial
  coefficients in t therefore realize all required field transvections.
- Transitivity on nonzero vectors is used only in dimension at least two.
  The rank-two group case uses the separate one-dimensional kernel argument.
- Kronecker exponents are injective on the finite Laurent support after
  shifting coordinate minima. The preserved constant and leading terms
  give a nonconstant polynomial with no root at zero; augmentation one
  excludes a root at one. No generic-specialization assumption is needed.

No mathematical source, constructor or certificate was changed and no
