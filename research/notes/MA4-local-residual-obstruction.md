# MA4: obstruction to a locally free construction

29 September 2026. A failed construction route, not an answer to MA4 and
not another counted partial candidate. The exact original question asks
for a simple torsion-free linear group, without finite generation. The
complete original matrix page and the rendered MA4 paragraph were read.

## The proposed route

Every countable locally free group embeds in SL_2(K) for a field K of
characteristic zero; a direct proof is below. It is torsion-free as well.
Thus it was tempting to try an increasing union of free groups made
simple by successively forcing normal-generation relations. The following
elementary obstruction prevents this route.

Call a group locally residually solvable if each of its finitely generated
subgroups is residually solvable. Free groups are residually nilpotent,
hence residually solvable, so locally free groups have this property.

**Lemma.** Let G be locally residually solvable. If a normal subgroup
N of G is both perfect and generated by finitely many elements as a
normal subgroup of G, then N=1.

**Proof.** Write N=normal_closure_G(S) for a finite set S. Since N=N',
each s in S is a finite product of commutators [u,v] with u,v in N.
Express all those finitely many u and v as finite products of
G-conjugates of elements of S and their inverses. Let H be generated by
S and every conjugating element appearing in these chosen expressions.
Thus H is finitely generated and residually solvable.

Put K=normal_closure_H(S). The chosen expressions show S is contained
in K'. Since K' is characteristic in K and K is normal in H, the group
K' is normal in H. Consequently K is contained in K', and K=K'.

For every homomorphism f from H to a solvable group, f(K) is perfect.
A perfect subgroup of a solvable group is trivial: it lies in every
term of the ambient derived series. Thus every such f kills S. Residual
solvability of H forces S={1}, which proves N=1. QED.

**Consequences.** A nonabelian simple group is perfect and normally
generated by any one of its nonidentity elements. It therefore cannot
be locally residually solvable. More generally, a nontrivial perfect
locally residually solvable group cannot have finite normal generating
number. In particular, no locally free group is nonabelian simple.

This is not an obstruction to all linear groups. In the familiar
finitely generated linear-group theorem, the residual targets are finite
groups, which need not be solvable. The proof above specifically uses
that solvable groups have no nontrivial perfect subgroup. Replacing
"residually solvable" with "residually finite" would invalidate it.

## A control: perfect locally free linear groups do exist

Perfectness alone is insufficient for the lemma. Here is a construction
that checks this boundary and also explains why passing to a simple
quotient cannot be assumed to preserve local freeness or linearity.

Use [x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y and let F=F(a,b). Set

    c=[a,b],       d=[a,b^2],
    phi(a)=c,     phi(b)=d.

The reduced words cd and dc are distinct: after their common initial
letters a^-1 b^-1, the former has a and the latter has b^-1. There is
no cancellation in either displayed concatenation. Thus c and d do
not commute. By the subgroup theorem for free groups, their subgroup
is free; being noncyclic and two-generated, its rank is two. The
surjection F(a,b) onto this subgroup is injective by Hopficity of a
rank-two free group. Hence phi is injective, with image contained in F'.

Form the direct limit

    L = colim(F --phi--> F --phi--> F --phi--> ...).

Each stage embeds, so L is nontrivial and locally free. Every generator
of each stage is a commutator in the next stage, whence L=L'. It is
torsion-free because every element lies in a free stage. The lemma
proves L is not simple and, more strongly, has no finite normal
generating set. One must not replace "simple" by "perfect" in its
corollary.

For completeness, L is linear in degree two. The matrices

    A = [[1,2],[0,1]],       B = [[1,0],[2,1]]

freely generate a rank-two subgroup of SL_2(Q). One elementary
ping-pong proof uses their fractional-linear action: nonzero powers of
A take |t|<1 into |t|>1, and nonzero powers of B take |t|>1 into
|t|<1. Thus every free stage has a faithful representation rho_i in
SL_2(Q).

Choose a nonprincipal ultrafilter U on the nonnegative integers and
let K=Q^N/U, a field of characteristic zero. For g represented at stage
k, use at coordinates i>=k the matrix rho_i(phi^(i-k)(g)), and use the
identity at earlier coordinates. The resulting class in SL_2(K) is
independent of the chosen stage: any two representatives give equal
matrices at all sufficiently late coordinates. Multiplication is also
respected on that cofinite set. Injectivity holds because each stage
map and each rho_i is injective, so a nonidentity element has a
nonidentity matrix at every sufficiently late coordinate.

The same argument works for any countable locally free group by
exhausting it by finitely generated free subgroups. Each such subgroup
embeds in F_2 and hence in SL_2(Q). One can also put the resulting
countable matrix group in SL_2(C): its entries generate a countable
characteristic-zero field, which embeds in C by choosing a
transcendence basis and then extending over the algebraic part. This
is an existence argument, not an effective list of complex matrices.

## Audit and research decision

The obstruction and control are self-contained apart from classical
free-group facts (subgroup freeness, Hopficity, residual nilpotence),
ping-pong, and the elementary field ultraproduct construction. The
finite-support step in the lemma is essential: it produces a single
finitely generated residually solvable H, even though G need not be
finitely generated. The control shows that one cannot simply assert
that a perfect locally residually solvable group is trivial.

No finite computation could verify the simplicity or residual property
at issue, and none was used. No novelty is asserted for this elementary
lemma or for the direct-limit construction. No matching general answer
to MA4 was found by the bounded source search. Stop the locally free
construction route; any positive construction must leave this class.
