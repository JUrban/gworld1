## Two kernel lemmas

Use the free metabelian Lie representation in the semidirect product of the abelian linear polynomial space and the free polynomial module with basis e_i. Send X_i to (x_i,e_i); the module component of [X_i,X_j] is x_i e_j-x_j e_i. On the derived ideal, bracketing by z in L1 is multiplication by its nonzero linear polynomial ell_z. The representation is injective in degrees two and three: its degree-three kernel in V tensor Lambda^2 V consists exactly of Jacobi tensors Lambda^3 V. This can be proved by comparing coefficients of e_i x_j x_k, including repeated indices. The degree-two module image is exactly the kernel of sum e_i P_i -> sum x_i P_i for linear P_i (skew coefficient matrices).

**Lemma A.** For z in L1 nonzero and Y in L2 nonzero, the kernel of

    (C,D) in L2 + L3 -> [C,Y]+[z,D]

is precisely {(lambda Y,0):lambda in Q}.

Proof: the first bracket maps to zero in the free metabelian Lie algebra. Thus ell_z M(D)=0, whence M(D)=0 and D=0 by degree-three injectivity. Finally [C,Y]=0 iff C,Y are dependent, since Lambda^2(L2) embeds into degree-four tensors.

**Lemma B.** For independent u,v in L1, with w=[u,v], the kernel of

    (C,D) in L3 + L3 -> [C,v]+[u,D]

is the one-dimensional space spanned by ([u,w],[v,w]).

Proof: in the metabelian representation, ell_v M(C)=ell_u M(D). The independent linear polynomials are relatively prime, so M(C)=ell_u T and M(D)=ell_v T for a module vector T with linear polynomial coefficients. All derived module vectors satisfy sum x_i P_i=0; hence so does T. Therefore T=M(W) for some W in L2. Degree-three injectivity gives C=[u,W], D=[v,W]. Jacobi now gives [C,v]+[u,D]=[[u,v],W]. The injective exterior-square map on L2 implies W is proportional to w. Conversely this displayed kernel vector plainly works.

Both claims hold in every finite rank, and can be checked symbolically in low ranks as an additional guard against sign/convention mistakes. The rational kernel determines a rank-one primitive integral kernel lattice.

## Nonzero g2

Use the class-three finite HNF list of abelianization pairs u,v. Degree-three matching determines the degree-two corrections uniquely, as in class four. For each resulting base pair solve the integer degree-four correction system on L3+L3.

Its full integral solution set is either empty or q0+Z k0, where k0 is a primitive integral vector spanning Lemma B's kernel. Simultaneous conjugation of an actual solution pair by the target g preserves its commutator exactly. At degree three this changes the factors by the nonzero integral vector

    K = ([u,g2],[v,g2]) = t k0,   t != 0.

Thus every solution is equivalent to one of the finitely many residues q0+n k0, 0<=n<|t|. For each residue choose group lifts with these corrections. The remaining degree-five discrepancy is an integer linear system of corrections in L4+L4. Higher factor corrections do not matter. This gives a finite procedure.

Important: use conjugation by **the target g** when normalizing an existing solution. A preliminary base pair need not yet have commutator exactly g, though its commutator has the same leading degree-two term. The normalization argument only needs the leading effect; all higher changes are covered in the final lift system.

## Zero g2, nonzero g3

Nielsen normalization puts abelianizations at (z,0). The class-three tensor reconstruction gives finitely many integral leading pairs z=kz0, Y=Y0/k in L1 and L2, with k a nonzero divisor of content(Y0).

For each, solve degree-four matching in variables C in L2 and D in L3. Lemma A makes its integral kernel generated by (Y/content(Y),0). The exact commutator-preserving move (x,y)->(yx,y) changes C by Y and leaves D unchanged. Hence only content(Y) residues need be considered. For each representative, solve the remaining linear degree-five system in corrections from L3 (first factor) and L4 (second factor): [C3,Y]+[z,D4].

Again normalization changes higher coordinates, but those are free final-stage corrections. This is a completeness argument, not merely a parameter heuristic.

## Zero g2,g3, nonzero g4

1. A nonzero abelianization survives: after normalization, x has leading z in L1 and y has leading Y in L3. ## Pure nonzero g5

If an abelianization is nonzero, the other factor lies in gamma_4; use the degree-one factor lemma to decide g5=[z,Y4]. Scaling can be absorbed entirely into Y4, so primitive z is enough.

If both abelianizations are zero, their degree-two parts commute (g4=0), hence are dependent by the injective Lambda^2 L2 map. Nielsen normalization makes the second factor lie in gamma_3. Both degree-two parts cannot vanish, since then the commutator has degree at least six. Thus the remaining case is

    g5=[C2,D3].

This is also a finite quadratic-factor test. Introduce commuting variables t_I indexed by ordered pairs I of basis letters. For each single middle letter j form

    q_j(t)=sum_(I,K of length 2) (g5)_(I,j,K) t_I t_K.

If g5=C2 D3-D3 C2, then q_j=C2(t)*(sum_K D_(j,K)t_K-sum_I D_(I,j)t_I). If all q_j vanish, D_(j,K)=D_(K,j): D3 is invariant under rotation by two places, hence under full cyclic rotation in degree three. Its cyclic symmetrization is zero as for every positive-degree Lie bracket, so D3=0, a contradiction. Therefore one nonzero quadratic gives at most two C2 directions.

For each linear factor, first check that its tensor belongs to L2, then choose a primitive integral vector C0 in the L2 Hall lattice and solve [C0,D3]=g5 over the integers. Scaling is absorbed into D3. This tests every possibility and produces a group witness immediately because weight five is central.
