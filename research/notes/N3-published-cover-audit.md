# N3: a published cover construction fails to be torsion-free

28 September 2026, approximately 17:32–17:43 UTC.
**Supplementary proof audit, not a solution to N3.**

The 2003 paper below states a full positive answer to the original question.
Its proposed pseudo-free groups, however, need not be torsion-free. This
note gives a class-five example with an explicit central element of order
two. A standalone integer-series verifier and GAP/nq both check it.
No novelty or external specialist validation of this observation is claimed.

## Statement and source

N3 asks whether every locally nilpotent group is a homomorphic image of
a torsion-free locally nilpotent group, with no cardinality restriction.
The complete original `sources/raw/probnil.html`, exact N3 fragment and
rendered paragraph were read and inspected. The screenshot and source hash
are in `research/statement-audits/N3/`; the entry has no linked background.

R. Göbel, S. Shelah and S. L. Wallutis, *On universal and epi-universal
locally nilpotent groups*, Illinois J. Math. **47** (2003), 223–236:
[author's published copy](https://shelah.logic.at/files/203632/Sh-742.pdf),
[author's bibliography/version page](https://shelah.logic.at/papers/742/).
Archived PDF, extracted text and retrieval/hash metadata:
`literature/raw/N3-Goebel-Shelah-Wallutis-Sh742.*`.

Definition 3.1 assigns a nilpotency bound c(U) to each finite subset of a
generating set, with c increasing under inclusion. The resulting quotient
of the free group is locally nilpotent. Proposition 3.5 claims it is always
torsion-free; Proposition 3.6 provides the epimorphism onto any given
locally nilpotent group. Corollary 3.7 states the full answer to N3.
The cardinal assumption in the separate Theorem 3.8 concerns a single
epi-universal group, and does not restrict Corollary 3.7.
Section 3 was read in full; printed pages 232–233 were also viewed and are
retained in `literature/figures/N3-GSW-page232.png` and `page233.png`.

## A preliminary error in the spanning argument

Write gamma_i for the lower central series, starting with gamma_1=F.
The paper instead starts with F^0=F, so F^n=gamma_(n+1).
Let F be free on a,b,c, and choose the increasing profile

    c({a})=c({b})=c({c})=1,
    c({a,b})=1, c({a,c})=c({b,c})=c({a,b,c})=3.

Modulo gamma_4(F), the normal subgroup K in Definition 3.1 is the normal
closure of [b,a]. But the displayed equality on page 232, with n=2,
drops normal conjugates and replaces it with the ordinary subgroup
generated by the corresponding subgroups on coordinate subsets. In
degree three this gives only [[b,a],a] and [[b,a],b]. It misses

    [[b,a],c] in K intersect gamma_3(F).

For an elementary separating representation in UT_4(Z), set

    a=I+E23, b=I+E12, c=I+E34.

Then [b,a]=I+E13, [[b,a],c]=I+E14, while the other two triple
commutators are trivial. The ordinary subgroup from {a,b} has no E14
coordinate. All weight-four commutators vanish in this representation.
Thus the two subgroups in the displayed equality really are different.
The inference using k^f=k[k,f] is inapplicable to lower-weight normal
generators whose conjugation differences lie in the layer being studied.

GAP independently checked this in the free rank-three class-three group:
the intersections with gamma_3 have Hirsch ranks three and two, respectively.
This failure of the spanning argument alone would not disprove the
torsion-freeness conclusion. The following example does.

## Explicit counterexample to Proposition 3.5

Use the profile with singleton bound one, every pair bound two, and full
three-element bound five. This is an allowed increasing function. Put

    N = F(a,b,c)/gamma_6(F),
    s=[b,a], t=[c,a], u=[c,b],
    r0=[s,a], r1=[s,b], r2=[t,a],
    r3=[t,c], r4=[u,b], r5=[u,c],
    H = N / normal_closure(r0,...,r5).

All commutators use [x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y, with multiple brackets
associated to the left. The two relations for each pair make its basic
commutator commute with both generators. They therefore normally
generate gamma_3 of that two-generator free group. Consequently H is
exactly Fr(M,c) from Definition 3.1 for this profile, not merely a
further quotient of it.

Consider the central weight-five word

    z = [[s,c],u] [[t,b],u].

We prove that its image in H has order exactly two.

### The square is trivial

In the free class-five group N the following identity holds:

    z^2 = [r1,c,c]^-1 [r3,b,b]
          [r4,a,c]^-2 [r5,a,b]^-2 [r5,b,a]^3.                 (1)

Every factor is central of weight five. Identity (1) can be checked by
expanding degree-five Lie brackets using antisymmetry and Jacobi, or by
the integer Magnus expansions used below. The right side lies in the
normal relator subgroup, so z^2=1 in H.

### The word itself is nontrivial

Work in the ring of integral noncommutative polynomials in A,B,C,
truncated above degree five, and use the Magnus substitutions
a -> 1+A, b -> 1+B, c -> 1+C. The inverse of 1+A is
1−A+A^2−A^3+A^4−A^5, and likewise for B,C.
The induced map on N is injective by integral Hall/Magnus collection.

For an element of gamma_3(N), subtract the constant term from its
Magnus expansion. This gives an injective additive map: the product of
two positive parts starts in degree six and hence vanishes. Denote the
image of a word v by m(v).

Let L be the integer span of the following 78 vectors:

    m(rj), m([rj,x]), m([rj,x,y]),
    j=0,...,5; x,y in {a,b,c}.                              (2)

This is exactly the image of the normal closure of the six relators.
Indeed, conjugation by a generator acts linearly on gamma_3(N). Its
difference from the identity raises degree by at least one. Three such
differences vanish, since they reach degree six. The span (2) is thus
invariant under generator conjugation and under its inverse, whose
action is 1−D+D^2. Conversely each vector in (2) comes from the normal
relator subgroup. This proves equality with that subgroup's image.

For a truncated polynomial v with no terms below degree three, define
the additive functional

    ell(v) = coeff_ABCC(v) + coeff_ACBB(v)
             + coeff_ABCBC(v)                         modulo 2.       (3)

Direct expansion gives the finite certificate

    ell(m(rj)) = ell(m([rj,x])) = ell(m([rj,x,y])) = 0
    for every j,x,y in (2),
    ell(m(z)) = 1.                                        (4)

Equations (3)–(4) separate m(z) from L, proving z != 1 in H. Together
with (1), this proves the asserted order two.

The complete signed word, the five nonzero coefficients in (1), and
the three monomials in (3) are saved in
`research/certificates/N3-pseudofree-class5/certificate.json`.
The dependency-free verifier reconstructs all 78 words from the six
relations, expands them from scratch using integer arithmetic, checks
(1) coefficient by coefficient, and checks every equality in (4).
It also verifies that the displayed bracket expression for z is exactly
the stored 52-letter freely reduced word. Thus the finite arithmetic
portion of the argument can be checked without trusting a lattice solver
or an nq presentation.

## Computation record and limits

All jobs used the recorded runner, at most two CPU cores simultaneously,
and at most 12 GB combined requested memory. No random choices were used.

- `n3-published-spanning-v1`: 1.97 seconds; exact integer matrices and
  independent free class-three calculation of the missing conjugate.
- `n3-pair-class2-v1`: 4.84 seconds; exploratory nq quotients for full
  bounds three through eight. At class four a finite relative order
  appears but the torsion subgroup is trivial. At class five GAP
  reports a torsion subgroup of order eight. This distinction is
  essential: finite relative orders alone do not prove group torsion.
  Higher-class output is retained as exploratory evidence only.
- `n3-class5-torsion-certificate-v1`: 1.02 seconds, failed assertion.
  No individual degree-five Hall word was a torsion witness. Its exact
  source is preserved in that run directory.
- `n3-class5-torsion-certificate-v2`: 12.86 seconds. Exact FLINT Hermite
  reduction of the 78-vector lattice found a product of two Hall words,
  the positive square identity, and the mod-two separator. The integral
  change-of-basis matrix was verified, including determinant ±1.
- `n3-class5-torsion-standalone-v2`: 0.22 seconds. Independent standard
  Python verifier, using no constructor, Hall implementation, SymPy,
  FLINT or GAP; checks (1)–(4) and the printed word expression.
- `n3-class5-torsion-gap-v2`: final independent GAP/nq check of the six
  relations, centrality, nontriviality and order two, plus identity (1)
  in the free class-five group. The first GAP run also passed but
  emitted a forward-global syntax warning; its source/log are retained.
  The final run fixes that warning.

Run directories are under `results/`; each `process.json` records exact

## Consequences for the portfolio

Proposition 3.5 is false for its stated unrestricted profiles. This
prevents accepting that proof of Corollary 3.7. It does **not** disprove
Corollary 3.7 or N3, and does not preclude a different construction or
a repair with stronger profile hypotheses. In fact this particular H
already has the torsion-free nilpotent cover N, so it cannot be a
counterexample to N3.

Searches for the paper title with “correction”, “error”, “gap”, and
“pseudo-free locally nilpotent” did not locate a repair. The author
version page lists the preprint and published version. This is not an
exhaustive literature audit or proof that the observation is new.
The earlier positive countable and periodic cases remain separate prior
results; their original proofs have not been re-audited here.

Keep N3 under bibliographic/proof review, with this explicit obstruction
attached. This adds no whole-entry or partial solution count. There has
been no author contact, no external review, and no use of a Kourovka-run
argument or code.
