# N8(b): proposed decision algorithm in nilpotency class three

First argument recorded 2026-09-28 around 10:26 UTC. **Research lead, not a verified or novel claim.** Original rendering and further literature audit pending. The website background says the free class-two case is decidable and higher classes remain open. This would answer only the class-three range, not the whole question.

Let N=F_r/gamma_4(F_r), with [x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y. Write V=N/gamma_2(N)=Z^r, L2=gamma_2/gamma_3=Lambda^2 V and L3=gamma_3, the degree-three free Lie ring.

Given g, first reject if g is outside gamma_2. The identity is a commutator.

## Nonzero image in L2

Let w be the image of g in L2. For any solution, abelianizations u,v satisfy u wedge v=w. If w is not decomposable over Q, reject. Otherwise its rational support is a two-plane, whose intersection P with V is a primitive rank-two lattice. If w=d times an oriented generator of Lambda^2 P, the lattice generated by u,v has index |d| in P. There are finitely many such lattices, effectively enumerable by Hermite normal forms.

Each oriented basis of a given lattice is SL(2,Z)-equivalent to a selected one. The operations (x,y)->(yx,y) and (x,y)->(x,xy), and inverses, preserve the group commutator exactly and generate SL(2,Z) on abelianizations. Thus every solution can be changed into one whose abelianization pair is on the finite list, while retaining g exactly.

For a chosen pair u,v select words x0,y0 with these abelianizations. All lifts are x=x0 c, y=y0 d with c,d in gamma_2. In class three,

    [x0 c,y0 d] = [x0,y0] [c,y0] [x0,d].

The last two factors are central and depend linearly on c,d modulo gamma_3. Therefore the remaining equation is an integer linear-system membership test in L3, solved by Smith normal form. This is a complete-looking finite procedure for w nonzero.

## Zero image in L2

For a nonidentity central g, the abelianizations of any solution are dependent. SL(2,Z) changes the pair to (z,0). Thus the problem reduces to

    [z,Y]=g, z in V, Y in Lambda^2 V.

Over a characteristic-zero field K, the kernel of the map

    T: V_K tensor Lambda^2 V_K -> L3_K, z tensor Y -> [z,Y]

is the Jacobi copy of Lambda^3 V_K. Its intersection with U tensor Lambda^2 V_K is zero whenever dim(U)<=2: an alternating three-tensor supported in U in the first slot is supported in U in all slots, and Lambda^3 U=0.

Consequently, if nonzero g has two expressions T(z tensor Y)=T(z' tensor Y'), then z,z' cannot be independent. It follows that the rank-one tensor z tensor Y representing g is unique. After selecting a coordinate patch z_i=1, there is at most one solution (z,Y) over the algebraic closure.

Enumerate the r coordinate patches and solve the polynomial equations T(z tensor Y)=g, z_i=1 by Groebner methods. Each patch has zero or one geometric point. If it has one, its coordinates are rational because the equations are rational and the point is Galois invariant. Effective zero-dimensional solving / radical computation recovers that rational point.

Rescale z to a primitive integer vector z0 and Y correspondingly to a rational exterior vector Y0. An integral solution exists exactly when Y0 is integral: any integral first vector on this line is k z0, k a nonzero integer, forcing the second factor Y0/k; integrality of that factor implies Y0 integral. If Y0 is integral, lift z0 and Y0 to N and obtain g.

## Explicit replacement for the Groebner step

The central case admits rational reconstruction without polynomial-system solving. Use the degree-three associative tensor coordinates of g, so that

    g_abc = z_a Y_bc - Y_ab z_c,

where Y is skew-symmetric. For each patch z_i=1, put t_j=g_iij. Then t_j=Y_ij.

If some t_j is nonzero, recover

    z_j = g_jij / (2 t_j),
    z_k = (g_jik - z_j t_k) / t_j for every k,
    Y_ab = g_iab + t_a z_b.

If all t_j vanish, recover Y_ab=g_iab. If Y is zero this patch cannot represent nonzero g. Otherwise choose Y_jk nonzero and recover

    z_k = -g_jkk/Y_jk,
    z_l = (g_ljk + Y_lj z_k)/Y_jk for every l.

In both cases verify z_i=1, skew-symmetry of Y and every equation g_abc=z_a Y_bc-Y_ab z_c. A valid rational reconstruction is then subjected to the primitive-vector integrality check above. Thus the algorithm needs only exact rational and integer linear algebra, even in the zero-L2 case.
