# A second arbitrary-class family: degree-two leading factors

Uncounted lead, first worked out approximately 20:23--20:27 UTC on
28 September 2026. The following argument needs a separate proof audit,
implementation and independent checks. It is not included in the ledger.

Proposed scope: c=2n+7 with n>=1 (every odd c>=9), any finite rank, and
targets with nonzero leading term in degree c-2 of the form

    w=ad_C^(n+1)(T), C in L2 nonzero, T in L3 nonzero.        (1)

All data in recognition may be rational; final group factors remain
integral. Put D=ad_C^n(T), of weight q=2n+3. The proposed leading type
is (2,q), leaving precisely two correction layers.

## 1. No degree-one leading factor

Fix any nonzero z in L1 and use the delta_z-stable homogeneous free
alphabet of L' from the class-nine proof. C belongs to its weight-two
generator space E and T to its weight-three generator space B. Thus
w has free-alphabet bracket length k=n+2, with k-1 copies of C and one
T. Its original degree is 2k+1. If w=[z,V], injectivity of delta_z and
preservation of bracket length force V to have length k and original
degree 2k, so V belongs to Lie_k(E).

The derivative of such a V contains exactly one B letter in delta_z E.
Consequently T must belong to delta_z E: projecting B modulo delta_z E
otherwise leaves a nonzero ad_C^(k-1)(Tbar). Write T=delta_z S.
The substitution replacing each delta_z E letter by its E antecedent
sends delta_z V to k*V and w to ad_C^(k-1)S. Therefore necessarily

    V=(1/k)*ad_C^(k-1)S.

If S is proportional to C, this V vanishes but w does not, impossible.
If C,S are independent, choose them as E basis vectors. The component
of delta_z(ad_C^(k-1)S) with B letter delta_z C and E multiset
C^(k-2)S is nonzero: in its associative expansion the word
(delta_z C)*C^(k-2)*S has coefficient one. It comes from differentiating
the first C in the leading associative word C^(k-1)S. All other terms
start with C or have a different placement of S. The desired right side
k*ad_C^(k-1)(delta_z S) has no such B component. This contradiction
excludes every z, hence every leading weight-one factor.

## 2. Every leading pair has type (2,q) and direction C

C and T can be included in a homogeneous free basis of L', since E=L2
and B=L3. Project that free algebra onto the free Lie algebra on C,T,
killing the other generators. The target (1) survives and has T-count
one. For a homogeneous element of original degree greater than two,
any nonzero projection has positive T-count: the free Lie algebra on
C alone contains only the line Q*C. If both leading weights were
greater than two, the projected bracket would have T-count at least
two and could not equal w. Thus one leading weight is two.

If w=[C',D'] with nonzero C' in E, quotient the free algebra L' by
the generator direction C'. Were C' not proportional to C, the image
of C would remain nonzero while T in B would be unaffected; (1) would
remain nonzero. The bracket [C',D'] becomes zero, a contradiction.
Hence C' is proportional to C. The earlier block-quadratic procedure
for p=2 should recognize this direction finitely, followed by a rational
linear test for T, and then all signed integral leading scales.

## 3. A free alphabet shifted by ad_C

Write a homogeneous free generating set for L' as {C} union S. The
ideal J generated by S has free generators ad_C^j(S), j>=0. This can
be justified without a new freeness black box. Their Lie subalgebra
contains S and is stable under ad_C; its semidirect sum with Q*C
therefore contains every generator of L'. To check freeness, in the
free associative algebra order C below every S letter. The leading
word of ad_C^j(S) is, up to sign, S*C^j. Products of these words have
unique decoding at their non-C letters. Leading terms therefore
prove freeness of the associative algebra on the jets, and hence
Lie freeness. The argument should be written with a compatible
degree-lexicographic order explicitly if made into a final proof.

Every homogeneous component of L' other than weight two lies in J;
the weight-two exception is the line Q*C. Ad_C shifts the new free
alphabet and preserves its bracket length and seed counts.

## 4. The same parity kernel and obstruction

In the first correction, U has original weight three, hence is a
linear combination of the weight-three seeds in S. D is the T-jet
ad_C^n T. Exactly the two-variable polynomial argument in the first
uniform-family proof gives zero kernel for odd n. For n=2m>=2, it
gives the line

    (T,-V_m),
    V_m=sum_(i=0)^(m-1)(-1)^i[ad_C^i T,ad_C^(2m-1-i)T].

The first correction weights are 3 and q+1. Their cross term has
weight q+4=c; self-interactions start beyond c. The final correction
is on L4+L_(q+2), mapping (U,V) to [U,D]+ad_C V in degree c.

Map the T-jets to A+(-2)^j B and all other J seeds to zero. This
intertwines ad_C with the derivation A->A, B->-2B. The selected
length-three AAB coefficient kills its image. It also kills [U,D]:
an original-degree-four U cannot have a nonzero image, since individual
T-jets have odd degrees 3+2j and two T-jets already have degree at
least six. The quadratic obstruction [T,V_m] again has coefficient
1-4^m, nonzero. The integral parameter therefore has at most two values.

For odd n the first correction is unique; for even n this quadratic
test leaves finitely many; in each case finish with the full integral
last-layer system. Integral leading scales and all lattice congruences
must be retained, as in the first family.

The n=0 case would be the already known type (2,3) Nielsen kernel,
with V_0=0 and no quadratic obstruction. It is intentionally excluded.
The first genuinely new test beyond class nine is n=2, class eleven,
with leading type (2,7). Class thirteen supplies the next odd-n case.
