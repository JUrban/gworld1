FREE GROUPS ARE L2-SUBGROUP RIGID
ANDREI JAIKIN-ZAPIRAIN
Abstract. In this paper, we introduce the notion of L2-subgroup rigid groups
and demonstrate that free groups are L2-subgroup rigid. As a consequence,
we establish the equivalence between compressibility, inertness, strong inert-
ness, and L2-independence for a finitely generated subgroup of a free group,
confirming a conjecture by Dicks and Ventura as well as the one by Antolin
and Jaikin-Zapirain.
1. Introduction
The study of finitely generated subgroups of a free group is a classical topic
in combinatorial group theory. The standard approach employs Stallings’ graph
theory techniques introduced in [Sta83] (see also [KM02]). However, it has been
realized that these techniques have limitations in addressing problems related to
the intersections of subgroups, as exemplified by the case of the Hanna Neumann
Conjecture. The proofs of the conjecture by Friedman[Fri15] and Mineyev[Min12],
as well as a more recent proof by Jaikin-Zapirain[JZ17] use different algebraic tools.
These ideas were further developed by Antolin and Jaikin-Zapirain [AJZ22], where
the notion ofL2-independence was introduced. This led to the proof of a generalized
version of the Hanna Neumann conjecture for various families of groups [BK23,
FM23]. In this paper, we introduce the notion ofL2-subgroup rigid group, providing
another powerful tool for proving new results on subgroups of groups possessing this
property.

We denote by IQ[G] the augmentation ideal of Q[G] and if H is a subgroup of
G we denote by GIQ[H] the left ideal of Q[G] generated by IQ[H]. Let L be a left

Lemma 2.1), and we call this subgroup the L2-closure of H in G. We say that G
isL2-subgroup rigid if for all finitely generated subgroups of G itsL2-closure can
be defined. In this paper we will show that free groups are L2-subgroup rigid.
Theorem 1.1. Free groups areL2-subgroup rigid.
It is very plausible that our proof can be extended to show that not only free
groups, but also other groups are L2-subgroup rigid. We have chosen to explore
this aspect in a forthcoming paper and, for simplicity, focus here only on the case
of free groups. As we will see later, even in the case of free groups, we already can
derive several important consequences.
A finitely generated subgroup H of a free group F is called compressed if for
any finitely generated subgroup L of F containing H, rk(H)≤ rk(L), and it is
called inert if for any finitely generated subgroup L of F , rk(H∩L)≤ rk(L).
The concept of a subgroup being compressed is quite intuitive; it indicates that
the subgroup is not contained in another subgroup of smaller rank. The concept
of an inert subgroup was introduced in the work of Dicks and Ventura while they
were studying equalizers in free groups [DV96].
We also say that H is L2-independent in F if the canonical map
D(F )⊗Q[H]IQ[H]→D (F )⊗Q[F ]IQ[F ]
is injective. It is clear that inert subgroups are compressed and Antolin and Jaikin-
Zapirain [AJZ22] proved that L2-independent subgroups are inert. On the other
hand, Dicks and Ventura [DV96, Problem 1] (see also [KM18, Problem 19.102])
asked whether compressed subgroups are also inert and Antolin and Jaikin-Zapirain
[AJZ22, Question 2] suggested that compressed subgroups may always be L2-
independent. We confirm these two conjectures.
Corollary 1.2. Let F be a free group and H a finitely generated subgroup. Then
the following are equivalent:
(1) H is compressed in F ;
(2) H is inert in F ;
(3) H is L2-independent in F .
In Corollary 3.13 we will also show that these three properties of H are also
equivalent ofH being strongly inert in F (see Section 2.8 for the definition).
It is clear that the intersection of two inert subgroups is inert and by [MVW07,
Theorem 3.9], it is algorithmically decidable, for a finitely generated subgroup of a
free group, whether it is compressed. In [Iva18] Ivanov proved that it is algorith-
mically decidable whether a finitely generated subgroup of a free group is strongly

The main step of our proof is found in Section 3.3, where we establish an analogue

4. Final comments
The paper raises several questions, which we will gather in this section. Our
proof ofDF2[F ]-subgroup rigidity of a free group F depends on a peculiarity of the
leftF2[F ]-module IF2[F ] (Lemma 3.6). However, we strongly believe that there are
other methods that work for all primes.
Conjecture 1. Let F be a free group and p an odd prime. Then F isDFp[F ]-
subgroup rigid.
The previous problem can be divided in two subproblems.
Conjecture 2. Let F be a free group and p an odd prime. Let H be finitely
generated subgroup of F and eH its L2-closure. Then
(1) βFp[F ]
0 (IFp[F ]/FIFp[H]) =βQ[F ]
0 (IQ[F ]/FIQ[H]) and
(2) the left Fp[F ]-moduleIFp[F ]/FIFp[ eH] isDFp[F ]-torsion-free.
The invariant π(H ≤ F ) is related with the primitivity rank of H in F
introduced by Puder and Parzanchevski [PP15]:
π(H≤F ) = min{rk(L) :H⪇L≤F, H is not a free factor of L}.
It is clear that
π(H≤F ) = min{π(H≤F ), rk(H)}.
By analogy, if K is a field, we can define
πK(H≤F ) = min{rk(N) : FIK[H]⪇N≤IK[F ],
FIK[H] is not a free summand of N}.
The following conjecture is a variation of [EWPS24, Conjecture 1.9].
Conjecture 3. Let F be a free group and H a finitely generated subgroup. Then
for any field K, πK(H≤F ) =π(H≤F ).
As mentioned in the introduction, we anticipate that our methods can be ex-
tended to establish L2-subgroup rigidity not only for free groups. To provide a
specific focus, we put forth the following conjecture.
Conjecture 4. Locally indicable groups are L2-subgroup rigid.
On the other hand we expect that there are non L2-subgroup rigid groups.
Problem 5. Construct non L2-subgroup rigid (torsion-free) groups.
