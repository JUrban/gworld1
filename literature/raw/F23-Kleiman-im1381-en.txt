          ABSTRACT. In this paper a new method of studying identity relations in groups is described.
          Among the results obtained by this method is an example of a group variety that does not
          have an independent basis of identities. A conjecture by P. Hall on marginal subgroups is
          refuted. The question of the relation between the representation of a group variety in the
          form of a union and the product of two proper subvarieties is considered. An example of a
          group variety having a set of continuum many different covering varieties is constructed
          and a number of results are obtained, as corollaries, on whether (solvable) group varieties
          can be generated by certain classes of groups.

   The following circle of questions concerns covering elements in the lattice of varieties of
groups and the generation of varieties by certain classes of groups. As Ol'shansku [11]
observed, every variety of groups has a covering variety. Using his arguments, it is not

easy to see that all Burnside varieties 93„ (n > 1), and also subvarieties of locally finite

semigroups there are examples of varieties having continuum many different covering
varieties (see [12]). In Theorem 7.3, we provide an example of a subvariety of 217 having a
continuum of covering varieties in 217.

   Shortly after Ol'shanskii [14] proved that not every variety of groups is finitely based,
and Adyan [15] and Vaughan-Lee [16] constructed the first examples of infinite indepen-
dent systems of group laws, Tarski posed the following problem (see [13], 4.64): does every
variety of groups have an independent basis for its identities? Ol'shanskii [11] proved the
only result in this direction: every subvariety of a finitely based locally finite variety of
groups is independently based. In particular, every solvable locally finite variety has an
independent basis. In Theorem 8.5, we give an example of a variety of solvable groups of
length 7 not having an independent basis. Thus the answer to Tarski's problem is in the
negative. It also proves the necessity of the condition on local finiteness in Ol'shanskii's
result. Earlier negative answers to the analogous result in the varieties of semigroups and
inverse semigroups were obtained by Trakhtman [17] and Kidman [18]. The variety of
Theorem 8.5 can be given explicitly by identities in less than 100 variables. It is curious to
note that this variety lies in 9i22l^ii> whereas every subvariety of 919}C is independently
based. This latter result follows from [11], insofar as the proof of the result of Ol'shanskii
quoted above uses only the fact that every subvariety of a locally finite variety is generated
by finite groups; that is, the condition on local finiteness can be replaced by the
assumption that every subvariety is generated by finite groups. We add that the variety
figuring in Theorem 8.5 is the intersection of two independently based varieties.
