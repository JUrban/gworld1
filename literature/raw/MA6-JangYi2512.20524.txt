number is free or not. In particular, the following conjecture is a long-standing open problem.
Conjecture 1.2. Every rational number α with |α| < 4 is non-free.

   In [7], A. F. Beardon revealed an interesting relationship between Pell’s equation and non-free rational
numbers. This result was later extended in [33]. Both papers provide classes of convergent sequences of
non-free rational numbers. Smilga constructed sequences of √ non-free rational numbers converging to several
real numbers in [32]. The list of limit points includes 2 + 2, which is currently the largest known limit
point of non-free rational numbers. Generalizing Smilga’s work, further families of sequences of non-free
rational numbers were obtained in [11]. Despite these contributions, it remains unknown whether there
exists a sequence of non-free rational numbers converging to 3 or 4 (see [13]). In this paper, we address the
case of 3.
Theorem 1.6 (Corollary 4.5). 3 is a limit point of non-free rational numbers.
Indeed, we prove that any rational number of the form
                                    2n3 + 2n5 − 1

                                    n3 n4 (2n5 − 1)
turns out to be a limit point of limit points of non-free rational numbers. This result reveals that many
rational numbers arise as limit points of limit points of non-free rational numbers. Moreover, we find that
the rational numbers
                                         3                        9n + 5
                                 3+              and 3 +
                                     2(9n − 1)              3(2n + 1)(9n + 4)
are non-free for all n ∈ Z. See Theorem 4.4 and Theorem 5.2, respectively.
   After the initial submission of this manuscript to the arXiv, we became aware that an equivalent result to
Theorem 1.6 was independently obtained by Ilia Smilga in [11]. While the statements are equivalent, the
proofs rely on different techniques: we use the orbit test, whereas their approach is based on half-relation
polynomials. In particular, our sequences are different from theirs.
   The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly recall the orbit test, the corresponding prin-
cipal congruence subgroup, and Pell’s equation. We use the orbit test and the corresponding modulo ho-
momorphism to prove the first main result, Corollary 3.2, in Section 3. Using a similar technique, we also
characterize which Gα contains special types of matrices in this section. In Section 4, we provide rational
numbers that are limit points of non-free rational numbers (Theorem 4.4). As a corollary, 3 turns out to be a
limit point of non-free rational numbers (Corollary 4.5). In Section 5, we explicitly construct sequences of
non-free rational numbers that converge to 3.
