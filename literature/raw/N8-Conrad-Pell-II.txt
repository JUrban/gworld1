                                      1. Introduction
   In Part I we met Pell’s equation x2 − dy 2 = 1 for nonsquare d in Z+ .1 We stated
Lagrange’s theorem that every Pell equation has a nontrivial solution (an integral solution
besides (±1, 0)) and saw what all solutions to x2 −dy 2 = 1 are if there’s a nontrivial solution.
As in Part I, “solution” means integral solution. Here we will prove Lagrange’s theorem
in Section 2 and show in Section 3 how to find all solutions of a generalized Pell equation
x2 − dy 2 = n. Examples are in Section 4.
