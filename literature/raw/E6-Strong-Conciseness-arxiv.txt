                                             STRONG CONCISENESS AND EQUATIONALLY NOETHERIAN
                                                                 GROUPS

                                                                   IKER DE LAS HERAS AND ANDONI ZOZAYA
arXiv:2502.07427v1 [math.GR] 11 Feb 2025




                                                   Abstract. A word w is said to be concise in a class of groups if, for every G in
                                                   that class such that the set of w-values w{G} is ﬁnite, the verbal subgroup w(G)
                                                   is also ﬁnite. In the context of proﬁnite groups, the notion of strong conciseness
                                                   imposes a more demanding condition on w, requiring that w(G) is ﬁnite whenever
                                                   |w{G}| < 2ℵ0 . We investigate the relation between these two properties and the
                                                   notion of equationally Noetherian groups, by proving that in a proﬁnite group G
                                                   with a dense equationally Noetherian subgroup, w{G} is ﬁnite whenever |w{G}| <
                                                   2ℵ0 . Consequently, we conclude that every word is strongly concise in the classes
                                                   of proﬁnite linear groups, pro-C completions of residually C linear groups and pro-C
                                                   completions of virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups, thereby extending well-known
                                                   conciseness properties of these classes of groups.




                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                              A word w in k variables is an element of the free group on k generators. For
                                           every group G, we can interpret a word as a map w : Gk → G defined by substitution
                                           of group elements for variables. Verbal problems in groups study the relationship
                                           between the set of w-values in G, defined as
                                                                         w{G} = {w(g1 , . . . , gk ) | gi ∈ G},
                                           and the verbal subgroup of w in G, defined as w(G) = hw{G}i. Consistent with the
                                           terminology introduced by P. Hall, we say that w is concise in a class of groups C if,
                                           for all G in C, the verbal subgroup w(G) is finite whenever w{G} is finite.
                                              In the context of profinite groups, this notion has been recently extended (cf. [7, 8]):
                                           we say that w is strongly concise in a class C of profinite groups if, for any G in C, the
                                           subgroup w(G) is finite whenever |w{G}| < 2ℵ0 . The study of conciseness and strong
                                           conciseness has drawn significant attention in recent years (cf. [3, 5, 9, 12, 16, 20]).
                                              Even though P. Hall conjectured that every word is concise in the class of all groups,
                                           this was proved false by Ivanov in [10], by building an example of a word that is not
                                           concise in a certain non-residually finite group. However, it remains unknown whether
                                           all words are concise – or even strongly concise – in the class consisting of all profinite
                                           groups.

   Several classes of groups are known to have the property that every word is concise
on them. For instance, every word is concise in the class of linear groups (Merz-
jalkov [15]), or in the class of residually finite groups all of their quotients are resid-
ually finite (Turner-Smith [17]). In particular, it follows from the latter that every
word is concise in the classes of virtually abelian-by-nilpotent or virtually abelian-by-
polycyclic groups.
   Among the aforementioned classes, strong conciseness has been so far only proved
for virtually nilpotent profinite groups, meaning that every word is strongly concise in
this class of groups (cf. [6, Theorem 1.1] and [7, Theorem 1.2]). In this article we es-
tablish the same property for profinite analogues of two previously mentioned classes:
profinite linear groups (profinite groups with an embedding into matrix groups), and
pro-C completions of either linear or abelian-by-polycyclic groups. Here and hence-
forth, C refers to a formation of finite groups – a class of finite groups closed under
taking quotients and subdirect products of its members. This study is based on
the relationship between conciseness, strong conciseness, and the notion of equation-
ally Noetherian groups, a key concept in algebraic geometry over groups; we refer
to Section 2 for the definition. We remark that the connection between equational
Noetherianity and residual finiteness has already been studied in [18].
  The main result of this paper is the following:
Theorem 1.1. Let G be a profinite group and let w be a word such that |w{G}| < 2ℵ0 .
Suppose that G has an equationally Noetherian subgroup that is dense in G with respect
to the profinite topology. Then w{G} is finite.
   As a result, since linear groups are well-known to be equationally Noetherian (cf.
[4, Theorem 3.5]), we extend Merzjalkov’s Theorem as follows:
Corollary 1.2. Every word is strongly concise in the class of profinite linear groups.
  Furthermore, Theorem 1.1 also applies to pro-C completions of residually C linear
groups, which might not be linear themselves. For instance, it is still unknown whether
non-abelian free pro-p groups are linear over any field (cf. [14, Conjecture 3.8]).
Corollary 1.3. Every word is strongly concise in the class of groups consisting of
pro-C completions of residually C linear groups.
   Finitely generated abelian-by-polycyclic groups are residually finite (cf. [13, §7.2])
and equationally Noetherian (cf. [18, Theorem 1.3]). Thus Theorem 1.1 implies
that every word is strongly concise in any profinite group containing a dense sub-
group of this kind. In the following theorem, we extend this result to (non-necessarily
finitely generated) virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups, although it remains un-
known whether they are equationally Noetherian.
Theorem 1.4. Let G be a profinite group, and suppose that G has a dense virtually
abelian-by-polycyclic subgroup. Then every word is strongly concise in G.
  As a consequence, we obtain:
Corollary 1.5. Every word is strongly concise in the class of:
   (i) pro-C completions of virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups,
  (ii) profinite virtually abelian-by-(finitely generated nilpotent) groups.
   In the previous result, finitely generated is understood as topologically finitely gen-
erated. The following remains open:

                                            2
Question 1.6. Is every word strongly concise in the class of virtually abelian-by-
polyprocyclic groups?
   The question falls outside the techniques of this paper, as a polyprocyclic group
may not contain a dense polycyclic subgroup. For instance, for an odd prime p, the
pro-p group G = Zp ⋊ Zp , topologically generated by x and y, and with the action
given by xy = x2 , does not contain any dense metacyclic subgroup. However, every
word is strongly concise in the class of virtually polyprocyclic pro-p groups, which
includes the group in the previous example (cf. [11, Theorem 1.2]).
  Finally, abelian-by-nilpotent groups may not be nor equationally Noetherian (cf. [1,
Propositions 4.1 & 5]) nor abelian-by-polycyclic, and, therefore, the following remains
open as well:
Question 1.7. Is every word strongly concise in the class of profinite virtually abelian-
by-nilpotent groups?

                                       2. Preliminaries
   Given a group G and the free group on k variables Fk = F (x1 , . . . , xk ), every
element w of the free-product G ∗ Fk can be represented using the variables x1 , . . . , xk
and, if necessary, additional constants a1 , . . . , aℓ from G. That is,
                                w = ŵ(a1 , . . . , aℓ , x1 , . . . , xk )
for a suitable group word ŵ in l + k variables. By analogy with word maps, w defines
in G a so-called generalised word map
        w : Gk → G,     (g1 , . . . , gk ) 7→ w(g1 , . . . , gk ) = ŵ(a1 , . . . , aℓ , g1 , . . . , gk ).
The verbal topology on Gk is the topology whose sub-basis for the closed sets consists
of the solution sets
                     n                                                              o
            VG (S) = (g1 , . . . , gk ) ∈ Gk | w(g1 , . . . , gk ) = 1 for all w ∈ S ,
where S ⊆ G∗Fk . We say that G is equationally Noetherian if, for all k, any descending
chain of verbally closed subsets in Gk stabilizes (cf. [18, Lemma 3.1] for alternative
characterizations). A straightforward consequence, which will play a central role in
the article, is that the connected component of the identity in G with respect to the
verbal topology, also known as the irreducible component of G, is a normal irreducible
subgroup of finite index (cf. [19, Lemma 5.2]).
   Before delving further, we should establish some terminology. Given a set X con-
tained in two distinct groups Gk and H k , one may consider the verbal topology on
both Gk and H k . To avoid confusion, we will denote them respectively as the G-
verbal and H-verbal topologies. Furthermore, in a profinite group G, these verbal
topologies will be frequently used simultaneously with the original profinite topology
of G. Therefore, topological concepts will be clarified with a descriptor indicating the
topology being used: profinite-continuous, verbally connected, H-verbally closed, etc.
   Finally, given a subset X ⊆ G, its profinite closure in G will be denoted by clp (X),
while the verbal closure in G will be denoted by clv (X), or clGv (X) when we want to
specify the group.

products:

                                      3. Proof of the main result
   In this section we prove Theorem 1.1 and deduce Corollary 1.3. We start stating
the following result, which is often key when working on strong conciseness. Recall
that a profinite space is a topological space that is Hausdorff, compact and totally
disconnected.
Proposition 3.1 ([7, Proposition 2.1]). Let F : X → Y be a continuous map between
non-empty profinite spaces, and suppose that |F (X)| < 2ℵ0 . Then, there exists a
non-empty profinite-open subset U ⊆ X such that F |U is constant.

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Let H be a profinite-dense equationally Noetherian subgroup
of G, and let H0 be the irreducible component of H, which turns out to be a normal
subgroup of finite index in H (cf. [19, Lemma 5.2]). On the one hand, since H
is profinite-dense in G, the subgroup clp (H0 ) has finite index in clp (H) = G. On
the other hand, Proposition 3.2 shows that clp (H0 ) is marginal for w in G. As
|w{G}| ≤ |G : clp (H0 )|k , where k is the number of variables in w, it follows that
w{G} is finite.                                                                   
   By combining the theorem above and the following simple lemma, Corollary 1.3
follows immediately, as a residually C group has a dense embedding into its pro-C
completion.
Lemma 3.3. Let G be a Hausdorff topological group and let H ≤ G be a sequentially
dense subgroup of G. If w{H} is finite for a word w, then w(G) = w(H).
Proof. For each tuple (g1 , . . . , gk ) in Gk there exist sequences (hi, n )n∈N in H, with
i ∈ {1, . . . , k}, such that (hi,n )n∈N converges to gi . Then, by the continuity of word
maps, it follows that
                         (w (h1,n , . . . , hk,n ))n∈N → w(g1 , . . . , gk )
is convergent. Since w{H} is finite and G is a Hausdorff topological space, the se-
quence (w(h1,n , . . . , hk,n ))n∈N eventually stabilises, i.e.
                            w (h1,n , . . . , hk,n ) = w(g1 , . . . , gk )
for every large enough n. Consequently, w{G} = w{H}, and so w(G) = w(H).                 

                   4. Virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups
  In this last section, we prove Theorem 1.4. The following lemma will be useful.
Lemma 4.1. Let G be a virtually abelian-by-polycyclic group. Then there exists an
abelian normal subgroup A of G such that G/A is virtually polycyclic.
Proof. Let H be a finite-index normal abelian-by-polycyclic subgroup of G, and let
B E H be an abelian subgroup such that H/B is polycyclic. Write n = |G : H| and
let g1 , . . . , gn ∈ G be representative elements for each right coset of H in G. Being B
normal in H, the subgroup A = ∩ni=1 B gi is abelian and normal in G. We will show
that G/A is virtually polycyclic.
   For that purpose define recursively A0 = B and Ai = Ai−1 ∩ B gi , for 1 ≤ i ≤ n.
Note that each Ai is a normal subgroup of H and that An = A. It thus suffices to
show that each H/Ai is polycyclic. We already know it for A0 = B, so assume by
induction that H/Ai−1 is polycyclic for i ≥ 1. Thus, the subgroup B gi Ai−1 /Ai−1 of

H/Ai−1 is polycyclic too, and hence B gi /Ai is polycyclic. Since the homomorphic
image H/B gi of H/B is also polycyclic, then H/Ai is itself polycyclic.        

Corollary 4.3. Let G be a virtually abelian-by-polycyclic group, and let A E G be an
abelian normal subgroup of G such that G/A is virtually polycyclic (cf. Lemma 4.1).
Let A0 ≤ A be the G-verbally connected component of the identity in A. Then A0 has
finite index in A and is normal in G.
Proof. Since Z[G/A] is right Noetherian (cf. [13, § 4.2.3]), Theorem 4.2 shows that
A satisfies the descending chain condition on G-verbally closed subgroups, and thus
|A : A0 | is finite by [19, Lemma 5.2]. Moreover, A0 is invariant under G-verbally
continuous automorphisms, so in particular it is invariant under the conjugation by
any element g ∈ G.                                                                
Proof of Theorem 1.4. Let H be a dense virtually abelian-by-polycyclic subgroup of
G. Suppose that |w{G}| < 2ℵ0 for some word w. Since every word is concise in the
class of virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups, by Lemma 3.3, it is enough to prove
that w{G} is finite.
   Let A be an abelian normal subgroup of H such that H/A is virtually polycyclic
(cf. Lemma 4.1). By Corollary 4.3, we may assume A is H-verbally connected, so
applying Proposition 3.2, we obtain that clp (A) is marginal for w in G.
   Let T ⊆ H be a finite subset of H such that H = hT iA, and write T = clp (hT i).
Note that hT i is a finitely generated virtually abelian-by-polycyclic group, so it is
equationally Noetherian by [18, Theorem 1.3] and [2, Theorem 1]. Hence, |w{T }| < ∞
by Theorem 1.1. On the other hand, G = T · clp (A), and since clp (A) is marginal for
w in G, we conclude that w{G} = w{T }.                                              
