                                                      Braid groups and Burnside groups
                                                                                 Ethan Dlugie
                                                                                    July 2026
arXiv:2607.13316v1 [math.GR] 14 Jul 2026




                                                                                     Abstract
                                                     There exists an exceptional quotient of braid groups Br4 ↠ Br3
                                                 that is related to many interesting constructions in algebra, topol-
                                                 ogy, and geometry. This quotient map also descends to a quotient
                                                 of “truncated” braid groups Br4 (d) ↠ Br3 (d), which have an added
                                                 torsion relation on their half twist generators. In this article, we find
                                                 a presentation for the kernel of this truncated quotient map that takes
                                                 the form of what we deem a “primitive” Burnside group. We give a
                                                 few finiteness results on these primitive Burnside groups. Our meth-
                                                 ods are purely group theoretic, but we comment on an interpretation
                                                 involving Lefschetz fibrations at the end.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           The braid groups are a family of groups with wide application in topology,
                                           number theory, algebraic geometry, and more. The n-strand braid group for
                                           us will be defined by the presentation

                                               Brn (d) = ⟨σ1 , . . . , σn−1 ∣ σi σi+1 σi = σi+1 σi σi+1 , σi σj = σj σi if ∣i − j∣ > 1⟩ .

                                           It is known that the normal subgroup ⟪σ1 σ3−1 ⟫ ◁ Br4 is isomorphic to a free
                                           group of rank 2 (with earliest written sources [Gas61, Theorem 7] and [GL69,
                                           Theorem 2.6]). A fun exercise shows that appending the relation σ1 σ3−1 = 1
                                           to Br4 yields a presentation of Br3 . Therefore we have a short exact sequence
                                                                                               ψ
                                                                           1 → F2 → Br4 Ð
                                                                                        → Br3 → 1                                           (1)

                                           which splits by a standard inclusion of braid groups Br3 ≤ Br4 .

                                                                                           1
    In this article, we are concerned with what we call truncated braid groups.
For positive integers n and d, such a group is defined as Brn (d) = Brn /⟪σ1d ⟫,
where ⟪−⟫ denotes normal closure. (Note that all σi are conjugate in Brn .)
These groups were first considered in [Cox59]. The quotient map ψ descends
to a quotient map of truncated braid groups:

                                ψ̄ ∶ Br4 (d) ↠ Br3 (d).

For small values of d, these groups are finite, allowing for computer investiga-
tion. The author was surprised to see that the kernel of ψ̄ in some cases was
isomorphic to a free Burnside group of rank 2. Recall that the free Burnside
group of rank n and exponent d is defined by the presentation

           Fn (d) = ⟨x1 , . . . , xn ∣ w(x1 , . . . , xn )d = 1 for all words w⟩.

The study of the free Burnside groups and their relatives, specifically ques-
tions about their finiteness, led to profound developments in algebra in the
20th century (see e.g. [Ols91]), including the work of Zelmanov that garnered
a Fields medal in 1994.
    To identify the kernel of ψ̄ in all cases, we introduce a new construction
in the world of Burnside-flavored groups.

Definition 1.1. The primitive Burnside group of rank n and exponent d is
the group P Fn (d) given by the presentation

    P Fn (d) = ⟨x1 , . . . , xn ∣ w(x1 , . . . , xn )d = 1 for all primitive words w⟩.

    Recall here that a word in a free group is primitive if it can be extended
to a basis for the free group. For example xy 3 is primitive in ⟨x, y⟩, but x2 y 2
is not.

Theorem 1.2. For all d, there is a short exact sequence
                                                   ψ̄
                       1 → P F2 (d) → Br4 (d) Ð
                                              → Br3 (d) → 1

In fact, the sequence is split, and we have a semidirect product decomposition

                             Br4 (d) ≈ P F2 (d) ⋊ Br3 (d).



                                             2
    Our methods are purely of combinatorial group theory, but they come
from topological intuition as explained in Section 3.
    The truncated braid groups are well understood as examples of complex
reflection groups. In particular, their orders are known. This yields the
following corollary concerning the orders of some primitive Burnside groups.

Corollary 1.3. We have the following identifications:

    • P F2 (2) ≈ F2 (2) of order 4

    • P F2 (3) ≈ F2 (3) of order 27

    • P F2 (4) and P F2 (5) are both infinite groups

   Note by contrast that F2 (4) is finite. The group F2 (5) is the lowest case
in which the finiteness or infiniteness of a free Burnside group is unknown.
Perhaps this knowledge of the associated primitive Burnside group could
shed some light on this question.

Lemma 2.2. The braid group acts transitively on conjugacy classes of prim-
itive words in F2 via the modular action.
Proof. Let u, v ∈ F2 be primitive words. Then there exists some f ∈ Aut(F2 )
with f (u) = v. Considering elements of the group up to conjugacy suggests
that we might look at their images in the abelianization F2ab ≈ Z2 . The
group elements map to primitive vectors [u], [v] ∈ Z2 , and the induced map
Aut(F2 ) → GL(2, Z) maps the automorphism to a linear transformation [f ] ∈
GL(2, Z). Without loss of generality, e.g. by inverting the value of f on a
second element of a basis with u, we assume that [f ] ∈ SL(2, Z).
    Now consider the induced map on the automorphisms of the modular
action. We see that ϕ(σ1 ) and ϕ(σ2 ) map to the matrices

                                1 −1                      0 −1
                         A=(         )      and B = (          )
                                0 1                       1 2

respectively. A quick computation gives

so we see that A and C generate all of SL(2, Z). Therefore there is an
element g ∈ Br3 such that [ϕ(g)] = [f ] ∈ SL(2, Z). Nielsen [Nie17] showed
that the kernel of the map Aut(F2 ) → GL(2, Z) is exactly the subgroup of
inner automorphisms, so ϕ(g) and f differ by a conjugation. Therefore, ϕ(g)
maps the conjugacy class of u to that of v.

Lemma 2.5 (Quotients of semidirect products). Suppose that

                                     →Q→1

of Q. Then the natural quotient map ψ̄ ∶ G/⟪N ⟫ → Q/N has kernel isomor-
phic to K/[K, N ] where [K, N ] = ⟨knk −1 n−1 ⟩k∈K,n∈N is the mixed commutator
subgroup. In addition, the short exact sequence
                                                 ψ̄
                     1 → K/[K, N ] → G/⟪N ⟫ Ð
                                            → Q/N → 1

is split in the clear way.
Proof. Write ψ ′ ∶ G → Q/N for the quotient of the original map ψ. The
splitting gives a unique way to write elements of G as products kq for some
k ∈ K and q ∈ Q, and then ψ ′ (kq) = qN . Therefore ker ψ ′ = {kq ∣ k ∈ K, q ∈
N } = KN .

Now ψ ′ descends to the map ψ̄ ∶ G/⟪N ⟫ → Q/N in question. It follows then
that ker(ψ̄) = KN /⟪N ⟫.

It also clear that [K, N ]N ≤ ⟪N ⟫, so that ⟪N ⟫ = [K, N ]N . So now we have

3    A topological interpretation
The ideas in this section are shared without proof, but we think they are
useful to share nonetheless. The map ψ ∶ Br4 → Br3 is not purely group

Figure 2: The space Conf 3 [d] is the 3-sphere with a Z/d orbifold locus along
a trefoil knot. The space Conf 4 [d] is a S1,1 bundle on this space away from
the knot, while the fibers collapse to pinched surfaces along the knot. The
pinch points correspond to 4-point configurations with two double collisions.

theoretic. It is the map on π1 induced by a map of configuration spaces
Conf 4 (C) → Conf 3 (C) given as {a, b, c, d} ↦ {ab + cd, ac + bd, ad + bc}. See
[CS25] for more discussion of this map and its relatives. This map of config-
uration spaces turns out to be a fiber bundle with fiber the once-punctured
torus S1,1 . The long exact sequence on homotopy groups yields the short
exact sequence (1).
    To obtain the analogous map on truncated braid groups, one must per-
form a sort of orbifold filling on the configuration spaces. For Conf 3 (C),
which is homotopy equivalent to the trefoil knot complement in the 3-sphere,
this is rather direct. See e.g. my earlier article [DS25]. Call the filled space
Conf 3 [d].
    For Conf 4 (C), one has to be a bit careful. The obvious compactification
of configuration space needs to be blown up along the part corresponding
to triple collisions of points, along the lines of [FM94]. After doing this,
orbifold surgery should be more direct, yielding a space Conf 4 [d]. The map
on orbifolds is no longer a fiber bundle but should be thought of as an orbifold
Lefschetz fibration, since the fibers over the singular part of Conf 3 [d] are
pinched surfaces and have nontrivial ambient orbifold isotropy. The cartoon
is something like Figure 2.
    The typical short exact sequence of Lefschetz fibrations should have an
orbifold version akin to

   1        π1 (S1,1 )/C d      π1 (Conf 4 [d])       π1 (Conf 3 [d])      1
                                     ≈                     ≈

                                   Br4 (d)               Br3 (d)

where C ⊂ π1 (S1,1 ) denotes the set of so-called vanishing cycles, a particular
set of simple closed curves in the fiber along with their orbits under the
monodromy action. In this case, the monodromy action Br3 ↷ π1 (S1,1 ) is
given by Definition 2.1, and C consists of all simple closed curves in S1,1
(which are the same as primitive elements of F2 ).
    This topological interpretation led me to discover the main result of this
work. I find the topological story more enlightening, but I felt that the group
theory was more direct than trying to develop a theory of orbifold Lefschetz
fibrations. Perhaps the development of such a theory could come in future
work.
