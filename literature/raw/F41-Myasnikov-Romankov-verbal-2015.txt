               On Asymptotic Properties of Verbal Subsets in a Group
                                               A. Myasnikov* and V. Roman’kov**

                                                      (Submitted by Steﬀen Lempp)
                         Stevens Institute of Technology, Castle Point Terrace 1, Hoboken, USA

          Abstract—We prove that the asymptotic density ρ(w[Fr ]) of the verbal set w[Fr ] in a free group
          Fr , r ≥ 2, is equal to 0 for every word w with exponent sum exp(w) = 1. When exp(w) = 1 one has
          w[Fr ] = Fr , and so ρ(w[Fr ]) = 1.

          DOI: 10.1134/S1995080215040113
          Keywords and phrases: free group, verbal set, asymptotic density.


                                                               1. INTRODUCTION
   In this paper we describe asymptotic densities of verbal sets in free groups. In particular, we show
that a proper verbal subset in a free non-abelian group is negligible, i.e., it has asymptotic density zero.
This also follows from a much larger (and much harder) result on the description of deﬁnable sets in free
groups [7], however, here we give a direct and simple proof.

                                                                 1.1. Verbal Subsets
    Let F be a free group of inﬁnite countable rank with basis X = {x1 , . . . , xk , . . . }. We view F
as the set of all reduced words in X equipped with the standard multiplication (concatenation and
consequent reduction). For a positive integer n by Fn we denote the free group of rank n with basis
Xn = {x1 , . . . , xn }, which is the subgroup of F generated by Xn . For an arbitrary group G and a word
w = w(x1 , . . . , xn ) ∈ F we denote by w[G] the set of all images of w under all possible homomorphisms
ϕ : F → G. In other words, w[G] consists of all elements of the form w(g1 , . . . , gn ), where g1 , . . . , gn are
elements of G. Let w(G) be the verbal subgroup of G, generated by the set w[G]. A subset S of G is
called verbal if S = w[G] for some word w ∈ F .
    In recent years there has been a great deal of interest in verbal sets and verbal subgroups in groups.
We refer to [12, 9, 8, 17] for results in ﬁnite groups, to [15, 16, 18, 14, 10] for verbal width in inﬁnite
groups, and to [6, 5, 1, 2] for stable verbal length.
    The structure of a verbal subset in Fn may well be very complex, as Razborov showed in [13] for the
verbal sets deﬁned by quadratic words of the type Πni=1 [xi , yi ]. We proved in [11] that for any group word
w and every n ≥ 2 the set w[Fn ] is rational in Fn if and only if w[Fn ] = Fn or w[Fn ] = 1. Recall, that a
subset R of a monoid G is called rational if R can be obtained from the ﬁnite subsets of G by a sequence
of rational operations, i.e., union, product and submonoid generation. Alternatively, a rational subset
can be deﬁned as a set recognized by a ﬁnite automaton over G. Furthermore, it was shown in [4] that
proper verbal subsets in Fn are not even context-free.
    For any positive integer i ∈ N and each word w ∈ F we deﬁne by σi (w) the i-th exponent of w
that is equal to the sum of all exponents of xi in w. By exp(w) we denote gcd(σi (w)|i ∈ N). We write
exp(w) = 0 when σi (w) = 0 for all i ∈ N, and call such w a commutator word. In this case w ∈ F  , the
derived (commutator) subgroup of F .

   For sake of completeness we formulate here the following known, easy, but useful result.
   Lemma 1.1. Let w = w(x1 , . . . , xn ) be a word in F and G be an arbitrary group. Then exp(w) = 1
implies w[G] = G for an arbitrary group G. On the other hand, if exp(w) = 1 then w[Fr ] = Fr for
every r ≥ 2.
   Proof. If exp(w) = 1 then there are integers k1 , . . . , kn such that
                                       k1 σ1 (w) + · · · + kn σn (w) = 1.
Then for an arbitrary element g in G one has g = w(gk1 , . . . , gkn ), and the ﬁrst claim follows. Otherwise,
if exp(w) = 1 then w[Ar ] = Ar even for a free abelian group Ar of rank r, hence the second claim.            2
     Wicks found [21] an algorithm for solving quadratic equations of the form [x, y] = g and x2 y 2 = g
in Fr , r ∈ N. In particular, he proved that an element g = 1 in Fr is a commutator, i.e., the equation
[x1 , x2 ] = g has a solution in Fr , if and only if g is a conjugate of a cyclically reduced word of the form
ABCA−1 B −1 C −1 , with A, B, C ∈ Fr , and where at most one of A, B, C is equal to the identity.
     We deﬁne the genus of an element v ∈ F  to be the least positive integer c such that v is a product
of c commutators in F . Every element of genus c in F  can be obtain by a non-cancellating substitution
into one of ﬁnitely many ﬁxed words, the so called Wicks form of genus c (see, for instance, [19, 20, 3]).
     Given a reduced word w = w(x1 , . . . , xn ) one may construct the verbal set w[Fr ] by repeating the
follows: choose some reduced words A1 , . . . , An ∈ Fr , substitute them for variables x1 , . . . , xn in w, and
then reduce obtaining a word in w[Fr ]. Clearly, any two such words Ai and Aj may cancel at most once
(if, as usual, we do the maximal possible cancellation) in the reduction process. Hence, there exists a
ﬁnite number of all possible “cancellation schemes” in reduction of the words w(A1 , . . . , An ). It follows
that for every word w there are ﬁnitely many words ui (y1 , . . . , ym ) such that every non-trivial word u from
w[Fr ] can be presented without cancellation in the form ui (P1 , . . . , Pm ) for some P1 , . . . , Pm ∈ Fr and a
suitable i. Notice, that the forms ui (y1 , . . . , ym ) are not quite the same as the Wick’s forms, since we do
not claim that every reduced word of the form ui (P1 , . . . , Pm ) actually gives an element from w[Fr ].

             2. ASYMPTOTIC DENSITY OF VERBAL SETS IN A FREE GROUP
   Let Fr be a free group and w = w(x1 , . . . , xn ) be an arbitrary reduced word. An equation
                                        w = g,    where    g ∈ Fr ,                                     (6)
is solvable in Fr if and only if g belongs to w[Fr ]. Hence, the question on the “probability” of the
equation (6) to have a solution in Fr reduces to the question on the asymptotic density of the verbal
set w[Fr ] in Fr . By Lemma 1.1 if exp(w) = 1 then ρ(w[Fr ]) = 1 for every stratiﬁcation {Tm }m∈N of Fr .
   It is customary in group theory and combinatorics to consider the word length | · | : Fr → N in the
basis Xr as the size function on Fr . The corresponding stratiﬁcations {Sm }m∈N and {Bm }m∈N are the
standard spherical and ball stratiﬁcations on Fr .


                 2.1. Asymptotic Densities of Verbal Sets in the Inﬁnite Cyclic Group
   It is easy to compute the asymptotic density of verbal sets of the inﬁnite cyclic group F1 .
   Proposition 2.1. The following holds in the inﬁnite cyclic group F1 with respect to the spherical
stratiﬁcation {Sm }m∈N :

    • if exp(w) = 1 then ρ(w[F1 ]) = 1, thus w[F1 ] is generic;

    • if exp(w) = 0, i.e. w is commutator word, then ρ(w[F1 ]) = 0, thus w[F1 ] is negligible;

    • if exp(w) = k, where k = 0, 1, then ρ̄(w[F1 ]) = 1 and ρ(w[Fr ]) = 0.


    Proof. The ﬁrst statement follows from Lemma 1.1 and the second one is obvious. The third statement
is also clear because w[F1 ] ∩ Sm = Sm when k|m, and w[F1 ] ∩ Sm = ∅, when k  |m.                   2

    • if exp(w) = 0, i.e., w is a commutator word, then ρ(w[F1 ]) = 0, thus w[F1 ] is negligible;

    • if exp(w) = k, where k = 0, 1, then ρ(w[F1 ]) = 1/k.

                  2.2. Asymptotic Densities of Verbal Sets in a Non-Cyclic Free Group
   Theorem 2.3. Let Fr , r ≥ 2, be a non-cyclic free group with basis Xr = {x1 , . . . , xr }. Then the
following holds with respect to the spherical, as well as the ball, stratiﬁcations of Fr :

      • if exp(w) = 1 then ρ(w[Fr ]) = 1, thus w[Fr ] is generic:

      • if exp(w) = 1, then ρ(w[Fr ]) = 0, thus w(Fr ] is negligible.

    Proof. The ﬁrst statement follows by Lemma 1.1.
    To prove the second statement we assume that exp(w) = 0 or exp(w) = e = 1. As was described
in the introduction every element in the intersection w[Fr ] ∩ Sk is the image of a non-cancellating
substitution of some P1 , . . . , Pm ∈ Fr into one of the ﬁnitely many cancellation schemes u(y1 , . . . , ym )
with |u(P1 , . . . , Pm )| = k. In a natural way we deﬁne the exponent sum τi (u) as the sum of the exponents
of all occurrences of the piece Pi in u for all i = 1, . . . , m. We claim that τi (u) = 0 or is divided by e. The
latter is possible only if e = 0. Indeed, similar assertion is obviously true when we consider the result
of ﬁrst substitution w(A1 , . . . , An ) (see the description of the process in the introduction). Then we
follow the reductio process and along the way we introduce the pieces Pi for i = 1, . . . , m. Every piece
P occurs as the maximal cancellation of two words At , As or as a “middle” of some At consisting of
those letters that do not cancel at all. Hence the exponent sum w.r.t. P in the form u obtained as the
result of reduction of w(A1 , . . . , An ) is equal to the algebraic sums of corresponding exponents in the
previous words. Hence, this sum is either equal to 0 or is a multiple of e. We can conclude that every piece
Pi occurs in u at least two times (may be with diﬀerent exponents ±1). A value of a non-cancellating
substitution to u of the pieces Pi for i = 1, . . . , m depends on not more than k/2 letters. A number of
such substitutions is ≤ (2r)k/2 , Since |Sk | = 2r(2r − 1)k−1 we derive that the asymptotic density for
every cancellation scheme u is 0. Since there are only ﬁnitely many cancellation schemes u the second
claim in the case of spheres follows. The result on the ball stratiﬁcation is obtained in a similar way. 2
