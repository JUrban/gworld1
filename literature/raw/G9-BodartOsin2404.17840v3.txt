                                                                                         Abstract

                                                   We provide examples of groups with transcendental spectral radius:
                                                    • We first construct finitely presented examples, using links between decidability
                                                      of the Word Problem and semi-computability of the spectral radius. This
                                                      argument extends to the exponential growth rate and the asymptotic entropy.
                                                    • We also construct a finitely generated example with decidable Word Problem,
                                                      using classical small-cancellation theory.
                                                   Along the way, we prove that C ′ (1/6) groups satisfy the Rapid Decay property,
                                                   and deduce some properties on their spectral radii of independent interest.

                                           Throughout the paper, we consider marked groups, that is, pairs (G, S) with G a group
                                           and S a finite symmetric generating multiset (i.e., S = S −1 ). We consider the simple
                                           random walk (Xn )n⩾0 on Cay(G, S), starting at X0 = eG , and define

                                                                                 pG,S (g; n) = P[Xn = g].

                                           Equivalently pG,S (g; n) = |S|1 n #{w ∈ S n | w̄ = g}1 , where w̄ is the evaluation of the
                                           word w as an element of G. The spectral radius is defined as
                                                                                            q
                                                                         ρ(G, S) = lim sup n pG,S (e; n).
                                                                                          n→∞

                                           This quantity depends on the global geometry of Cay(G, S), and is usually hard to
                                           compute. Computations of exact values are limited to virtually free groups, and some
                                           groups constructed from them (direct product, extension by an amenable group). In all
                                           these cases, the value ρ(G, S) turns out to be algebraic (combining [MS83] and [Kes59]).
                                           This changed recently when Kassabov and Pak proved that some marked groups have
                                           transcendental spectral radius [KP24]. However, their proof is non-constructive.

                                                 Most of the time, G and S are clear from the context and g = eG , hence we only write p(n)




                                                                                              1
Our main result is the following:
Theorem A. There exists a finitely presented group G such that the spectral radius
ρ(G, S) is transcendental for all finite symmetric generating sets S.

This answers Question 7.2 of [KP24]. In Section 3, Theorem A is extended to the
exponential growth rate and the asymptotic entropy.

Since the proof of Theorem A uses crucially that G has undecidable Word Problem, we
propose another result of the same flavor:
Theorem B. There exists a finitely generated group G with decidable Word Problem
such that the spectral radius ρ(G, S) is transcendental for a specific generating set S.

The construction uses classical C ′ (1/6) small-cancellation theory. As a key ingredient,
we use that these groups satisfy the Rapid Decay property (uniformly):
Theorem C. If G = ⟨S | R⟩ is a C ′ ( 61 ) presentation (with R possibly infinite), then
(G, dS ) has the Rapid Decay property with P (r) = (10r + 1)3 (see Definition 4.1).

Arzhantseva and Drut, u have already proven that C ′ (1/10) groups have the Rapid Decay
property [AD12]. Our proof is a simplification of their, and gives a single polynomial
P (r). A version of this proof, due to the second author, was first circulated in 2014.

As a byproduct, we prove the continuity of the spectral radius on subset of C ′ (1/6)
presentations in the space of marked groups:
Corollary D. Let Gi = ⟨S | Ri ⟩ be a sequence of C ′ (1/6) presentations such that
(Gi , S) → (G, S) in the space of marked groups, then ρ(Gi , S) → ρ(G, S).

This means that, even though the spectral radius is a global invariant, it is determined
by the local geometry of the Cayley graph, among C ′ (1/6) groups. Of course, the same
statement is not true in the entire space of marked groups, since free groups (with
ρ < 1) are residually finite and therefore limits of finite groups (with ρ = 1).

                              2. Proof of Theorem A

2.1. From semi-computability to the Word Problem. The main observation is
Theorem 2.1. Let (G, S) be a recursively presented group such that the spectral radius
ρ(G, S) is upper semi-computable. Consider a subset W ⊆ S ∗ such that, for each
w ∈ W , either w̄ = eG or the normal subgroup ⟨⟨w̄⟩⟩G is non-amenable. Then there
exists an algorithm which, given a word w ∈ W , decides whether w̄ = eG or not.

(In particular, if W = S ∗ , then G has decidable word problem.) This theorem relies
crucially on a classical result due to Kesten.
Theorem 2.2 ([Kes59]). Let (G, S) be a marked group and N ⊴ G a non-amenable
normal subgroup, then we have ρ(G, S) < ρ(G/N, S).

Proof of Theorem 2.1. Suppose that G is presented as ⟨S | R⟩. Take w ∈ W , we run
two parallel processes to decide whether w̄ = eG or not.
  • As G is recursively presented, we can enumerate all words v ∈ S ∗ such that v̄ = eG .
    If at any point v = w, we stop the algorithm and conclude that w̄ = eG .
  • The group Gw = G/ ⟨⟨w̄⟩⟩G = ⟨S | R, w⟩ is recursively presented, hence ρ(Gw , S) is
    lower semi-computable. We can compute two sequences (xk ) and (yk ) such that

                            xk ↘ ρ(G, S) and yk ↗ ρ(Gw , S).

    As xk and yk are real algebraic, we can compare them. If at any point xk < yk , we
    stop the algorithm and conclude that w̄ ̸= eG . It should be noted that, if w̄ ̸= eG ,
    then the inequality xk < yk will hold eventually as ⟨⟨w̄⟩⟩G is non-amenable and
    therefore ρ(G, S) < ρ(G/⟨⟨w̄⟩⟩G , S) = ρ(Gw , S).
In both cases, the algorithm finishes and decides correctly if w̄ = eG or not.

                       3. Extension to other parameters

Similarly to [KP24], the same ideas can be used to provide finitely presented groups
for which the value of various parameters f : M → R (where M is the space of marked
groups) are not computable. The key ingredients are the following:
  • A recursively presented group (G, S) and a subset W ⊆ S ∗ such that
    – the Word Problem restricted to W is undecidable, and
    – f (G, S) < f (G/⟨⟨w̄⟩⟩G , S) for all w ∈ W such that w̄ ̸= eG .
  • For every recursively presented group (Q, S), the value f (Q, S) is lower semi-
    computable, and the result is effective in the sense of Lemma 1.2.
Under these conditions, we may conclude f (G, S) is not upper semi-computable. In the
following subsections, we check both conditions for the growth rate and the asymptotic
entropy (up to a change of sign). In both cases, we may take G = H ∗ Z where H is
any finitely presented group with undecidable Word Problem.

                     4. Property (RD) for C ′ (1/6) groups

4.1. (RD) and (CR). Recall the definition of the Rapid Decay property of [Jol90]:
Definition 4.1 (Property (RD)). Let G be a group with a word metric dS . We say
that G has the Rapid Decay property if there exists a polynomial P ∈ R[X] such that

                                  ∥f ∥op ⩽ P (r) · ∥f ∥2

for all f ∈ CG supported on the ball BS (e, r).
Remark 4.2. The polynomial depends on choice of a word metric dS , but the fact that
G satisfies the property does not.

Throughout the years, many sufficient conditions for the (RD) property have been
developed (see [Sap15]). We will use a condition due to Chatterji and Ruane [CR03].
We state it in the restricted setting of groups acting on themselves:
Definition 4.3 (Property (CR)). A marked group (G, dS ) has the (CR) property if
there exists a G-equivariant map C : G × G → P(G) (the powerset of G) such that
(a) For all x, y ∈ G, we have x ∈ C(x, y).
(b) For all x, y, z ∈ G, we have C(x, y) ∩ C(y, z) ∩ C(z, x) ̸= ∅.
(c) There exists a polynomial Q(r) such that, for all x, y ∈ G and all r ⩾ 0,

                                |C(x, y) ∩ BS (x, r)| ⩽ Q(r).

(d) There exists a polynomial R(r) such that, for all x, y ∈ G satisfying dS (x, y) ⩽ r,
    the diameter of C(x, y) is bounded by R(r).
Remark 4.4. Once again, the specific polynomials Q(r) and R(r) depends on the
choice of the word metric dS , but their mere existence does not.

The link is made through the following result:
Proposition 4.5 ([CR03, Proposition 1.7]). If (G, S) has the (CR) property for Q(r)

and R(r), then it satisfies the Rapid Decay property for P (r) = Q(R(r)) 2 .

6.3. Continuity of ρ(G, S). We have proven that the restriction of ρ : M → (0, 1] to
