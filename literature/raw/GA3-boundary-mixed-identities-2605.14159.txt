                                           Boundary dynamics, triple transitivity, and mixed identities in
                                                           weakly hyperbolic groups
                                                                                  Ekaterina Rybak

                                                                                       Abstract
                                                      We study the interplay between the algebraic and dynamical properties of groups
                                                 that admit a general type action on a δ-hyperbolic space such that the induced action
                                                 on the limit set in the Gromov boundary is faithful. We divide the class of such groups
                                                 into two subclasses based on a dynamical criterion: groups whose induced action on the
arXiv:2605.14159v3 [math.GR] 28 Jul 2026




                                                 limit set is topologically free, and those whose action is not. We prove that satisfying
                                                 the criterion is equivalent to a purely algebraic property of being mixed identity free,
                                                 generalizing results of P. Fima, F. Le Maı̂tre, S. Moon, and Y. Stalder from groups
                                                 acting on trees to groups acting on arbitrary hyperbolic spaces. As one corollary, we
                                                 obtain that the reduced C ∗ -algebra of such groups is selfless, using the results of N.
                                                 Ozawa. For the subclass of groups whose action on the limit set is not topologically
                                                 free, we prove the rigidity result for all 3-transitive faithful actions and bound the
                                                 transitivity degree by 3, generalizing the result of A. Le Boudec and N. Matte Bon. As
                                                 an application, we show that non-affine simple Kac-Moody groups over finite fields are
                                                 MIF, answering the question of J. Belk, F. Fournier-Facio, J. Hyde, and M. Zaremsky.

1     Introduction
1.1       Weakly hyperbolic groups

One of the generalizations of the class of non-elementary hyperbolic groups is the class of
acylindrically hyperbolic groups. Recently, studies of acylindrically hyperbolic groups have
attracted a lot of attention. A group is acylindrically hyperbolic if it admits an acylindrical
action of general type on a hyperbolic space. The acylindricity condition is a generalization
of properness of the action. We refer the reader to [Osi16] for the definitions. The class of
acylindrically hyperbolic groups is large; in particular, it includes all non-elementary hyper-
bolic and relatively hyperbolic groups, mapping class groups of punctured closed surfaces
except for surfaces with genus g = 0 and fewer than three punctures, Out(Fn ) for n ≥ 2,
directly indecomposable right-angled Artin groups, and 1-relator groups with at least 3
generators. Despite being such a broad class, the requirement of the acylindricity of the
action is restrictive enough to obtain nontrivial results about the algebraic properties of
acylindrically hyperbolic groups. For example, acylindrically hyperbolic groups are closed
under taking infinite normal subgroups [Osi16], are SQ-universal, and have finite amenable
radical [DGO]. Finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic groups have cut points in all of
their asymptotic cones [Sis] and have exponential conjugacy growth [HO13].
   We want to enlarge the class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups even further and study
what properties hold when the acylindricity condition is removed. Following the terminology
from [MT], we say
Definition 1.1. A group G is weakly hyperbolic if it admits a general type action on a
geodesic hyperbolic space. An isometric action on a hyperbolic space is of general type if
orbits are unbounded and G contains two loxodromic elements with disjoint fixed points on
the Gromov boundary.

    We refer the reader to Section 2.1 for details on Gromov hyperbolic spaces and the
classification of group actions on them. Examples of weakly hyperbolic groups that are not
necessarily acylindrically hyperbolic come from groups acting on trees and, more generally,
groups of isometries of hyperbolic spaces.
    Many properties of particular examples of weakly hyperbolic groups are well documented
in the literature. Various results on random walks in weakly hyperbolic groups were obtained
in [MT], [Sun], [BMSS], [AMS], [AS], [GTT22], [Cho], and [Hor]. However, not many group
structural results are known about the whole class. Using a simple ping-pong argument,
one can prove that any weakly hyperbolic group contains a nonabelian free subgroup (see
[Gro87, 8.2.F] or [Ham, Thm. 2.7]). In [Osi17], it is proved that for every invariant random
subgroup H of a countable weakly hyperbolic group G, when G acts on some hyperbolic

general type action without any properness condition is not very limiting. Therefore, we
want to impose an additional condition on the group action.
    Recall that the limit set ΛS (G) in the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic space S is the
closure of the set of accumulation points of orbits under the G-action.
Definition 1.2. We say that a group G is faithful weakly hyperbolic if it admits a general
type action on a hyperbolic space S such that the induced G-action on ΛS (G) is faithful.

    Note that not every weakly hyperbolic group is faithful weakly hyperbolic. A source
of examples of weakly hyperbolic groups that are not faithful weakly hyperbolic groups is
given in Proposition 3.31. However, by Proposition A, any weakly hyperbolic group has a
faithful weakly hyperbolic quotient. Here, ES (G) is the elliptic radical with respect to the
action of G on S. It is the set of all elements of G that act on ΛS (G) trivially. We refer the
reader to Section 2.3 for the basic properties of ES (G).
Proposition A. Suppose that a group G admits a general type action on a hyperbolic space
S. Then G/ES (G) is faithful weakly hyperbolic.

Definition 1.3. A weakly hyperbolic group G is called lim-free if it admits a general type
action on a hyperbolic space S such that the induced action on ΛS (G) is topologically free.

    We have an obvious inclusion: Lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups are faithful weakly
hyperbolic groups. While the property of being weakly hyperbolic is not closed under taking
non-trivial normal subgroups, both properties of being faithful weakly hyperbolic and of
being lim-free are closed under taking non-trivial normal subgroups by Lemma 2.26. We
will see that lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups and faithful weakly hyperbolic groups that
are not lim-free are two distinct classes. Groups from the former class include acylindrically
hyperbolic groups without non-trivial finite normal subgroups (see Corollary 3.12) and
behave similarly to them, while groups from the latter class have very different algebraic
properties.

1.2   MIF weakly hyperbolic groups

Let Fn denote a free group of rank n. Recall that a group G satisfies a mixed identity w = 1
for some w ∈ G∗Fn if every homomorphism G∗Fn → G that is the identity on G sends w to
1. A mixed identity w = 1 is non-trivial if w is a non-trivial element of G ∗ Fn , and a group
G is mixed identity free (or MIF for brevity) if it does not satisfy any non-trivial mixed
identity. The property of being MIF imposes strong restrictions on the algebraic structure
of G. If G is MIF, then G has infinite conjugacy classes, it is directly indecomposable, and
it has infinite girth (see [HO16, Prop. 5.4]). A countable group G is MIF if and only if G
and G ∗ Fn are universally equivalent as G-groups for all n ∈ N (see [HO16, Prop. 5.3]). A
surprising connection between MIF groups and the Boone-Higman conjecture was recently
discovered in [BFFHZ].
   In our first main theorem, part (b) reveals a rigidity phenomenon: properties of one
general type action impose strong constraints on all general type actions. Furthermore,
part (c) shows that the geometrically defined class of lim-free groups can be characterized
within the class of faithful weakly hyperbolic groups by a purely algebraic condition.

Theorem B. For any faithful weakly hyperbolic group G, the following conditions are equiv-
alent:

 (a) G is lim-free;

 (b) for any general type action of G on a hyperbolic space with trivial elliptic radical, the
     induced action on the limit set is topologically free;

 (c) G is MIF.

about groups acting on simplicial trees. An action of a group G on a tree T is called

    Let G be a group and w(t) ∈ G ∗ ⟨t⟩ be a mixed identity. The solution set {g ∈ G |
w(g) = 1} is called the primitive solution set corresponding to w(t). The Zariski topology
on G is defined by taking the collection of primitive solution sets to be a subbasis for the
closed sets of the topology. A Zariski-closed subgroup (or more generally, a subset) of G
is called algebraic. The natural question to ask is what proper subgroups of a group G
can be algebraic. If G is torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic, then all such subgroups
are cyclic [KM]. In [Jac], structural conditions for non-elementary algebraic subgroups of
acylindrically hyperbolic groups were obtained. By a similar argument as in the proof of
Theorem B, we get
Corollary C. Let G be a lim-free weakly hyperbolic group. Then G does not contain any
non-trivial proper normal Zariski-closed subgroups.

    Non-lim-free faithful weakly hyperbolic groups also have interesting common purely
algebraic properties. For example, they contain infinite direct sums of infinite non-abelian
subgroups (see Corollary 2.36). Recall that the monolith Mon(G) of a group G is the

                                             5
intersection of all non-trivial normal subgroups of G, and G is said to be monolithic if
Mon(G) ̸= {1}. As a corollary of [GR, Corollary 1.7], we obtain the following.
Proposition D. Let G be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group. If G is not lim-free, then it
is monolithic with an infinite simple monolith.

    In this way, non-lim-free faithful weakly hyperbolic groups are very different from acylin-
drically hyperbolic groups, whose monolith is always trivial (see Proposition 3.11). This
allows us to obtain the result of M. Hull and D. Osin [HO16, Corollary 5.10] that acylindri-
cally hyperbolic groups without nontrivial finite normal subgroups are MIF as a corollary
of Theorem B and Proposition D (see Corollary 3.12).
   Another corollary of Proposition D gives us a large class of lim-free weakly hyperbolic
groups.
Corollary E. Every residually finite faithful weakly hyperbolic group is lim-free. In partic-
ular, every finitely generated linear faithful weakly hyperbolic group is lim-free.

   The famous Tits Alternative states that a finitely generated linear group is either vir-
tually solvable or contains a nonabelian free subgroup. As another corollary of Theorem B,
we obtain an analogous statement for all subgroups of PSL2 (C).
Corollary F. Every subgroup of PSL2 (C) is either virtually solvable or MIF.

    We should note that in [GGS], it is proved that any center-free unbounded and non-
virtually solvable countable subgroup of SL2 (k) is highly transitive, where k is a local field,
which implies being MIF by the result of [HO16]. In [Tom], Tomanov gives necessary and
sufficient conditions for Zariski-dense subgroups of linear groups to be MIF. Corollary F does
not require any assumptions on subgroups of PSL2 (C) and is not an immediate consequence
of Corollary E, [Tom], or [GGS].
    A property called selflessness was recently introduced for a C∗ -algebra by L. Robert.
It implies many important regularity properties such as simplicity, stable rank one in the
tracial setting, and strict comparison. We refer the reader to [Rob] for the definitions. In
[AGKEP], Tattwamasi Amrutam, David Gao, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, and Gre-
gory Patchell proved that acylindrically hyperbolic groups without finite normal subgroups
have selfless reduced C ∗ -algebras. In their proof, they used a stronger quantitative version
of the property of being MIF and the rapid decay property. This attracted attention to the
quantitative version of being MIF, which resulted in a series of works: [BS], [AG], [Vid].
Later, in his remarkable paper, Ozawa obtained a dynamical condition for the group to
have a selfless reduced C ∗ -algebra. Using the results of [Oza], we obtain
Corollary G. If a weakly hyperbolic group G is lim-free, its reduced group C ∗ -algebra Cλ∗ (G)
is selfless.

   Corollary G gives us the first examples of finitely presented simple groups with selfless
reduced C ∗ -algebras.

1.3    Rigidity of 3-transitive actions

Recall that a G-action on some set Ω is k-transitive for some k ∈ N if for any two k-tuples
of distinct points (ω1 , . . . , ωk ) ∈ Ωk and (v1 , . . . , vk ) ∈ Ωk , there exists g ∈ G such that
gωi = vi for all i = 1, . . . , k. As discussed earlier, Theorem B gives a rigidity phenomenon
for general type actions of lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups. If a group is faithful weakly
hyperbolic but not lim-free, we obtain a rigidity phenomenon for all 3-transitive actions on
any set, as our second main theorem shows.
Definition 1.4. Two actions of a group G on sets Ω and Ω′ are conjugate if there exists a
bijective G-equivariant map Ω → Ω′ .
Theorem I. Suppose that the action of a group G on a hyperbolic space S is of general type
and that the induced action of G on ΛS (G) is faithful and not topologically free. Assume
that G acts faithfully and 3-transitively on a set Ω. Then there exists a G-orbit O ⊂ ΛS (G)
such that the actions of G on Ω and on O are conjugate.

    This theorem is the analogue of [BM, Theorem 1.4] for group actions on simplicial trees.
We generalize their arguments to groups acting on arbitrary hyperbolic spaces and obtain
their result as a corollary (see Corollary 3.28).
    The transitivity degree of a group G is the maximum k such that G admits a k-transitive
faithful action on some set. Among infinite groups, examples of groups with transitivity
degree 1, 2, 3, and ∞ are known. Whether there exists an infinite group with transitivity
degree 4 is an open question. The next corollary shows that if such an example exists, it
cannot be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free.
Corollary J. Let G be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free. Then the
transitivity degree of G is at most 3.

     Recall that an action of a group G on a topological space X is topologically transitive
if, for every pair of nonempty open subsets U and V of X, there is an element g ∈ G such
that g(U ) ∩ V ̸= ∅. It is topologically k-transitive for k ∈ N if the induced action of G on
the k-fold Cartesian product X k is topologically transitive.
    Since Theorem I tells us that the limit set “sees” all 3-transitive actions of a faithful
weakly hyperbolic group G that is not lim-free, it suffices to examine just the G-action on
the limit set to determine whether the transitivity degree of G is 3 or less, as the following
corollary shows.
Corollary K. Let G be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free. If the
transitivity degree of G is 3, then for any general type action of G on any hyperbolic space
S, if the induced action on ΛS (G) is faithful, it is topologically 3-transitive.

and by dS the metric on S. All group actions on S are by isometries. For a group G acting

2.2   Minimal actions on trees

We will use the facts established in this subsection to obtain the results about groups acting
on simplicial trees from [BM] and [FMMS] as corollaries of Theorem B and Theorem I.



                                                  15
Proposition 2.20. [Bas, Proposition 7.5] Suppose that a group G acts on a simplicial
tree T by isometries. If G contains a loxodromic element, then there is a unique minimal
nonempty G-invariant subtree L ⊆ T , which is the union of the axes of all loxodromic
elements.

   The next lemma shows that if a group G, acting on a tree T , contains loxodromic
elements, then the minimality of the action is almost equivalent to ΛT (G) = ∂T , in the
sense that every nontrivial invariant subtree is “large”.
Lemma 2.21. Suppose that the group G acts on a simplicial tree T by isometries, and
LT (G) ̸= ∅. If the action of G on T is minimal, then ΛT (G) = ∂T . Moreover, without the
minimality assumption, the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. ΛT (G) = ∂T ;
  2. For every G-invariant subtree S ⊆ T , we have ∂T = ∂S.

Proof. By Proposition 2.20, the union of the axes of all loxodromic elements L ⊆ T is the
unique nonempty G-invariant subtree. Since the action of G on T is minimal, L = T . By
the construction of L, the set of fixed points of loxodromic elements of G is dense in ∂L.
Therefore, ΛL (G) = ∂L = ∂T .
    Now, assume that ΛT (G) = ∂T . Let S ⊆ T be a G-invariant subtree. Note that
ΛT (G) ⊆ ∂S since ΛT (G) does not depend on a chosen point t ∈ T and we can choose
t ∈ S. Then we have ∂T ⊆ ∂S, so ∂T = ∂S. Conversely, assume that for every G-invariant
subtree S ⊆ T , we have ∂S = ∂T . Let L be the union of the axes of all loxodromic
elements. By Proposition 2.20, L is a G-invariant subtree of T , and ΛL (G) = ∂L by the
construction of L. Since the limit set does not depend on a chosen point t ∈ T , we have
ΛT (G) = ΛL (G) = ∂L = ∂T .

Lemma 2.26. Let H be a non-trivial normal subgroup of a faithful weakly hyperbolic group

Lemma 3.9. If an action of a group G on a dense subset O of a Hausdorff topological

Proof. Let U1 , . . . , Uk and V1 , . . . , Vk be nonempty open subsets of O. Being a dense sub-

Lemma 3.27. Assume that G acts faithfully and 3-transitively on a set Ω. Then for any
ω ∈ Ω, the action of a subgroup Gω on S is quasi-parabolic.

Proof. Fix any ω ∈ Ω. By Proposition 3.16, the action of Gω on S is non-elementary.
Therefore, it suffices to prove that it is not of general type. For the sake of contradiction,
assume that the action of Gω on S is of general type. The action of G on Ω is 3-transitive.
Therefore, the action of Gω on Ω \ {ω} is 2-transitive. Let ω1 ∈ Ω \ {ω} and ∆ = (ω1 , ω).
We apply Proposition 3.16 to G∆ ≤ Gω and obtain that G∆ is itself non-elementary. By
Proposition 3.19, the action of G∆ on S cannot be of general type, and by Proposition 3.20,
it cannot be quasi-parabolic. This leads to a contradiction.

   Now we are ready to prove Theorem I.

Proof of Theorem I. By Lemma 3.27, the action of Gω on S is quasi-parabolic for any
ω ∈ Ω. Let q(ω) ∈ ΛS (Gω ) be the unique fixed point of Gω . Consider the map q : Ω → ∂S,
given by ω 7→ q(ω). This map is G-equivariant. For the sake of contradiction, assume that
q is not injective, i.e., there exists ω1 , ω2 ∈ Ω, such that ω1 ̸= ω2 , but q(ω1 ) = q(ω2 ). The
action of G is 2-transitive on Ω, so for any distinct ω1′ , ω2′ ∈ Ω there exists g ∈ G such
that gω1 = ω1′ and gω2 = ω2′ . Therefore, Gω1 = g −1 Gω1′ g and Gω2 = g −1 Gω2′ g. Thus,
q(ω1′ ) = gq(ω1 ) = gq(ω2 ) = q(ω2′ ). Then the map q is constant, and it follows that G
fixes a point in ΛS (G), which contradicts the assumption that the action of G on S is of
general type. Therefore, q is injective, and the G-action on Ω is conjugate to the action on
O = q(Ω).

3.4   Examples and necessity of assumptions

Example 3.29. Verbal products. This example provides a family of non-faithful weakly
hyperbolic groups that satisfy a mixed identity.
    Let V = {fν (x1 . . . xnν ) : ν ∈ I} be a subset of words in an infinitely generated free
group. A verbal V -subgroup V (G) of a group G is a subgroup generated by all possible
values of all words of V , when x1 , x2 , . . . run through the entire group G independently of
each other. A verbal V -subgroup V (G) is normal Q      in G. Let Gi , i ∈ I be some collection of
groups and let F = ∗i∈I Gi . Let π : F −→ i∈I Gi be the natural epimorphism. Then the
Cartesian subgroup C < F is the kernel of π. Let V (F ) be a verbal V -subgroup of F . Then
the verbal product ∗V Gi of groups Gi with respect to V is F/(V (F ) ∩ C).

Lemma 3.30. Let A and B be nontrivial groups, and let V be a nonempty subset of words
in a free group F (x1 , · · · , xk ). Then the group G = A ∗V B satisfies a mixed identity.



                                               38
Proof. Let v = v(x1 , x2 , x3 , · · · , xk ) be a nonempty word in V , and let a ∈ A and b ∈ B be
non-trivial elements of G. Consider the element

   w(a, b, x1 , x2 , x3 , · · · , xk ) = v([ax1 , b], [ax2 , b], [ax3 , b], · · · , [axk , b]) ∈ G ∗ F (x1 , · · · , xk ).

For any i ∈ {1, . . . , k} and values of xi in G, elements [axi , b] are in the Cartesian
subgroup of A ∗ B, thus v([ax1 , b], [ax2 , b], [ax3 , b], · · · , [axk , b]) ∈ V (A ∗ B). Therefore,
w(a, b, g1 , g2 , g3 , · · · , gk ) = 1 in G for any values g1 , · · · , gk ∈ G. Since a, b are non-trivial in
G, w(a, b, x1 , x2 , x3 , · · · , xk ) is non-trivial in G ∗ F (x1 , · · · , xk ).

Proposition 3.31. Let A, B be nontrivial groups, and let at least one of A or B be weakly
hyperbolic. Let V be a nonempty subset of words in a free group F (x1 , · · · , xk ). Then
G = A ∗V B is a weakly hyperbolic group that is not faithful weakly hyperbolic.

Proof. Without loss of generality, assume that A is weakly hyperbolic. There exists a
surjective homomorphism of G onto A, thus G is also weakly hyperbolic. Let φ(C) be the
image of the Cartesian subgroup C < A ∗ B under the homomorphism φ : A ∗ B → G.
  If φ(C) = 1, then G is a direct product of two non-trivial groups, so it is not MIF and
Mon(G) = 1. Thus, by Proposition D, G cannot be faithful weakly hyperbolic.
    Assume now that φ(C) ̸= 1. Since φ is surjective, φ(C) is normal in G. Therefore, by
Lemma 2.10, for any general type action of G on any hyperbolic space S, the subgroup φ(C)
is either elliptic or of general type. Since V (φ(C)) = {1}, φ(C) satisfies some non-trivial
identity v = 1, v ∈ V and cannot contain a non-abelian free subgroup. So, φ(C) is elliptic,
and since it is normal in G, φ(C) < ES (G) by the maximality of ES (G). Therefore, ES (G)
is non-trivial, and G is not a faithful weakly hyperbolic group.

Example 3.32. Non-lim-free HNN extensions and amalgams. The following propo-
sition provides restrictions on associate subgroups of HNN-extensions and amalgamated
products when the action on the boundary of the Bass-Serre tree is not topologically free.
Definition 3.33. Let H be a group and let φ : C → φ(C) be an isomorphism between
subgroups of H. Then HNN-extension associated to (H, C, φ) is the group defined by the
following presentation

                          HNN(H, C, φ) := ⟨H, t | t−1 ct = φ(c) for all c ∈ C⟩,

where t is an extra generator, not belonging to H, called a stable letter.

    Recall that it is called ascending if one of the subgroups C, φ(C) is equal to H.
Definition 3.34. Let l1 : C → A and l2 : C → B be injective group homomorphisms. We
will denote by Cj the image of lj and by φ : C1 → C2 the isomorphism sending l1 (c) to
l2 (c) for all c ∈ C. The free product with amalgamation (or amalgam for short) associated
to (A, B, C, φ) is the group defined by the following presentation:

       A ∗C B := ⟨A, B | l1 (c) = l2 (c) for all c ∈ C⟩ = ⟨A, B | c = φ(c) for all c ∈ C1 ⟩ .

                                                            39
   Recall that such an amalgam is said to be non-trivial if A, B ̸= Cj for j = 1, 2, and
non-degenerate if moreover [A : C1 ] ≥ 3 or [B : C2 ] ≥ 3.
   Given an HNN-extension G = HNN(H, C, φ) or an amalgam G = A ∗C B, we can
construct a Bass-Serre tree T associated to G. The group G acts on T transitively on the
edges of T . Stabilizers of edges are conjugates of C or φ(C). The action of G on T is
always minimal. We refer the reader to [Ser] for more information. If HNN(H, C, φ) is
non-ascending or G = A ∗C B is non-degenerate, then the action of G on T is of general
type (see, for example, [FMMS, Sections 2.5, 2.6]).

Proposition 3.35. Let G = HNN(H, C, φ) be a non-ascending HNN-extension or a non-
degenerate amalgam G = A ∗C B. If the induced action of G on the boundary ∂T of the
Bass-Serre tree is faithful but not topologically free, then C is non-abelian.

Proof. By Lemma 2.35, the action of G on ∂T is micro-supported, and, by Lemma 2.31,
RistG (U ) is non-abelian for every nonempty open subset U ⊆ ∂T . Take an arbitrary open
U that is not dense in ∂T . Consider two adjacent vertices v1 and v2 in T , such that the
ends of infinite rays from these vertices are in ∂T \ U . Then RistG (U ) fixes both v1 and v2 ;
thus, stabilizes the edge e incident to v1 and v2 . Since stabilizers of edges are conjugate to
C, there exists g ∈ G such that RistG (U ) ≤ C g . Therefore, C is non-abelian.

boundary of its Bass-Serre tree is not topologically free if and only if |m| = |n|.

Example 3.38. Topologically free action on the boundary. This example shows why
in Theorem 3.10 we must consider topologically free actions on the limit set, not the whole
boundary as an equivalent condition. A similar example is given in [FMMS, Proposition
9.2], where they discuss the necessity of minimality of the action on the tree in their case,
which exactly gives faithfulness of the action on the limit set.
    Let G = F2 × Z, where F2 is a free group of rank 2. The action of F2 on its Cayley graph
T is of general type. Let E be a countable bundle of rays from a unique point o, enumerated
by integers. The group Z acts on E as follows: n · rm = rn+m for every n ∈ Z and every
ray rm ⊂ E. This action of Z on E is elliptic since it fixes a point o. Let S be a space,
constructed by gluing over each vertex of T a copy of E at its fixed point o. Then G acts on


                                                     40
S by isometries, where elements from F2 act on a copy of T and elements of Z act on copies
of E in S. This action is of general type, and the induced action on ∂S is topologically free.
However, it is not topologically free (and not even faithful) on ΛS (G) = ∂T , since elements
(1, n) ∈ F2 × Z, n ̸= 0 fix ∂T . Obviously, G satisfies a nontrivial mixed identity [f x , n] = 1,
where f ∈ F2 , x is a free variable, and n ∈ Z \ {0}.
Example 3.39. Monolithic lim-free groups. This example shows that the converse of
Proposition D is not true, i.e., there exist lim-free weakly hyperbolic (hence MIF) groups
with infinite simple monolith.
   Consider PSL2 (C), PSL2 (R), PSL2 (Q), or any PSL2 (F ), where F is a field of char = 0
with cardinality less or equal to continuum. By the proof of Corollary F, they are lim-free,
and it is well known that they are simple (see [Dix]), so coincide with their monolith.
Example 3.40. Non-faithful weakly hyperbolic MIF groups.

Proposition 3.41. Every countable group can be embedded into a finitely generated MIF
weakly hyperbolic group, which is not faithful.

Proof. Let K be an arbitrary countable group. By the Higman-Neumann-Neumann Em-
bedding Theorem [HNN], K can be embedded in a finitely generated group G. By the
construction in [HNN], G is a non-ascending HNN-extension; therefore, it is weakly hyper-
bolic. Then by [HL, Thm 1.1], there exists a finitely generated group H, such that:

   1. G ≤ H ≤ Sym(G × Z);

   2. There is a short exact sequence:

                                   1 −→ N −→ H −→ (G × Z) −→ 1,

      where N is locally finite;

   3. H is MIF.

The group H maps surjectively on G × Z, and G × Z is weakly hyperbolic, since G is weakly
hyperbolic. Therefore, H also admits a general type action on the same hyperbolic space
as G. Note that the subgroup N is non-trivial; otherwise H would be isomorphic to G × Z,
which is impossible since H is MIF and G × Z has a non-trivial center. Since N is locally
finite, it consists of torsion elements of H. Since N is normal in H, it cannot be a parabolic
subgroup with respect to any action on any hyperbolic space, so it is always elliptic, and,
by Proposition 2.24, it should be in an elliptic radical of H with respect to any general
type action. Therefore, any elliptic radical of H is non-trivial, and H is not faithful weakly
hyperbolic.

Example 3.42. Finitely presented simple MIF groups.
   Kac-Moody groups. We partially answer Question 3.6 from [BFFHZ].

                                               41
Proof of Proposition H. Let Γ(F ) be a finitely presented Kac-Moody group over a finite
field, constructed in [CR], such that its quotient by the finite center G(F ) = Γ(F )/Z(Γ(F ))
is an infinite finitely presented simple group. We want to show that G(F ) is lim-free weakly
hyperbolic. First, we will show that G(F ) is weakly hyperbolic.
    The group G(F ) is a nonuniform lattice in a product of two buildings. Since F is finite,
the associated building is locally finite; hence its Davis realization X is a proper CAT(0)
space. Since F is a field, by the proof of [CF, Corollary 1.3 (page 19, line 1 of the proof)],
G(F ) acts Weyl-transitively on each of the two buildings. By [CF, Proposition 5.3], the
action of G(F ) on one of these buildings X contains two rank-one elements g1 and g2 with
disjoint limit points in the boundary of X.
    By [PSZ, Theorem B], there exists δ such the curtain model XD associated to X is δ-
hyperbolic and Isom(X) ≤ Isom(XD ). By [PSZ, Theorem C], two rank-one elements g1 and
g2 of the action of G(F ) on X become loxodromic elements of the action of G(F ) on XD .
Finally, by [PSZ, Theorem L], the boundary ∂XD embeds as an Isom-invariant subset of ∂X;
thus, the four limit points of rank-one elements g1 and g2 in ∂X must also be four distinct

injective hull, such that the canonical embedding XD → E(XD ) is an Isom(XD )-equivariant
quasi-isometry. In particular, it induces an Isom(X)-equivariant homeomorphism between
the Gromov boundaries of XD and E(XD ). Thus, g1 and g2 act loxodromically on E(XD )

yields a contradiction with the fact that the asymptotic dimension of G(F ) is bounded
from above by the asymptotic dimension of the product of two buildings it acts on [SWZ,
last paragraph], which is finite by [DS]. Therefore, the action of G(F ) on ΛE(XD ) (G(F )) is

    Burger-Mozes group. In [BM97], the finitely presented simple Burger-Mozes group G
was constructed. It acts by isometries on a locally finite tree T such that the induced action
