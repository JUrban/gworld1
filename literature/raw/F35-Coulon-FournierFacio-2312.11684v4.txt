                                                Infinite simple characteristic quotients
arXiv:2312.11684v4 [math.GR] 13 Sep 2026




                                                     Rémi Coulon and Francesco Fournier-Facio

                                                                            Abstract
                                                    We construct continuum many infinite, simple, characteristic
                                                quotients of non-abelian free groups, answering a 1978 question of
                                                James Wiegold. The method is very flexible, allowing to impose
                                                certain properties on the quotients, to generalize the construction
                                                to large classes of groups with hyperbolic features, and to produce
                                                quasi-isometrically diverse examples.


                                           1    Introduction
                                           A subgroup of a group Γ is called characteristic if it is invariant under
                                           all automorphisms of Γ, and by extension the corresponding quotients
                                           are called characteristic. Wiegold asked if non-abelian free groups admit
                                           infinite simple characteristic quotients [KM, Question 6.45]. The main
                                           goal of this paper is to prove the following theorem, answering positively
                                           Wiegold’s question.

                                           Theorem 1.1 (Corollary 4.2). For all n ⩾ 2, the free group of rank n
                                           admits continuum many, pairwise non-isomorphic, 2-generated, infinite,
                                           simple, characteristic quotients.

                                               We will state stronger versions later in the introduction (see Theorems
                                           1.5 and 1.6).
                                               Wiegold’s question on the (non-)existence of such a group falls in the
                                           context of growth sequences of simple groups. For a finitely generated
                                           group Γ, we denote by d(Γ) its rank, that is the smallest n such that Γ
                                           is a quotient of the free group of rank n, denoted by Fn . The growth
                                           sequence of Γ is the sequence {d(Γp )}p⩾1 . Growth sequences of finite
                                           groups were studied by Wiegold and co-authors, who gave very precise
                                           asymptotic formulas: see [Wie74, EW95] and the references therein.

                                                                                1
    The case of infinite groups is more mysterious, and was also studied
by Wiegold and several other authors: see [Obr93, Erf95] and the ref-
erences therein, as well as [Wis02, Bri22] for approaches of a different
flavor. In particular, Wiegold and Wilson proved that if Γ is a finitely
generated infinite simple group, then d(Γp ) ⩽ d(Γ) + 1 for all p ⩾ 1
[WW78]. However, there seems to be no example of a finitely generated
infinite simple group whose growth sequence is not constant. The ques-
tion on the existence of such a group, and more precisely of a finitely
generated infinite simple group such that d(Γ2 ) = d(Γ) + 1, has been
called “irreducibly difficult” [WW78, Obr93].
   This last question admits a reformulation purely at the level of free
groups. For a group Γ of rank at most n, let Nn (Γ) := {N ◁ Fn : Fn /N ∼
                                                                       =
Γ} be the set of normal subgroups of Fn whose quotient is isomorphic
to Γ. An observation of Wiegold [Wie88] shows that if Γ is a finitely
generated infinite simple group of rank n, then d(Γ2 ) = n + 1 if and
only if #Nn (Γ) = 1. This naturally led Wiegold to ask for two weaker
examples:
       • [KM, Question 16.100] If n ⩾ 2, does there exist an infinite simple
         group Γ of rank at most n such that the action of Aut(Fn ) on Nn (Γ)
         is transitive?1

       • [KM, Question 6.45] If n ⩾ 2, does there exist an infinite simple
         group Γ of rank at most n such that the action of Aut(Fn ) on Nn (Γ)
         has a fixed point?
Theorem 1.1 answers the latter question in the affirmative for all n ⩾ 2.
    These questions also fall into the broader context of the study of the
dynamics of Aut(Fn ) on the space of marked groups Gn . This is a compact
space that parametrizes all groups of rank at most n, and is a central tool
to study geometric and model-theoretic properties of finitely generated
groups [Gri84, Cha00, CG05, MOW21, Osi21]. The dynamics of Aut(Fn )
on Gn is very rich, and can shed light on generic properties of finitely
generated groups [Osi25]. The above questions arise very naturally in
this context, and are instances of the following general problem: To which
extent do certain algebraic properties of the underlying isomorphism type
influence the orbit of Aut(Fn ) on a marked group?
Remark 1.2. Analogous conjectures on finite groups have also received
much attention:

   • [Pak01, Conjecture 2.5.4] If n ⩾ 3, and Γ is a finite simple group,
     then the action of Aut(Fn ) on Nn (Γ) is transitive.

   • [Lub11, p.25] If n ⩾ 3, and Γ is a finite simple group, then the

The first conjecture is commonly attributed to Wiegold, and the case
of n = 2 is known to fail [NN51, GS09]. The second conjecture is by
Lubotzky, and was recently shown to fail for n = 2 [CLT26, Han].
    Besides the ranks of products of infinite simple groups, the ranks of
infinite simple groups themselves are also not well understood. There is
only one known construction of a sequence of finitely generated simple
groups with unbounded rank, due to Osin and Thom [OT13]. Our con-
struction does not directly provide simple quotients with arbitrarily large
rank. On the contrary, as we have stated, one can easily ensure that they
are 2-generated. In particular, this provides a negative answer to [KM,
Question 17.62], which asks whether every characteristic quotient of Fn
has rank n.
    Our construction of the quotients in Theorem 1.1 boils down to the
following general result:
Theorem 1.3 (Corollary 3.6). Let G be an acylindrically hyperbolic
group, and let H < G be finitely generated, infinite and normal. Then
there exists a subgroup N < H, normal in G, such that H/N is 2-
generated, infinite, and simple.
   As a corollary we obtain:
Corollary 1.4 (Corollary 3.7). Let Γ be a finitely generated group such
that [Γ : Z(Γ)] = ∞ and Aut(Γ) is acylindrically hyperbolic. Then Γ
admits an infinite, 2-generated, simple, characteristic quotient.
   The existence of infinite simple characteristic quotients of Fn (i.e.,
Wiegold’s question [KM, Question 6.45]) then follows, by the acylindrical
hyperbolicity of Aut(Fn ), due to Genevois and Horbez [GH21]. In fact,
acylindrical hyperbolicity is known in much greater generality, due to
several authors [Gen24, GM19, Gen19, GH21, EH, JMS], which allows
us to obtain infinite simple characteristic quotients in large classes of
negatively curved groups; see Example 3.8 for a comprehensive list.
   Theorem 1.3 is proven via a small cancellation construction over acyl-
indrically hyperbolic groups. This construction is quite flexible, and we
obtain additional control over the infinite simple characteristic quotients.

                                     3
In particular, we can map finite sets injectively, preserve the torsion, and
embed outer automorphism groups. Moreover, we can embed any count-
able subgroup in an infinite simple characteristic quotient. This leads to
the following stronger and more general version of Theorem 1.1:
Theorem 1.5 (Theorem 4.1). Let Γ be a torsion-free, non-elementary
hyperbolic group. Then, for every countable group L, there exists a char-
acteristic subgroup N < Γ such that Γ/N is simple, 2-generated, contains
L as a subgroup, and contains an element of order n ∈ N if and only if
L does.
    It is well-known that a group Γ as in the statement is SQ-universal,
that is, every countable group embeds in a quotient of Γ [Ol’95, Del96].
Theorem 1.5 shows that SQ-universality can be achieved just using sim-
ple characteristic quotients. By choosing L in a family of pairwise non-
isomorphic finitely generated groups, we deduce in particular that Γ
admits continuum many pairwise non-isomorphic simple characteristic
quotients, and moreover the torsion can be prescribed (Corollary 4.2).
    We also strengthen Theorem 1.1 in another direction, by producing
different examples up to quasi-isometry:
Theorem 1.6 (Theorem 5.1). There exist continuum many, pair-
wise non-quasi-isometric, infinite, 2-generated, simple, characteristic
quotients of Fn .
    Theorem 5.1 will be proven via a criterion for quasi-isometric diver-
sity in the space of marked groups [MOW21]. In order to apply it, we
will need to slightly modify our construction to produce infinite sim-
ple characteristic quotients of Fn as marked limits of finitely presented
acylindrically hyperbolic characteristic quotients of Fn .
    We end the paper with two questions on possible extensions and vari-
ations of our results.

2     Small cancellation theory
A method to construct simple quotients of groups goes as follows. Start
with a finitely generated “negatively curved” group Γ, and for an element
1 ̸= g ∈ Γ add relations that ensure that each generator of Γ belongs to
the normal closure of g. If this is done in a way that the quotient is
still “negatively curved”, then the process can be iterated, and the direct
limit will be normally generated by each of its non-identity elements,
thus simple. Versions of this construction can be found e.g. in [Ol’79].
     What makes this process rigorous is the theory of small cancellation.
In our setting, in order to obtain characteristic quotients of Fn , we will
perform a similar construction at the level of the automorphism group
Aut(Fn ). Therefore the right notion of “negative curvature” for us is
acylindrical hyperbolicity.

2.1    Acylindrically hyperbolic groups
Acylindricity appears in the work of Bowditch [Bow08] as a way to ex-
tend the techniques of hyperbolic geometry beyond the scope of proper
and co-compact actions. The class of all groups with a non-elementary,
acylindrical action on a hyperbolic space was later named acylindrically
hyperbolic groups by Osin [Osi16]. It encompasses several groups of in-
terest in geometric group theory, such as non-elementary hyperbolic and
relatively hyperbolic groups, mapping class groups and outer automor-
phism groups of free groups.
    As in [BH99, III.H, Definition 1.1], we say that a metric space is δ-
hyperbolic (in the sense of Gromov) if its geodesic triangles are δ-slim. A
metric space is hyperbolic, if it is δ-hyperbolic for some δ ∈ R+ . In this
article we always assume for simplicity that δ > 1.
Definition 2.1. Let G be a group acting by isometries on a metric
space X. We say that the action is acylindrical if for all ε > 0 there exist
R > 0 and n ∈ N such that the following holds. For all x, y ∈ X such
that d(x, y) ⩾ R, the set {g ∈ G : d(x, gx) ⩽ ε, d(y, gy) ⩽ ε} contains at
most n elements.
    Recall that an action of a group G on a hyperbolic space X is non-
elementary if G has more than two accumulation points in the boundary
∂X. A group G is acylindrically hyperbolic if it admits a non-elementary,
acylindrical action on a hyperbolic space X. Note that the action of a
subgroup of G on X is acylindrical, and therefore every subgroup of G
acting non-elementarily is also acylindrically hyperbolic.

                                     5
Lemma 2.2 ([Osi16, Lemma 7.2]). Let G be a group with a non-
elementary acylindrical action on a hyperbolic space X. If H < G
is an infinite normal subgroup, then the action of H on X is also
non-elementary. Therefore, H is acylindrically hyperbolic.

    Given a (possibly infinite) generating set A ⊂ G, we denote by
Γ(G, A) the Cayley graph of G with respect to A. For every R ∈ R+ , we
write BA (R) for the set of all the elements of G corresponding to vertices
of Γ(G, A) in the ball of radius R centered at the identity. The space X
appearing in the definition of acylindrically hyperbolic group can always
be chosen to be a Cayley graph.

Theorem 2.3 ([Osi16, Theorem 1.2]). Let G be an acylindrically hyper-
bolic group. There exists a (typically infinite) generating set A ⊂ G such
that the Cayley graph Γ(G, A) is hyperbolic and the action of G on it is
non-elementary and acylindrical.

   Another important property of acylindrically hyperbolic groups is
that they have maximal finite normal subgroups.

Theorem 2.4 ([DGO17, Theorem 2.23], [Hul16, Lemma 5.10]). If G
is an acylindrically hyperbolic group, then G contains a unique maximal
finite normal subgroup K. Moreover, G/K is acylindrically hyperbolic,
and has no non-trivial finite normal subgroup.

    In the remainder of the article we denote by K(G) the unique maximal
finite normal subgroup of an acylindrically hyperbolic group G.

Definition 2.5. Let G be a group with a non-elementary acylindrical
action on a hyperbolic space X. Let H be a subgroup of G.

   • H is elliptic (with respect to this action) if some, hence any, orbit
     of H is bounded.

   • H is suitable (with respect to this action) if the action of H on X
     is non-elementary (i.e., neither elliptic nor virtually cyclic) and H
     does not normalize a non-trivial finite subgroup of G.

      Note that a suitable subgroup can only exist if K(G) = {1}. A good
source of suitable subgroups is normal subgroups [CIOS25, Lemma 3.23].
Here we will need something slightly stronger: recall that a subgroup
H < G is subnormal if there exists a chain of subgroups H = H0 < H1 <
· · · < Hk = G such that Hi is normal in Hi+1 for 0 ⩽ i < k.


                                    6
Lemma 2.6. Let G be a group with a non-elementary acylindrical action
on a hyperbolic space X. Assume that K(G) = {1}. Then every non-
trivial subnormal subgroup of G is suitable with respect to this action.

Proof. Let H be a non-trivial subnormal subgroup of G, and let H =
H0 < · · · < Hk = G be a chain witnessing this. The proof is by induction
on the length k of this chain. The case k = 0 is trivial. Assume now that
k ⩾ 1 and the statement holds for k − 1. In particular, H1 is suitable,
hence non-elementary. It follows from Lemma 2.2 that the action of H0
on X is acylindrical and non-elementary. By [Hul16, Lemma 5.5], there
exists a unique maximal finite subgroup K ⊂ G that is normalized by
H0 . Let g ∈ H1 and h ∈ H0 . Since H0 is normal in H1 , the element
g −1 hg ∈ H0 normalizes K, or equivalently h normalizes gKg −1 . By
uniqueness we obtain gKg −1 = K. Consequently H1 normalizes K.
Since H1 is suitable, this forces K = {1}.

Lemma 2.7. Let G be a group with a hyperbolic Cayley graph X, such
that the action of G on X is non-elementary and acylindrical. Let H < G
be suitable with respect to this action. Then there exists a 2-generated
subgroup L < H that is also suitable with respect to this action.

Proof. Let g ∈ G be a loxodromic element. Then there exists a unique
maximal elementary subgroup containing g [DGO17, Lemma 6.5, Corol-
lary 6.6], which we denote by E(g). By [Hul16, Corollary 5.7], the suit-
able subgroup H contains two non-commensurable elements h1 , h2 such
that E(hi ) = ⟨hi ⟩ for i = 1, 2. In particular, h1 ∈
                                                    / E(h2 ), and h1 does
not normalize a non-trivial finite subgroup. Letting L := ⟨h1 , h2 ⟩ we
conclude.
   We need one more lemma that shows that elliptic subgroups are, in
a way, uniformly bounded.

Lemma 2.8. Let G be a group. Let A be a generating set of G such that
Γ(G, A) is δ-hyperbolic. For every elliptic subgroup E, there is g ∈ G
such that gEg −1 is contained in BA (10δ).

Proof. There is a vertex v ∈ Γ(G, A) such that the orbit Ev has diameter
at most 10δ. (Such statement appears in many places in the literature
with different values in place of 10δ. These differences depend mostly on
the definition of hyperbolic space used by the authors, see e.g. [BH99,
III.Γ, Lemma 3.3]. We chose on purpose a very generous formulation.
Recall that we assume that δ > 1, so that we always have a vertex is
our set of “almost fixed points”.) However the action of G on the vertex

set of Γ(G, A) is transitive, hence there is an element g ∈ G such that
gv = v0 where v0 is the vertex representing the identity of G. Then the
orbit (gEg −1 )v0 is contained in BA (10δ).

2.2    Small Cancellation Theorem
Small cancellation theory is a powerful and versatile tool to produce and
study groups with prescribed properties. It appears in many flavors in the
literature [LS01, Ol’91, Gro01, Osi10, Cou14, DGO17]. The general phi-
losophy is roughly the following: If a group G admits a “non-degenerate”
action on a negatively curved space X, then so does any quotient of G ob-
tained by adjoining relations which are “sufficiently independent”, hence
paving the way to iterated constructions. For this paper, we rely on the
work of Hull [Hul16].

Theorem 2.9 (Small Cancellation Theorem [Hul16, Theorem 7.1]). Let
G be a group and A ⊂ G a generating set of G. Assume that Γ(G, A)
is hyperbolic and the action of G on this space is acylindrical and non-
elementary. Let H < G be a finitely generated subgroup that is suitable
with respect to the action on Γ(G, A). Let B ⊂ G be a finite subset and
R ∈ R+ . Then there exists a quotient π : G → Ḡ with the following
properties.

  1. There is a subset Ā ⊂ Ḡ containing π(A) such that Γ(Ḡ, Ā) is
     hyperbolic and the action of Ḡ on this space is non-elementary and
     acylindrical.

  2. π is injective on BA (R).

  3. π(B) ⊂ π(H).

  4. π(H) is a 2-generated suitable subgroup of Ḡ for its action on
     Γ(Ḡ, Ā).

  5. Every element of Ḡ of order n ∈ N is the image of an element of
     G of order n.

  6. ker π is the normal closure of finitely many elements in B · H.

Proof. Without loss of generality we can assume that B contains a finite
generating set of H. Consider a 2-generated suitable subgroup L of
H as given by Lemma 2.7 and [Hul16, Theorem 7.1] with L (rather
than H). In particular, (1), (2), and (5) directly follows from [Hul16,
Theorem 7.1]. Hull’s statement also tells us that π(B) ⊂ π(L), hence

                                    8
π(B) ⊂ π(H), which proves (3). On the one hand L ⊂ H. On the other
hand we assumed that B contains a generating set of H, so that our
previous discussion yields π(H) ⊂ π(L). It follows that π(H) = π(L)
is 2-generated. According to [Hul16, Theorem 7.1] it is also a suitable
subgroup of Ḡ for its action on Γ(Ḡ, Ā), which completes the proof of
(4). The last item is a consequence of the construction; see the proof of
[Hul16, Theorem 7.1].

Remark 2.10. Theorem 2.9 was later refined in various directions, see
e.g. [MO19, Proposition 3.3] or [CIOS25, Theorem 3.27]. Using these
stronger small cancellation theorems would allow to improve Theorem
1.3 to encompass countable groups H that are not necessarily finitely
generated. However, we do not know of any interesting application of this
more general statement. In addition, the assumption of finite generation
allows for an easier small cancellation setup (which for instance does
not require to introduce hyperbolically embedded subgroups). The next
corollary is the only statement in the article, where assuming that H is
finitely generated cannot be avoided. Having finitely presented quotients
as below will play a crucial role in Section 5.

Corollary 2.11. There exists a finitely presented, 2-generated, acyl-
indrically hyperbolic quotient G0 of Aut(Fn ) such that the composition
Fn → Aut(Fn ) → G0 is surjective, and K(G0 ) = {1}.

Proof. The group Aut(Fn ) is acylindrically hyperbolic, with no finite
normal subgroups, see [GH21]. The subgroup Fn ∼     = Inn(Fn ) < Aut(Fn ) is
infinite and normal, thus suitable (Lemma 2.6). We fix a finite generating
set B of Aut(Fn ). Theorem 2.9 produces an acylindrically hyperbolic
quotient π : Aut(Fn ) → G0 such that π(Fn ) is 2-generated and contains
π(B) while K(G0 ) = {1}. This shows in particular that Fn surjects onto
G0 which is therefore 2-generated. Moreover, by Theorem 2.9 again, π
may be chosen to be the quotient of Aut(Fn ) by finitely many relations.
Since Aut(Fn ) is finitely presented [Nie24], so is G0 .


3    Simple characteristic quotients
The construction of characteristic quotients relies on the following simple
but key observation.

Lemma 3.1. Let Γ be a group. Let π : Aut(Γ) → Q be a homomor-
phism. Then the kernel of the composition π ◦ ad : Γ → Inn(Γ) → Q is
characteristic in Γ.

                                     9
Proof. Let g ∈ ker(π ◦ ad) and α ∈ Aut(Γ). Then adg ∈ ker(π) so
adα(g) = α adg α−1 ∈ ker(π) too. This shows that α(g) ∈ ker(π ◦ ad) and
concludes the proof.
    As a warm-up with provide first a short proof of the existence of infi-
nite simple characteristic quotients of free groups. We thank an anony-
mous referee for suggesting this argument.

Theorem 3.2. Let n ⩾ 2. There exists an infinite simple characteristic
quotient of Fn .

Proof. There exists an acylindrically hyperbolic quotient π : Aut(Fn ) →
G0 such that π is surjective when restricted to Fn (Corollary 2.11). Every
quotient of G0 is a characteristic quotient of Fn (Lemma 3.1). Therefore,
it suffices to show that G0 admits an infinite simple quotient.
    Let H be Higman’s group [Hig51]: this is a finitely generated group
with no non-trivial finite quotients, which is moreover acylindrically hy-
perbolic [MO15, Corollary 4.26]. By [Hul16, Corollary 1.6] there exists
an acylindrically hyperbolic group G1 that is a common quotient of G0
and H. Since G1 is finitely generated, it follows from Zorn’s lemma that
it contains a maximal proper normal subgroup N . Recall that G1 is a
quotient of the Higman group. Hence the index of N in G1 must be
infinite. Thus G2 = G1 /N is an infinite simple quotient of G0 .

Remark 3.3. The above proof goes through Zorn’s Lemma, so the group
it produces is not explicit, and it is harder to control its finer properties.
We now give an alternative argument relying on the small cancellation
theory techniques introduced in the previous section. It naturally pro-
vides us with additional control, which we will exploit here and in the
next sections.

Definition 3.4. For a group Γ we denote by τ (Γ) the set {n ∈ N :
Γ contains an element of order n}.

Theorem 3.5. Consider a short exact sequence

                          1 → H → G → Q → 1,

where H is infinite and finitely generated. Let A ⊂ G be a generating
set of G. Assume that Γ(G, A) is hyperbolic and the action of G on
this space is acylindrical and non-elementary. Suppose in addition that
K(G) = {1}. For every R ∈ R+ , there is a subgroup N < H, normal in
G, with the following properties.


                                     10
   • H/N is simple.

   • H/N is 2-generated but not finitely presentable.

   • The projection G → G/N is injective on BA (R).

   • τ (H/N ) = τ (H).

   • Q embeds in Out(H/N ).

Proof. By assumption Γ(G, A) is δ-hyperbolic, for some δ ∈ R+ . Without
loss of generality we can assume that R ⩾ 10δ. We fix a finite generating
set B of H and an enumeration {h1 , h2 , . . .} of H \ {1}. We are going to
build by induction a nested sequence of subgroups Nk ⊂ H, normal in
G, with the following properties. Let Hk := H/Nk and Gk := G/Nk .

  1. There is a generating set Ak of Gk containing the image of A such
     that Γ(Gk , Ak ) is hyperbolic and the action of Gk on this space is
     non-elementary, acylindrical. Moreover K(Gk ) = {1}.

  2. Hk is infinite. Moreover, for each i ∈ {1, . . . , k}, either hi ∈ Nk , or
     Hk is the normal closure in Hk of (the image of) hi . In addition,
     Hk is 2-generated, provided k ⩾ 1.

  3. The projection G → Gk is injective on BA (R).

  4. The following diagram commutes

                   1         H            G         Q         1
                                                     ∼
                                                     =

                   1         Hk           Gk        Q         1.

From now on, we make an abuse of notation and denote in the same way
an element g ∈ G and its images in the quotients of G.
    We start our induction with N0 = {1} for which the above assertions
hold. Assume that we have already built the first k + 1 subgroups N0 <
N1 < · · · < Nk , where k ⩾ 0. If hk+1 ∈ Nk , we set Nk+1 = Nk and move
on to the next step. Let us assume that this is not the case. We denote
by Ak the generating set of Gk given by the induction hypothesis (1).
Recall that Ak contains the image of A. Consequently the projection
G → Gk induces a 1-Lipschitz map Γ(G, A) → Γ(Gk , Ak ). In particular,
the image of BA (R) is contained in BAk (R). Denote by Lk the normal
closure of hk+1 in Hk . As Gk has no non-trivial finite normal subgroup, Lk

                                     11
is a suitable of Gk with respect to its action on Γ(Gk , Ak ) (Lemma 2.6).
Denote by Bk the image of B in Hk . Note that Bk is a finite generating
set of Hk . According to the Small Cancellation Theorem (Theorem 2.9)
there is a quotient Gk+1 of Gk such that the projection π : Gk → Gk+1
has the following properties.

   • There is a subset Ak+1 ⊂ Gk+1 containing the image of Ak (hence
     the image of A) such that Γ(Gk+1 , Ak+1 ) is hyperbolic and the
     action of Gk+1 on this space is non-elementary and acylindrical.

   • π is injective on BAk (R).

   • π(Bk ) ⊂ π(Lk ), so that the image Hk+1 of Hk in Gk+1 is normally
     generated (as a subgroup of Hk+1 ) by hk+1 .

   • π(Lk ) = π(Hk ) is a 2-generated, suitable subgroup of Gk+1 .

   • Every element in Gk+1 of order n is the image of an element in Gk
     with order n.

   • ker π is contained in Hk .

We now write Nk+1 for the kernel of the quotient G → Gk+1 , and remark
that Nk < Nk+1 < H; in fact Nk+1 is also the kernel of the restriction
H → Hk+1 .
    Since π(Lk ) is a suitable subgroup of Gk+1 , it follows that Gk+1 has no
non-trivial finite normal subgroup, hence (1) holds. Moreover the normal
subgroup Hk+1 is non-trivial, therefore infinite. By construction Hk+1 is
normally generated by hk+1 . However Hk was normally generated by
each element in {h1 , . . . , hk } \ Nk , hence (2) holds. Points (3) and (4)
directly follow from the construction and the previous discussion. This
completes the induction step.
    We now define N to be the increasing union of all the subgroups Nk
and let H∞ := H/N and G∞ := G/N . By construction, every non-trivial
element h ∈ H is either in N or normally generates H∞ . Hence H∞ is
simple. As a quotient of every Hk , it is 2-generated. Suppose by contra-
diction that H∞ is finitely presentable. Then N is the normal closure of
a finite subset of H. Since N is the directed union of the Nk , we deduce
that N = Nk , for k large enough. Therefore H∞ = Hk . But this is a
contradiction, because H∞ is simple, while Hk is acylindrically hyper-
bolic, being an infinite normal subgroup of the acylindrically hyperbolic
group Gk (Lemma 2.2). It directly follows from the construction that
the projection G → G∞ is injective on BA (R).

    An induction argument shows that every element of order n in H∞
has a pre-image g of order n in G. However the kernel of the projec-
tion G → G∞ is contained in H, hence g belongs to H. Consequently
τ (H∞ ) ⊂ τ (H). Let us prove the converse inclusion. Let h ∈ H be a
finite order element. In particular, ⟨h⟩ is elliptic, thus it is conjugate to
a finite subgroup contained in BA (R), on which the projection G → G∞
is injective (Lemma 2.8). Hence ⟨h⟩ embeds in G∞ , and a posteriori in
H∞ . This shows that τ (H) ⊂ τ (H∞ ).
    Finally, note that the following diagram commutes
                1         H           G          Q         1
                                                  ∼
                                                  =

                1        H∞          G∞          Q         1
and its rows are short exact sequences. The action by conjugation of G∞
on H∞ defines a morphism G∞ → Aut(H∞ ), so that the image of any
element of H∞ is inner. Hence it induces a morphism χ : Q → Out(H∞ ).
Let us prove that χ is injective. Consider an element q ∈ Q and g ∈ G
a pre-image of q. Assume that χ(q) = 1. In particular, there is u ∈ H
such that gu centralizes H∞ . Since H is finitely generated, this property
is ensured by finitely many relations in N , therefore there exists k ∈ N
such that gu centralizes Hk . Since Hk is non-elementary (Lemma 2.2),
⟨gu⟩ must be finite [BF02, Proposition 6]. But Hk is a suitable subgroup
of Gk (Lemma 2.6) so it cannot normalize a non-trivial finite subgroups.
It follows that gu = 1 in Gk ; in particular g ∈ Hk and so q = 1.

Corollary 3.6. Let G be an acylindrically hyperbolic group. Let H < G
be finitely generated, infinite and normal. Then there exists a subgroup
N < H, normal in G, such that H/N is 2-generated, infinite, and simple.

Proof. Let G0 := G/K(G), which is an acylindrically hyperbolic group
with no non-trivial finite normal subgroup (Theorem 2.4). Let H0 be the
image of H in G0 . Theorem 3.5 gives a subgroup N0 < H0 , normal in
G0 , such that H0 /N0 is 2-generated, infinite and simple. Let N ′ be the
pre-image of N0 in G, and let N := H ∩ N ′ . Then N is a subgroup of H,
normal in G, and H/N ∼   = H0 /N0 is infinite and simple.
   Combining with Lemma 3.1, we obtain Corollary 1.4:

Corollary 3.7. Let Γ be a finitely generated group such that [Γ : Z(Γ)] =
∞ and Aut(Γ) is acylindrically hyperbolic. Then Γ admits a character-

and simple.

Proof. The hypothesis implies that Inn(Γ) is an infinite normal subgroup
of the acylindrically hyperbolic group Aut(Γ). Thus the result is a com-
bination of Corollary 3.6 and Lemma 3.1.

Example 3.8. Corollary 3.7 applies when Γ is a finitely generated group
such that one of the following holds.

        • Γ is non-elementary hyperbolic [Gen19, GH21].

        • Γ is non-elementary hyperbolic relative to a collection of finitely
          generated subgroups, none of which is relatively hyperbolic [GH21,
          Theorem 1.3].

        • Γ has infinitely many ends [GH21, Theorem 1.1].

        • Γ is a graph product of finitely generated groups over a finite graph
          that is not a join and not reduced to a single vertex [EH, Theorem
          A.27] (see also [GM19, Theorem E] and [Gen24, Theorem 1.5]).

        • Γ is a torsion-free Artin group, whose underlying graph is connected
          and has a separating vertex that is not in the center of Γ [JMS,
          Theorem E].

4         SQ-universality
Corollary 3.7 proves the existence of a simple characteristic quotient of

by embedding arbitrary countable groups. We get the following more
precise and more general version of Theorem 1.5.

Theorem 4.1. Let Γ be a torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic group,
and let C be a finite subset of Γ. Let L be a countable group. Then Γ
admits a characteristic subgroup N with the following properties.
   • Γ/N is simple.
   • Γ/N is 2-generated but not finitely presentable.
   • The quotient Γ → Γ/N is injective on C.

   As a corollary, we obtain the following generalization of Theorem 1.1.
Corollary 4.2. Let Γ be a torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic group.
For every set of primes P, there exist continuum many, pairwise non-
isomorphic, infinite, 2-generated, simple characteristic quotients Γ/N of
Γ such that τ (Γ/N ) = P. In particular, Γ admits continuum many
torsion-free, characteristic, simple quotients.
Proof. Let E be a countable group such that τ (E) = P, for example
E := ∗p∈P Z/pZ. Let {Li }i∈I be a family of continuum many pairwise
non-isomorphic finitely generated torsion-free groups, such as Camm’s
simple amalgamated products [Cam53]. Then by Theorem 4.1, for each
i ∈ I there exists a characteristic subgroup Ni of Γ such that Γ/Ni is
2-generated, infinite and simple; moreover E ∗ Li embeds in Γ/Ni and
τ (Γ/N ) = τ (E ∗ Li ) = τ (E) = P.
    Now Γ/Ni is a countable group containing the finitely generated sub-
group Li . Since every countable group contains only countably many
finitely generated subgroups, it follows that the family Γ/Ni achieves
continuum many isomorphism types.
Remark 4.3. We could also more directly obtain continuum many
pairwise non-isomorphic groups by varying P among all infinite sets of
primes.

Proposition 4.4. Let G be a group and A ⊂ G a generating set of

suitable with respect to its action on Γ(G, A). Let L be a countable group
and R ∈ R+ . There is a quotient π : G → Ḡ such that if we denote
H̄ := π(H), and N := ker(π), then the following hold.

   • There is a subset Ā ⊂ Ḡ containing π(A) such that Γ(Ḡ, Ā) is

   • If in addition, H is normal in G, then N < H and τ (H̄) = τ (L) ∪

Proof. We start by noticing that, since every countable group embeds in
a finitely generated group preserving the values of torsion [LS01, Theo-

P . Since P is hyperbolic relative to {G, L}, it is known that Γ(P, A′ ) is
δ-hyperbolic (for some δ ∈ R+ ) and the action of P on this space is non-

H does not normalize any non-trivial finite subgroup of P . On the other
hand, the graph Γ(G, A) admits a G-equivariant isometric embedding in

therefore the subgroup H is suitable for the action of P on Γ(P, A′ ).

     n in P .

whether any of these would have interesting connections to independent
problems, the same way that Wiegold’s question [KM, Question 6.45] is
connected to the problem of growth sequences of simple groups.


5     Quasi-isometric diversity
Our goal in this section is to strengthen the uncountability result (Corol-
lary 4.2) to moreover distinguish groups up to quasi-isometry.

Theorem 5.1. There exist continuum many, pairwise non-quasi-
isometric, 2-generated, infinite, simple, characteristic quotients of Fn .

    We will prove Theorem 5.1 by applying a criterion of Minasyan–Osin–
Witzel [MOW21]. This requires a small amount on background on the
space of marked groups Gn (n ⩾ 2) [Gri84]. This is the set of normal
subgroups of Fn , seen as a subspace of 2Fn with the product topology;
in particular it is a compact metrizable space. We will often identify
a normal subgroup N < Fn with the corresponding marked quotient
Fn → Fn /N . We will use some standard terminology from descriptive
set theory [Kec95, I.8]: A space is perfect if it is closed and has no isolated
points; a countable intersection of open sets is called a Gδ -set. In our
settings, a set is comeager if contains a dense Gδ -set (this relies on the
Baire Category Theorem).

                                      18
    Following [MOW21], we say that a subspace of Gn is quasi-
isometrically diverse if every comeager subset of it has continuum
many quasi-isometry classes. The proof of Theorem 5.1 will use the
following criterion for quasi-isometric diversity:

Theorem 5.2 ([MOW21, Corollary 1.2]). Every non-empty perfect sub-
set of Gn containing a dense subset of finitely presented groups is quasi-
isometrically diverse.

Remark 5.3. The statement of [MOW21, Corollary 1.2] is about non-
empty perfect subsets of G, which is a directed union of the spaces Gn for
n ⩾ 2. Since Gn is compact, a perfect subset of Gn is also a perfect subset
of G, thus Theorem 5.2 is a direct consequence of [MOW21, Corollary
1.2].

    We will apply Theorem 5.2 as in the proof of [MOW21, Corollary
4.10]. Namely, we will exhibit a set H of finitely presented marked
groups, and a set S of simple characteristic marked groups, such that
S is comeager in the closure cl(H). Note that the groups that appear in
intermediate steps of our construction (Theorem 3.5) are not necessarily
finitely presented, unless G = H.
    To bypass this difficulty we use Corollary 2.11: There exists a 2-
generated, finitely presented, acylindrically hyperbolic quotient G0 of
Aut(Fn ) such that the composition Fn → Aut(Fn ) → G0 is surjective.
We fix the marked group G0 for the rest of this section. Let H ⊂ Gn
denote the set of marked groups that are finitely presented, acylindri-
cally hyperbolic, and factor through Fn → G0 . We further let S denote
the subset of cl(H) consisting of simple groups whose marking factors
through Fn → G0 . Since G0 is 2-generated, so is every element in S. We
make the following key observation:

Lemma 5.4. Every quotient of G0 is a characteristic quotient of Fn . In
particular, every marked group in S is a simple characteristic quotient of
Fn .

Proof. By the choice of G0 (Corollary 2.11), a quotient Fn → G0 → H
factors as Fn → Aut(Fn ) → G0 → H. The kernel of this composition is
characteristic, by Lemma 3.1.
   Finally, we need to establish a topological property of S.

Lemma 5.5. The subset S ⊂ cl(H) is comeager.



                                    19
Proof. The set of marked groups that factor through Fn → G0 is open,
because G0 is finitely presented. Moreover, simple groups form a Gδ -set
in Gn [MOW21, Corollary 4.5]. Therefore the set of simple quotients of
G0 is a Gδ -set, which implies that S is a Gδ -subset of cl(H).
    To show that S is dense in cl(H), it suffices to show that each H ∈ H
is an accumulation point of S. A neighbourhood basis of H := Fn /NH
consists of the sets

                 UW := {N ∈ Gn : N ∩ W = NH ∩ W };

where W ⊂ Fn is finite. By Theorem 3.5 applied with H = G (and Q =
1), there exists an infinite simple quotient S of H such that the finite set
W NH ⊂ Fn /NH = H maps injectively into S. Thus S ∈ UW . Moreover,
the construction of Theorem 3.5 produces a sequence of quotients of H,
each of which is acylindrically hyperbolic, and obtained from the previous
one by an application of Theorem 2.9. Since H is finitely presented, this
shows that each of the groups in this sequence belong to H, and therefore
S ∈ cl(H), and so S ∈ S.
    We are ready to prove Theorem 5.1.
Proof of Theorem 5.1. Let G0 , H, S be as above. Recall that S con-
sists of simple characteristic quotients of Fn by Lemma 5.4, and that
S ⊂ cl(H) is comeager, in particular it is dense. By definition H is also
dense in cl(H), and moreover H ∩ S = ∅, because acylindrically hyper-
bolic groups cannot be simple. Since an isolated point must belong to
every dense subset, it follows that cl(H) has no isolated points, and there-
fore is perfect. Theorem 5.2 now applies to give quasi-isometric diversity
of cl(H). Since S is comeager in cl(H), it follows from the definition
that S has continuum many quasi-isometry classes. Finite groups form
a single quasi-isometry class, so we conclude that there are continuum
many quasi-isometry classes of 2-generated, infinite, simple, characteris-
tic quotients of Fn .


6     Outlook
We end with two questions on possible generalizations and applications
of our results. The first is about how far the list from Example 3.8 can
be extended:

Question 6.1. Let Γ be a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic
group. Does Γ admit an infinite simple characteristic quotient?

                                    20
   In [Gen19, Question 1.1], Genevois asked whether the automorphism
group of a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group is always
acylindrically hyperbolic. In light of Corollary 1.4, an affirmative answer
to Genevois’s Question implies an affirmative answer to Question 6.1.
    The second question asks for a middle ground between Lubotzky’s
Conjecture (Remark 1.2), which is about finite simple characteristic quo-
tients, and our construction, which produces non-finitely presentable sim-
ple characteristic quotients.

Question 6.2. Let n ⩾ 2. Does there exist a characteristic subgroup
N < Fn such that Fn /N is infinite, simple and finitely presentable?
