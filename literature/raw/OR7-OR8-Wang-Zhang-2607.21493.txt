                                               NOT FORCE HOPFICITY, RESIDUAL FINITENESS, OR
                                                             AUTOMATICITY

                                                   Abstract. Let G = F/ ⟨⟨r⟩⟩ be a one-relator group with the relator r ∈ [F, F ]
                                                   or r = [u, v] (u, v ∈ F ), where F is a finitely generated free group. Baumslag
                                                   asked whether G is Hopfian, residually finite or automatic. In the case of
                                                   r ∈ [F, F ], a negative answer to the residual finiteness and automaticity has
                                                   already been obtained by a result of Olshanskii. In this note, we construct a

                                                   both cases.




                                                                               1. Introduction
                                              One-relator groups have been one of central subjects in combinatorial and geo-
                                           metric group theory since the pioneering work of Magnus. His Freiheitssatz and
                                           solution of the word problem established the basic inductive methods of the theory
                                           [14, 15]. For one-relator groups with torsion, Newman [17] later proved his influential
                                           spelling theorem, which yields strong control over their normal forms and implies
                                           their hyperbolicity. Building on the development of geometric group theory, Wise
                                           [21] proved that one-relator groups with torsion are virtually special and hence
                                           residually finite and linear, resolving a longstanding conjecture of Baumslag. Further
                                           structural progress was made by Louder and Wilton, who proved that one-relator
                                           groups with torsion are coherent [11] and subsequently developed the theory of
                                           negative immersions to establish coherence and related subgroup properties for a
                                           broad class of torsion-free one-relator groups [12]. Most recently, Jaikin-Zapirain
                                           and Linton [10] proved that every one-relator group is coherent, thereby settling the
                                           general coherence conjecture. Their work also gives homological coherence results
                                           for group algebras and extends to wider classes of groups of cohomological dimen-
                                           sion two. Together, these results illustrate how classical combinatorial arguments,
                                           geometric methods, and modern homological techniques have progressively deepened
                                           the understanding of one-relator groups.
                                              In this note, we focus on one-relator groups whose relator is a commutator, and
                                           provide a solution to the following open questions of Baumslag.

Question 1.1. [3, Questions OR7, OR8] Let G = F/ ⟨⟨r⟩⟩ be a one-relator group,
where F is a finitely generated free group and r ∈ [F, F ] or r = [u, v] for some
u, v ∈ F .
     (a) Is G Hopfian?
    (b) Is G residually finite?
     (c) Is G automatic?
   For items (b) and (c) for the case of r ∈ [F, F ], Baumslag said that there exists
such one-relator groups that are neither residually finite nor automatic, which can
be obtained from a result of Olshanskii [18]. To our knowledge, there are no known
counterexamples or affirmative proofs for item (a) for the case of r ∈ [F, F ] or items
(a), (b) and (c) for the case of r = [u, v]. Thus, the purpose of this note is to provide
such counterexamples to these questions.
   Recall that a group is Hopfian if its every surjective endomorphism is injective,
and it is residually finite if the intersection of all its normal subgroups of finite index
is trivial. The following definition of automatic group is from [5, Definition 2.3.1].
Refer to [5, Chapter 2] for more details.
Definition 1.2 (Automatic group). Let G be a group. An automatic structure
on G consists of a set A of semigroup generators of G, a finite state automaton W
over A, and finite state automata Mx over (A, A), for x ∈ A ∪ {ϵ}, satisfying the
following conditions:
     (1) The map π : L(W ) → G is surjective.
     (2) For x ∈ A ∪ {ϵ}, we have (w1 , w2 ) ∈ L(Mx ) if and only if w1 x = w2 and
         both w1 and w2 are elements of L(W ).
The symbol ϵ represents the null-string and L(W ) is the language recognized by
W . We call W the word acceptor, Mϵ the equality recognizer, and each Mx , for
x ∈ A, a multiplier automaton for the automatic structure. An automatic structure
is sometimes called an automation. An automatic group is one that admits an
automatic structure.
Notations. Throughout this paper, we use the commutator convention:
                                   [x, y] := x−1 y −1 xy,
and denote the Baumslag-Solitar group as follows:
                   BS(p, q) := c, t t−1 cp t = cq ,         p, q ∈ Z\{0}.
    Our main result is as follows.
Theorem 1.3. For any integer m ≥ 1, let
                              Gm = a, t      [t, a[a, t]−m ] .
Then the following hold:
   (1) The group Gm contains BS(m, m + 1) as a retract.
   (2) The group G2 is not Hopfian, and hence it is not residually finite.
   (3) The group G1 is not automatic.
    As a direct corollary, we have:
Corollary 1.4. For a one-relator group F/ ⟨⟨r⟩⟩, neither of the assumptions
                             r = [u, v]     or      r ∈ [F, F ]
implies Hopficity, residual finiteness, or automaticity.

   In Sect. 2, we show that the one-relator group Gm each has a Baumslag-Solitar
subgroup as a retract. In Sect. 3, we prove the non-Hopficity of G2 by constructing
an epimorphism of G2 , which induces a non-monomorphic epimorphism on its
Baumslag-Solitar subgroup. Since every finitely generated residually finite group
is Hopfian (a well known result of Mal’cev [16]), we can deduce that G2 is not
residually finite from its non-Hopficity. In Sect. 4, we prove the non-automaticity of
G1 , by showing G1 does not satisfy the quadratic isoperimetric inequality. Finally in
Sect. 5, we complete the proof of our main results and pose some further questions.

Proposition 2.1. For every m ≥ 1, the subgroup ⟨c, t⟩ of Gm is naturally isomorphic

                   Bm := BS(m, m + 1) = c, t t−1 cm t = cm+1 .

                    ιm : Bm −→ Gm ,           ιm (c) = c,    ιm (t) = t.

Proof. Assume that w = 1 and choose a reduced van Kampen diagram for w over

The usual innermost-annulus reduction shows that a reduced diagram contains no

ℓ ∈ pZ, and in the second it forces ℓ ∈ qZ. Thus w contains a prohibited pinch, a
contradiction.                                                                       □

           3. Non-Hopficity and non-residual finiteness of G2
    Now for m = 2, the presentation (2.2) becomes
(3.1)                 G2 = a, c, t      t−1 at = ac,      t−1 c2 t = c3 .
Then we have:
Proposition 3.1. The group G2 is not Hopfian, and hence not residually finite.
Proof. Define a map on the generators of G2 by
                       Φ(a) = ac2 ,         Φ(c) = c2 ,          Φ(t) = t.
For the first relation in (3.1), we have
                               Φ(t−1 at) = t−1 (ac2 )t
                                            = (t−1 at)(t−1 c2 t)
                                            = (ac)c3
                                            = Φ(ac),
and for the second relation,
                   Φ(t−1 c2 t) = t−1 c4 t = (t−1 c2 t)2 = c6 = Φ(c3 ).
Therefore Φ induces an endomorphism of G2 . Note that the image ϕ(G2 ) contains
t, c2 , and ac2 . It therefore contains
a = (ac2 )c−2 = Φ(ac−1 ),      c3 = t−1 c2 t = Φ(t−1 ct),          c = c3 (c2 )−1 = Φ(t−1 ctc−1 ).
Thus Φ : G2 → G2 and Φ|B2 : B2 → B2 are both surjective, where
                    B2 := ⟨c, t | t−1 c2 t = c3 ⟩ ∼

by Proposition 2.1.
  We now consider
                       w := [t−1 ct, c] = t−1 c−1 tc−1 t−1 ctc ∈ B2 .

Note that the three possible intervening powers of c have exponents −1, −1, 1. None
gives a pinch of the form t−1 c2k t or tc3k t−1 . By Lemma 2.2, w ̸= 1 in B2 , and
therefore also in G2 . On the other hand,
                Φ(w) = [Φ(t−1 ct), Φ(c)] = [t−1 c2 t, c2 ] = [c3 , c2 ] = 1.
Therefore, Φ is surjective but not injective. It follows that G2 is not Hopfian and
hence not residually finite.                                                      □

generated by X. For a word w ∈ F (X) representing the identity in P, define

where ui ∈ F (X), ri ∈ R, and εi ∈ {±1}. Equivalently, AreaP (w) is the least
number of 2-cells in a van Kampen diagram for w over P. Furthermore, we have
the definition of Dehn function δ : N → N:
   δP (n) := max {AreaP (w) | |w| ≤ n, w ∈ F (X) represents the identity in P} ,
where |w| denotes the word length of w in F (X), i.e., the number of letters in the
freely reduced word representing w.
   We shall use the following standard consequence of automaticity, see [5, Theorem
2.3.12].
Theorem 4.1 ([5], Theorem 2.3.12). If a group H is automatic, then every finite
presentation P of H satisfies an inequality
                  AreaP (w) ≤ C|w|2 + C,              δP (n) ≤ Cn2 + C
for all null-homotopic words w and n ≥ 1, with a constant C depending only on P.
   The isoperimetric inequalities and the Dehn functions for Baumslag-Solitar groups
indeed have been extensive studied, for example, [5, 7, 9]. The following theorem is
due to S. M. Gersten [7, Theorem B].
Theorem 4.2 ([7], Theorem B). If X is the 2-complex canonically associated to the
Baumslag-Solitar presentation ⟨x, y | y −1 xk y = xℓ ⟩ where |k| ̸= |ℓ|, then the Dehn
function δX (n) grows faster than any polynomial function of n; that is, there are no
constants A, d > 0 with δX (n) ≤ And for all n ≥ 1.
   Note that the original statement of Theorem 4.2 is about a function λX (n), which
is not greater than the Dehn function δX (n). We also need the following result [2,
Corollary 2] due to Baumslag, Miller III and Short.
Theorem 4.3 ([2], Corollary 2). If the finitely presented group B with finite
presentation B is a retract of the finitely presented group A with finite presentation
A, then δB (n) ⪯ δA (n), that is, δB (n) ≤ CδA (Cn + C) + Cn + C for some constant
C.
   We now prove the non-automaticity of G1 . Note that G1 has a presentation
(4.1)                 P1 := a, c, t    t−1 at = ac,     t−1 ct = c2 .

Its Baumslag-Solitar group retract BS(1, 2) has the following presentation
                                    B1 := ⟨c, t | t−1 ct = c2 ⟩.
Hence, we can easily obtain the following proposition.
Proposition 4.4. The group G1 is not automatic.
Proof. Assume G1 is automatic. By Theorem 4.1, there exists a real number C > 0
such that δP1 (n) ≤ Cn2 + C for any n ≥ 1. By Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 4.3,
we have δB1 (n) ⪯ δP1 (n). Then, there exists another real number D > 0 such that
         δB1 (n) ≤ DδP1 (Dn + D) + Dn + D ≤ DC(Dn + D)2 + DC + Dn + D,
which contradicts Theorem 4.2. Therefore, G1 is not automatic.                                  □

           5. Proof of the main conclusions and further questions
Proof of Theorem 1.3. Proposition 2.1 proves the retract assertion. Proposi-
tions 3.1 and 4.4 prove, respectively, that G2 is not Hopfian and not residually finite,
and that G1 is not automatic.                                                        □
     Finally, we propose the following question based on Baumslag’s question.
Question 5.1. Let G = F/ ⟨⟨r⟩⟩ be a one-relator group with the relator r =
[u, v], (u, v ∈ F ) and without Baumslag-Solitar subgroups, where F is a finitely
generated free group.
     (1) Is G Hopfian?
     (2) Is G residually finite?
     (3) Is G automatic?
  Since all hyperbolic groups are Hopfian [20, 19, 6] and automatic [5], items (1)
and (3) of Question 5.1 naturally hold if the following conjecture holds.
Conjecture 5.2 ([8, 1], Gersten). Is every one-relator group without Baumslag-
Solitar subgroups hyperbolic?
