## Removing the remaining scalar exception by weight

Give E_j weight u+p*j. A finite word combination D is called homogeneous
of weight q when every word with a nonzero coefficient has total weight
q. This is a condition on its actual coefficients, not a label attached
to the vector space.

Let D have m E letters and V have m+1 letters. Assume D is nonzero,
homogeneous of weight q>u, and belongs to the concrete Lie subalgebra
generated by the E_j. If

    delta(V) = [E_0,D],

then the checked conclusion is

    R([E_0,V]) != 0.

Indeed, a zero diagonal image would give D=c E_0^m and V=0 by the
associative theorem. Nonzero D makes c nonzero. The Lie theorem forces
m=1, but then the nonzero word E_0 has weight u, contradicting q>u.
This explicitly closes the m=1 case retained in the preceding supplement.
The parameters p,u,q are natural numbers; the theorem only needs q>u,
so no extra positivity assumptions on p or u are silently inserted.

In Section 6 of [the general proof](general-proof.md), u=p+t and q>p+t.
Its opposite sign convention can be handled by replacing D with -D;
the weight, Lie-membership and nonzero conditions are preserved. The
formal theorem uses the displayed positive sign convention.

## The concrete block hypotheses

For fixed m, let R act on the `(m+2)`-letter word component by positional
encoding followed by the explicit diagonal substitution. Its target is
the rational polynomial ring in m variables. Write

    a = -(Z_0 + ... + Z_(m-1))/2.

The checked dictionary gives R(delta(H))=0 and

    R([E_n,F]) = a^n R([E_0,F]).

Fix a natural offset t and a nonzero rational b. Let F_j have m+1
letters and G_j have m+2 letters. The earlier compatibility equations
are actual word-vector equalities

    b [E_0,F_j] + sum_(k<j) c_jk [E_(n_jk),F_k] = delta(G_j)
                                                        for every j<t.

Here c_jk are rational scalars and n_jk are natural indices. Applying R
gives the triangular polynomial system. Strong induction proves
R([E_0,F_j])=0 for all j<t. Multiplication by a polynomial is used as a
Q-linear operator; it is not mistaken for multiplication by a rational
constant or replaced by a fraction-field calculation.

Every later column has the actual form

    B_s = delta(W_s) +
          sum_(t<i<=2t) d_si [E_(e_si),F_(2t-i)].

The endpoint i=2t is included. Since 2t-i<t throughout this interval,
R annihilates each term and every B_s. It also annihilates the full
derivative image. Therefore it annihilates the entire subspace

    S = im(delta on Words(m+2)) + span_Q{B_s}.

The index set of columns may be arbitrary; span means finite linear
combinations. No bound on m, word indices, degree, t, or column count is
imposed. All coefficients and columns are fixed across the parameter
values under comparison. The result does not allow them to vary with
the parameter being solved for.

## Separation and finite branching

Under the weighted Lie hypotheses above, set Q=b^2[E_0,V]. Then
R(Q)=b^2 R([E_0,V]) is nonzero, whereas R(S)=0. The checked theorem is

    Q is not in S.

This concerns the complete correction span and every later column
together. It is stronger than testing only the derivative image, or
testing each later column individually against Q.

For arbitrary fixed word vectors l,k, suppose three rational values
x,y,z satisfy

    x^2 Q + x l + k in S,
    y^2 Q + y l + k in S,
    z^2 Q + z l + k in S.

The formal corollary proves x=y or x=z or y=z. Hence there are at most
two distinct rational values, and consequently at most two integer
values. The nonzero lattice step b need not be one. The formalization
does not enumerate integer roots, solve integral congruences or implement
the recursion; those remain in the written algorithm.
