# N8(b): a candidate decision algorithm in every finite rank and class

29 September 2026. This is the consolidated general argument derived
during the experiment. It is a candidate mathematical proof, subject to
independent specialist review and a separate novelty assessment. Its
components and finite checks are distinguished in `general-audit.md`.
The complete arbitrary-rank algorithm is not implemented end to end.

**Proposed theorem.** Given nonnegative finite rank r, finite nilpotency
class c, and a word g in the standard free nilpotent group
N=F_r/gamma_(c+1)(F_r), there is an algorithm deciding whether

    [x,y]=g,                 [x,y]=x^(-1)y^(-1)xy,

has a solution in N. On a positive answer the algorithm constructs x,y.
In particular this answers the full quantifier in N8(b), rather than
only bounded classes or selected leading degrees. Part(a), for general
finitely generated class-two groups, has a separately credited prior
negative answer and is not a new result here.

All computations below are finite exact operations. No feasible running
time or complexity bound is asserted. Rational changes of Lie coordinates
are used to prove separation; actual solution lattices stay integral.

## 1. Conventions and imported structure

Let L_Z be the free integral graded Lie ring on the r generators,
L=L_Z tensor Q, and L_j its degree-j component. Hall bases, multiplication
and collection in N, and the truncated rational BCH logarithm are
effective. The integral Hall group coordinates parametrize elements
bijectively by integer tuples. The rational Malcev Lie algebra of N
is L truncated above c; its graded component is identified with L.

We use the following classical facts, with their exact roles stated.

* **Shirshov--Witt and the homogeneous free-basis lemma.** A graded
  subalgebra of L has a homogeneous free basis obtained by lifting a
  homogeneous basis of its abelianization. The lifts generate by
  induction on positive degree and are irredundant; the homogeneous
  Shirshov lemma makes them free. Any specified linearly independent
  classes can be included. This is applied to subalgebras of the
  untruncated L, before any finite truncation.
* **Inner solutions for free-basis coefficients.** If a,b belong to
  a free basis of a free Lie algebra and [a,V]+[U,b]=0, then U,V
  belong to Lie(a,b). The two-coefficient theorem is due to
  Remeslennikov--Stoehr (2007). An accessible complete proof is
  Altassan (2013), Theorem3.3, pp.24--25. If the ambient rank is two
  the assertion is immediate; otherwise that theorem applies. All
  algebras here have finite or countable rank. The assertion is
  never applied to arbitrary coefficients without constructing a
  free basis containing them.
* **Lazard elimination.** The ideal generated by b in Lie(a,b) is
  free on (ad_a)^j b, j>=0. Thus its enveloping associative algebra
  is freely generated by this chain. This is the classical
  elimination theorem, also stated as Altassan's Theorem2.1, p.19.
* Standard Hall/Malcev coordinates, rational polynomial factorization,
  Groebner bases and integer Smith/Hermite operations are effective.

Shirshov--Witt also gives the needed centralizer fact: two independent
commuting elements would span a two-dimensional abelian subalgebra,
which cannot be free as a Lie algebra. Hence commuting nonzero Lie
elements are proportional. The argument remains true after extending
the scalar field to an algebraic closure.

## 2. A finite complete list of leading pairs

The identity target is immediate. Rank at most one, or class at most
one, has no other commutator. For a nonidentity target let d be its
first nonzero lower-central degree, and W its leading integral Lie
coordinate. If d=1 reject.

The exact transformations

    (x,y) -> (yx,y),          (x,y) -> (x,xy)

and their inverses preserve [x,y]. They realize elementary SL_2(Z)
operations on common leading coordinates. An exact rotation is

    (x,y) -> (y^(-1),y^(-1)xy),

with leading action (C,D)->(-D,C). Consequently factor weights can
be ordered p<=q. If p=q and the leading terms are dependent, write
them aE,bE with E primitive integral and use the Euclidean algorithm
in SL_2(Z) to replace their coefficients by (gcd(a,b),0). One factor
then has greater leading weight. It cannot become trivial when g!=1.

After this normalization the leading bracket is nonzero: unequal
weights cannot commute, and equal weights commute only when dependent.
Thus every solution has a normalized leading pair

    C in (L_Z)_p, D in (L_Z)_q, [C,D]=W, p<=q, p+q=d.       (1)

For p<q there are finitely many pairs of projective factors over an
algebraic closure. Here is an elementary projective argument. The
bracket is a bilinear map with no zero divisors. On the Segre variety
P(L_p) x P(L_q), its linear projection therefore has centre disjoint
from the variety. A positive-dimensional projective fibre would meet
the hyperplane given by that centre inside the fibre's ambient linear
space, a contradiction. Every fibre is consequently finite.

This finiteness is effective over Q. Normalize the first nonzero Hall
coordinate of C to1 in finitely many disjoint affine charts and solve
[C,D]=W. Each chart has a zero-dimensional solution set. Compute a
Groebner basis. In a nonzero finite-dimensional quotient algebra, the
coordinates of any rational point are rational roots of the characteristic
polynomials of coordinate multiplication. Factor those polynomials,
enumerate the finite products of rational roots and test the original
equations. This enumerates every rational point, including points of
a nonreduced scheme. Empty charts are detected by the unit ideal.

For each resulting first-factor line choose its primitive integral
representative C0. Solve [C0,D0]=W over Q; the solution is unique by
the centralizer fact. An integral pair on this line has the form

    (k C0,D0/k),             k a nonzero integer.

Therefore D0 must be integral. If it is, keep every signed divisor k
of the nonzero gcd of its Hall coordinates. This is a finite complete
list, with no scale allocation discarded.

For p=q, the map Lambda^2 L_p -> L_(2p) given by the bracket is
injective: split associative tensor words into two blocks of length p.
The image of C wedge D is C tensor D-D tensor C. Solve for the unique
integral exterior preimage of W. It must have alternating rank two.
Its rational support intersected with L_Z is a saturated rank-two
lattice P. Choose an oriented basis of P in which the exterior tensor
is h e1 wedge e2 with h>0. All integral factors form oriented bases
of index-h sublattices of P. Enumerate their finitely many Hermite
bases. The exact SL_2(Z) moves above connect every other oriented basis
of the same lattice to its representative while leaving higher group
coordinates unrestricted. Thus these representatives are complete.

This establishes a finite complete set of leading group branches for
every target. It uses the projective-fibre route, not the stronger
rotation/Klyachko lemma from the earlier central-target implementation.

## 3. Correction kernels before and after the Nielsen offset

Fix a leading pair (1), put n=c-d, and number corrections by their
offset s. The new group coordinates at offset s have weights p+s
and q+s. Their leading correction map is the constant integral map

    A_s(U,V)=[U,D]+[C,V].                                (2)

The spaces in (2) are the full ambient components L_(p+s),L_(q+s).
Higher fixed coordinates do not change this first-degree map.

For 0<s<q-p, a kernel has dimension at most one. Indeed U=0 forces
V=0. For nonzero U use the graded subalgebra

    Q C + Q U + direct_sum_(j>=p+s+1) L_j.

It is closed and C,U are independent in its abelianization, so they
belong to a homogeneous free basis. Since q>p+s, both D,V lie in
this subalgebra. The inner-solution theorem gives D,V in Lie(C,U).
If two independent first components U1,U2 occurred, include all three
C,U1,U2 in the same free basis. Then D would lie in both Lie(C,U1)
and Lie(C,U2), whose intersection is Q C, contrary to its weight q.
Call any nonzero kernel in this range **exceptional**.

If p<q and s=q-p, the kernel is Q(D,0). To prove it, use
Q C + direct_sum_(j>=q) L_j and include C and a basis of L_q
among its free generators. Kill C and all generators above degree q
in (2). This gives [U,D]=0 in the free algebra on L_q, so U is a
multiple of D. Then [C,V]=0 forces V=0. The exact substitution
x->y^k x supplies an integral period on this kernel. If D is
nonprimitive, all residues modulo its content must be kept.

For s>q-p, every kernel pair belongs to Lie(C,D). Apply the
inner-solution theorem in

    Q C + Q D + direct_sum_(j>=q+1) L_j,

which has a homogeneous free basis containing C,D, and contains U,V.
For p=q use L_(>=p) instead. The next section supplies full-rank
integral period lattices for all these kernels. No classification or
bound on the number of exceptional offsets is needed.

## 4. Exact periods for the universal kernels

In the free rational Lie algebra on a,b of weights p,q, truncated
above c, write W0=log(exp(-a)exp(-b)exp(a)exp(b)). There is a filtered
automorphism sigma with identity associated graded and

    sigma([a,b])=W0.                                    (3)

For a finite construction, every derived Lie element is in
[a,H]+[H,b], by Jacobi induction. At successive weights p+q+s,
correct the current images of a,b by terms of weights p+s,q+s
to remove the homogeneous discrepancy in (3). Earlier degrees do
not change. The process terminates at c, and a filtered map with
identity associated graded has a finite inverse. This is the usual
degreewise symplectic-expansion construction; the credit and finite
implementation are recorded in `universal-gauge-audit.md`.

Let u,v be homogeneous of weights p+s,q+s and satisfy
[u,b]+[a,v]=0. The derivation delta(a)=u,delta(b)=v raises weight
by s and kills [a,b]. Hence

    alpha = sigma exp(delta) sigma^(-1)

is a rational filtered automorphism fixing W0. Its first increments
are u,v at offset s. Its k-th power has polynomial coefficients in k.

Let Gamma be the group generated by A,B with weights p,q, obtained
by discarding Hall commutators of weighted degree greater than c.
The discarded Hall tail is normal; collecting retained words never
lowers weight. Its retained Hall coordinates are independent integral
coordinates, and its rational Malcev algebra is H. Convert
exp(alpha^k(a)), exp(alpha^k(b)) to Hall coordinates. They are rational
polynomials in k, with integral constant terms at k=0. Choose M>0
divisible by all denominators of their nonconstant coefficients.
At both k=M and k=-M the coordinates are integral. Thus alpha^M
and its inverse restrict to actual automorphisms of Gamma, fixing
[A,B] exactly.

For any pair x in gamma_p(N), y in gamma_q(N), substitution A->x,
B->y defines a homomorphism Gamma->N. The two words alpha^M(A),
alpha^M(B) therefore give an exact reversible pair substitution
preserving [x,y], with first increments M u(C,D), M v(C,D).
No automorphism of the ambient group N is asserted.

Noncommuting C,D freely generate their subalgebra. Every homogeneous
kernel pair after the Nielsen offset has rational expressions in C,D,
found by finite linear algebra, and its formal preimage satisfies the
same kernel equation in H. Apply the preceding construction to every
element of a complete integer kernel basis. The resulting integer
multiples span a full-rank period sublattice. Consequently each layer
with fixed lower coordinates needs only finitely many kernel residues.
All residues are retained; a rational representative alone is insufficient.

## 5. A diagonal polynomial separation lemma

Let E_0,E_1,... freely generate an associative algebra over Q and
let delta(E_i)=E_(i+1). Encode a polynomial with exactly m E letters
by replacing the coefficient of E_(i1)...E_(im) with the coefficient
of z1^i1...zm^im. This positional encoding is linear, not an algebra
homomorphism. Delta becomes multiplication by z1+...+zm.

Suppose delta V=[E_0,D], where D has m letters, and let P encode D.
Then the encoding of V is

    (P(z2,...,z_(m+1))-P(z1,...,zm))/(z1+...+z_(m+1)).     (4)

Its polynomiality implies cyclic invariance of P on the hyperplane
H={y0+...+ym=0}, where P is regarded as P(y1,...,ym).

Let R be the restriction of an (m+2)-letter encoding to

    z_(m+2)=z1,             z1+...+z_(m+2)=0.              (5)

**Lemma.** If R([E_0,V])=0, then P is constant.

Put z=(z1,...,zm), S=sum z_i and a=-S/2. Applying (4) to the two
terms of [E_0,V] and using (5) gives

    2P(z)=P(z2,...,zm,a)+P(a,z1,...,z_(m-1)).

The calculation divides by a on the open set a!=0, and then extends
as a polynomial identity. Cyclic invariance rewrites it as

    2P(z)=P(z+a e1)+P(z+a em).                            (6)

On H, let T_(i->j) halve yi and add its other half to yj. Equation(6)
says P is the average of its values after T_(0->1),T_(0->m).
Cyclic invariance gives the same averaging identity at every index,
with its two cyclic neighbours. All these maps preserve H and do
not increase the l1 norm.

On any closed l1 ball in H, P attains a maximum. If y is a maximum
point, both terms of each averaging identity must also attain that
maximum. Therefore every finite composition of the transfers preserves
maximality. A fixed product B of clockwise transfers, taking a full
cycle and repeating it m+1 times, is a positive column-stochastic
matrix. Every coordinate can send positive mass to every other by
a clockwise path, with retained halves allowing waiting.

Let epsilon>0 be B's smallest entry and l=m+1. Write
B=epsilon J+(1-l epsilon)C, where J is all ones and C is nonnegative
column-stochastic when the second coefficient is positive. On H,
Jy=0, so B strictly contracts the l1 norm. If that coefficient is
zero, B annihilates H directly. Thus B^k y->0, and continuity makes
the maximum P(0). Apply the same argument to the minimum. The ball
was arbitrary, so P is constant. This proves the lemma.

In the free Lie algebra on the E_i, a nonzero constant positional
polynomial would be a multiple of E_0^m. This is a Lie element only
for m=1. Thus, except for D=lambda E_0,V=0, a nonzero Lie solution
has R([E_0,V])!=0. Delta is injective on positive-length tensors,
since multiplication by their positional sum is injective.

Two further identities are immediate from (5):

    R(delta F)=0,
    R([E_j,F])=z1^j R([E_0,F]).                           (7)

They apply componentwise at each fixed E-letter count. These are
the identities that will eliminate all later parameter columns.

## 6. Separation in an arbitrary first-exception block

Suppose every pair coordinate below an exceptional offset t<q-p
has been fixed. Assume n>=2t. Keep every group coordinate at offsets
t,...,2t-1 and solve all commutator equations in those degrees jointly.
They form an affine integer linear system: two increments from different
factors first interact at offset2t, and two from one factor interact
with the other leading factor still later. Its complete solution set
is empty or an affine integer lattice S.

The first-degree kernel is a line. If its coefficient is constant on S,
the complete prefix through t is fixed. Otherwise choose an integral
basis of S adapted to that coefficient:

    k=k0+bT, b!=0,

with all other free parameters having zero first-exception component.
Every such other direction starts at an offset >t. No quotient of S
is needed, even when universal kernels occur in this block.

At offset2t the cokernel equation has the form

    q(T)+B S'=0 modulo im A_(2t),                         (8)

where q has degree at most two and B is constant. Mixed products of
T with any later parameter occur after2t. We prove the stronger fact

    q2 not in span_Q(im A_(2t), columns of B).             (9)

Here q2 is the quadratic coefficient expressed before taking the quotient.

### Normalize the fixed prefix over Q

Write X=log x,Y=log y. In the free graded subalgebra L_(>=p), include
C in a homogeneous free basis. The filtered substitution replacing C
by C plus the fixed components of X at offsets1,...,t-1, and fixing
the other basis elements, is invertible after truncation. Its inverse
makes X=C modulo degree p+t. It is fixed independently of the block
parameters and is identity on the associated graded. This rational
coordinate change is used only to prove (9).

The part of log[x,y] linear in Y is [X,Z], where

    Z=h(ad_X)Y,          h(z)=(1-exp(-z))/z, h(0)=1.

Every other BCH term has at least two Y occurrences and one X.
If it depends on a block parameter, its weight is at least p+2q+t,
which exceeds d+2t because q>p+t. These terms are therefore fixed
through the degree at issue and may be subtracted from the target.
They are not asserted to vanish.

Through their required degrees, X,Y,Z are affine in all block parameters.
Two varying X increments have weight at least2(p+t)>p+2t, and two
varying Y increments have weight at least2(q+t)>q+2t. In h(ad_X)Y,
two varying X increments have weight at least q+2(p+t)>q+2t, and
a varying X and Y together have weight at least p+q+2t>q+2t.
Fixed positive-weight factors only increase these bounds. The fixed
filtered substitution is linear on Lie elements and preserves them.

### Project the whole block

Take the graded free subalgebra

    A=Q C + direct_sum_(j>=p+t) L_j.

It contains every required component of X,Z after normalization.
Its entire degree-(p+t) component is independent in its abelianization.
Include C and the nonzero first kernel component U in a homogeneous
free basis, then project onto Lie(C,U) by killing all other basis
elements. The inner-solution theorem gives D,V in Lie(C,U), where
[U,D]+[C,V]=0. In particular this projection fixes D,V. Including
the entire L_(p+t) in A also covers arbitrary particular solutions
at t; no unsupported restriction to the kernel line is imposed on them.

Set E_j=(ad_C)^j U and delta=ad_C. The ideal of U is free on the E_j.
Let D_m be the nonzero part of D with the most E letters. Since q>p+t,
D has no pure-C or scalar-U part that could be its only component.
The corresponding V_(m+1) satisfies

    delta V_(m+1)=-[E_0,D_m].

Section5, with the harmless sign change, gives
R([E_0,V_(m+1)])!=0. A constant positional D_m is excluded by the
Lie condition for m>1 and by q>p+t for m=1.

Every projected first-factor component at offset i<=2t has at most
one E letter, since 2(p+t)>p+2t. It is therefore zero or a multiple
of E_j with pj=i-t; pure C has the wrong weight. This applies to
all particular and all parameter components. Consequently the
projection of [L_(p+2t),D] has at most m+1 E letters, and R kills
[C,L_(q+2t)]. In E-letter count m+2 it annihilates im A_(2t).

For 0<=j<t, let F_j be the E-letter-(m+1) part of the fixed Z
component at offset j. Then F_0=0. Take the coefficient of T in
the already satisfied degree d+t+j equation and apply the projection
and R in E-letter count m+2. Varying Z paired with a positive-offset
fixed X would require an X-offset below t, which was normalized to
zero. Pairing with C is a delta image. Thus the only remaining terms
are the triangular convolution

    b R([E_0,F_j]) + sum_(i=t+1)^(t+j)
        b_i z1^((i-t)/p) R([E_0,F_(t+j-i)]) = 0.           (10)

Only integral exponents occur. Starting with F_0=0, induction and
b!=0 show R([E_0,F_j])=0 for every j<t. This uses the compatibility
of the *full surviving block*, not a hypothesis on arbitrary deformations.

At degree d+2t, any other parameter starts at an offset >t. Its
varying X components pair only with fixed Z-offsets <t; (7) and
(10) kill them. Its varying Z components pair with C or with fixed
X-offsets <t; these are killed or zero respectively. Hence R kills
every column of B, including all universal columns.

The coefficient of T^2 is b^2[U,V]. Its E-letter-(m+2) part is
b^2[E_0,V_(m+1)] and has nonzero R-image. This proves (9).

Finally (9) is a statement about the actual group coordinates. After
the lower block equations have been satisfied, the target error
begins at degree d+2t. Its Hall and logarithmic coordinates agree
in that degree, and the filtered normalization is identity there.
Thus the Lie functional pulls back to the actual group correction
space. No integral solution has been replaced by a rational point.

## 7. Terminating integral recursion

Process each of the finitely many leading pairs from Section2.
At a layer with zero kernel, solve the full integral affine system
and retain its unique solution, or reject. At a universal layer,
retain all residues of a full-rank exact period lattice from Section4.
The lower prefix stays fixed and all later coordinates remain free.

At the first remaining exceptional offset t, if n<2t, all remaining
coordinates enter the full remaining commutator equation jointly
linearly. Solve that integer system and finish this branch. Otherwise
form the full affine block S from Section6. Reject it if empty.

If its first-exception coefficient is constant, fix the entire prefix
through t and restart at t+1. Otherwise use rational linear algebra
on the actual matrices in (8) to find a scalar functional annihilating
im A_(2t) and every column of B but not q2; (9) proves it exists.
Applying it yields a nonzero scalar quadratic in T. Enumerate all
integer roots, at most two. For each root fix the resulting entire
prefix through t and restart at t+1.

This restart deliberately leaves the higher block coordinates free
again. Every old solution is retained because its first parameter
had one of these necessary root values. Forgetting higher restrictions
can only repeat possible continuations; a positive answer still
requires every equation through class c. The full integer block,
its possibly nonunit step b, and every later integral congruence are
retained by their respective exact linear solves. A rational root
or rational correction is never accepted in place of an integer one.

Each recursion strictly increases the fixed offset. Each branch
has finitely many children; there are at most n offsets. Universal
period construction terminates by explicit denominator clearing.
Hence the finite tree terminates. When a branch reaches the last
offset, assemble its Hall-coordinate factors and verify their
commutator. Conversely any original solution is represented in a
leading branch and survives every necessary equation or exact
normalization, so if all branches fail there is no solution.

This proves the proposed algorithm without an exception-count bound,
unbounded search over first parameters, or an integer-curve oracle.

## 8. Status and credits

The new point relative to the earlier partial algorithms is Section5's
diagonal separation and its full-block application in Section6.
The group-theoretic and Lie-theoretic machinery named in Sections1--4
is credited prior machinery. The formal symplectic expansion, integral
power construction and finite leading-factor algorithms were written
and audited in earlier stages of this experiment; historical partial
proofs remain available rather than being silently rewritten.

The three new actual group fixtures test a nonzero later obstruction,
a leading term with two E letters, and a nonzero fixed lower X prefix
with a retained two-Y BCH term. Forty-four complete Lie spaces have
independent Python/GAP reconstructions. These checks test specific
steps, not the general proof or its novelty. See `general-audit.md`
for source-reading limits, dependency checks, exact computations,
failures, manifests, and the current claim status.
