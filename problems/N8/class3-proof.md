# A decision procedure for one commutator in free nilpotent groups of class three

**Candidate partial answer to N8(b), 28 September 2026.** The argument below covers every finite rank in nilpotency class three. It does not answer the question for all nilpotency classes. Bibliographic novelty remains provisional; no outside review has occurred.

## Statement

For every positive integer r there is an effective procedure which, given a word representing g in N = F_r / gamma_4(F_r), decides whether g = [x,y] for some x,y in N. In the affirmative case it produces words for x and y. The procedure is uniform in r. Here [x,y] = x^-1 y^-1 x y.

The source is GroupWorld N8(b), `sources/raw/probnil.html`, with background in `sources/raw/Back2.html`. The original paragraph and rendered screenshot were checked. Its class-two predecessor is Roman'kov, *Diophantine questions in the class of finitely generated nilpotent groups*, J. Group Theory 19 (2016), 497–514, Theorem 2.1. The present argument is restricted to class three.

## Effective coordinates

Put V = N/gamma_2(N), a free abelian group with the specified basis. Standard collection in a free nilpotent group gives

    gamma_2(N)/gamma_3(N) = Lambda^2 V,
    gamma_3(N) = L_3(V),

where L_3(V) is the degree-three part of the free Lie ring on V. These identifications are effective: weight-two basic commutators give the first basis, and weight-three Hall commutators give the second.

One can perform all computations in the integral associative tensor algebra truncated after degree three, mapping the j-th free generator to 1+X_j and its inverse to 1−X_j+X_j^2−X_j^3. Products and inverses are finite integer operations. This representation detects equality in N. Indeed, vanishing in degree one puts a word in gamma_2, and vanishing in degree two puts it in gamma_3. The degree-three tensors of the Hall commutators are linearly independent, so vanishing there puts it in gamma_4. The independence can also be checked separately in each multidegree: one basis vector for each repeated-index multidegree and two independent vectors for each set of three distinct indices. Collection shows these exhaust gamma_3.

For g in gamma_2, write w for its degree-two tensor, identified with an exterior vector. If w=0, the degree-three tensor is the image of g in L_3(V); since gamma_4 is trivial, no further coordinates are needed.

Reject immediately when g has nonzero abelianization. Accept g=1. Rank one is thereby handled completely. Below g is nonidentity in gamma_2.

## Commutator-preserving changes of two elements

The transformations

    (x,y) -> (yx,y),        (x,y) -> (x,xy)

and their inverses preserve [x,y] exactly. For example,

    [yx,y] = x^-1 y^-1 y^-1 y x y = [x,y],
    [x,xy] = x^-1 y^-1 x^-1 x x y = [x,y].

On the two abelianization columns these are the two elementary determinant-one column operations. They generate SL(2,Z). Thus any determinant-one integer change of the pair's abelianizations can be effected while leaving its commutator exactly equal to g.

## Case 1: w is nonzero

If u,v are the abelianizations of a solution x,y, then u wedge v=w. The skew matrix of w must have rank two over Q. This condition is decidable by rational elimination; if it fails, reject.

Let P be the intersection of the rational support plane of w with V. It is an effectively computable primitive rank-two lattice. For example, clear denominators in rational equations for the plane and compute the integral kernel using Smith normal form. Choose an orientation of a basis p,q of P such that

    w = d (p wedge q),       d>0.

The integer d exists because P is a direct summand of V, so Lambda^2(P_Q) intersect Lambda^2(V) = Lambda^2(P).

The lattice generated by any pair u,v with u wedge v=w has index d in P. There are finitely many such sublattices; in the basis p,q, enumerate their column Hermite forms

    [[a,b],[0,c]],       a,c>0, ac=d, 0<=b<a.

Choose the displayed positively oriented basis for each sublattice. Any other oriented basis differs by SL(2,Z). The preceding commutator-preserving operations therefore show that it is enough to test this finite list of abelianization pairs.

For one pair u,v choose fixed lifts x0,y0. Every pair with these abelianizations is x=x0 c0, y=y0 d0, with c0,d0 in gamma_2. In a class-three group, gamma_2 is abelian and gamma_3 is central; the commutator identities give

    [x0 c0,y0 d0] = [x0,y0] [c0,y0] [x0,d0].             (1)

The last two factors are central, and depend only on c0,d0 modulo gamma_3. Thus they define a homomorphism of free abelian groups

    Lambda^2 V direct-sum Lambda^2 V -> L_3(V).

Its columns are computed by commutating each weight-two basic commutator with y0, and x0 with each such basic commutator. Because [x0,y0] and g have the same degree-two image, their quotient is in gamma_3. Equation (1) is solvable exactly when this quotient is in the image of that integer matrix. Smith normal form decides membership and supplies coefficients when it holds. Lift the coefficients to c0,d0 and return x0 c0,y0 d0.

The list is finite, every step terminates, and the preceding normalization proves completeness.

## Case 2: w is zero

The abelianizations u,v of a solution are dependent. If both vanish, the commutator is trivial, contrary to our current hypothesis. Write u=a p, v=b p for a primitive vector p and integers a,b, not both zero. A determinant-one change takes (a,b) to (gcd(a,b),0). The corresponding group transformations reduce the pair to x,y with abelianizations z,0, with z nonzero. Consequently y belongs to gamma_2 and

    g = [z,Y] in L_3(V),       z in V, Y in Lambda^2 V,   (2)

where Y is the image of y modulo gamma_3. Conversely every integral solution of (2) lifts to a group solution: commutation of lifts depends only on z,Y and its value lies in gamma_3.

We give a terminating rational reconstruction and integrality test for (2).

### Uniqueness lemma

Over Q, let T: V_Q tensor Lambda^2 V_Q -> L_3(V_Q) be T(z tensor Y)=[z,Y]. Its kernel is the space of alternating three-tensors. To see this directly, write an element of V_Q tensor Lambda^2 V_Q with coefficients A_ijk, skew in j,k. Its image in the associative tensor algebra has coefficients A_ijk−A_kij. The image vanishes precisely when A is invariant under cyclic permutation; together with skewness in the last two places this says A is fully alternating.

An alternating three-tensor lying in U tensor Lambda^2 V_Q is zero if dim(U)<=2. Choose a basis beginning with a basis of U. Its first index must lie in U, and alternation then forces all three indices to lie there, which is impossible for a nonzero alternating three-tensor in dimension at most two.

If two rank-one tensors z tensor Y and z' tensor Y' have the same nonzero image under T, their difference is in the kernel and has its first factor supported on the at-most-two-dimensional span of z,z'. It is therefore zero. Hence **the rank-one tensor representing a given nonzero g is unique**, when it exists.

### Rational reconstruction

Let g_abc be the integral associative degree-three coefficients of g. Write Y as a skew-symmetric matrix, so (2) says

    g_abc = z_a Y_bc − Y_ab z_c.                          (3)

For each i from 1 to r attempt the normalization z_i=1. Any nonzero rational solution permits at least one of these normalizations, with Y rescaled inversely. Put t_j=g_iij, which must equal Y_ij.

If some t_j is nonzero, equations (3) force

    z_j = g_jij/(2 t_j),
    z_k = (g_jik − z_j t_k)/t_j  for all k,
    Y_ab = g_iab + t_a z_b       for all a,b.

If all t_j vanish, equations (3) force Y_ab=g_iab. If this matrix is zero, the patch cannot represent our nonzero g. Otherwise choose j,k with Y_jk nonzero; then equations (3) force

    z_k = −g_jkk/Y_jk,
    z_l = (g_ljk + Y_lj z_k)/Y_jk  for all l.

In either case the formulas are finite rational operations. Check that z_i=1, Y is skew-symmetric, and every equation (3) holds; otherwise discard the patch. These formulas were obtained by substituting repeated or fixed indices into (3), so a genuine solution in the patch is necessarily recovered. If all patches fail, no rational—and hence no integral—solution exists.

### Integrality and witness

For a successful rational pair choose the primitive integer vector z0 on its rational line and rescale Y inversely to Y0, so z0 tensor Y0=z tensor Y. If all entries of Y0 are integers, lift z0 and Y0 to N to obtain a solution.

Conversely, every possible integral solution has the same rank-one tensor by the uniqueness lemma. Its first vector is k z0 for a nonzero integer k, and its exterior vector must be Y0/k. If this is integral, then Y0 is integral. Thus failure of the integrality check rules out every integral solution, including those in other coordinate patches. This completes the decision procedure and proof.

## Supporting verification and limits

The exact prototype is `scripts/n8_class3.py`. It uses truncated integral Magnus expansions for group operations, rational reconstruction for the central case, and SymPy's integer Smith decomposition for the finite lattice tests. Returned witnesses are checked against the input expansion. The test driver has a fixed seed and exports witnesses for a separate GAP nq evaluation; see the recorded runs in `results/` and `research/certificates/N8-class3/`.

The negative controls include:

- an element with nonzero abelianization;
- [a,b][c,d], whose degree-two skew matrix has rank four;
- [a,b]^2 in rank two, a known non-commutator from Akhavan-Malayeri–Rhemtulla, *Commutator length of abelian-by-nilpotent groups*, Glasgow Math. J. 40 (1998), 117–121, Theorem 1;
- [[a,b],c][[a,c],b], a central non-commutator in rank three. Its degree-three tensor has coefficients 1,1,1,1,−2,−2 on the six permutations of a,b,c and zero on repeated indices. If (3) held, its repeated coefficients would force z_i Y_ij=0 for all i,j. In rank three, nonzero Y would then force z to have support on a single coordinate. Such a tensor has zero coefficients when that coordinate is in the middle, whereas the displayed g has all six coefficients nonzero. Contradiction.

Tests support the implementation and detect sign, lattice and collection errors. The completeness argument above, rather than bounded computation, supplies the decision theorem. The word-list implementation expands powers and is not intended as an efficient large-input algorithm. Neither specialist validation nor a completed novelty determination is claimed.
