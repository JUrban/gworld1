# Single commutators in the rank-two free nilpotent group of class seven

Candidate extension, 28 September 2026. The single-commutator problem in
`F_2/gamma_8(F_2)` is decidable, constructively on positive inputs. This
extends the same partial N8(b) candidate; it does not settle the full
arbitrary-rank, arbitrary-class problem. Independent review and a fuller
novelty audit remain outstanding. Validation details are in the audit.

We use `[x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y` and the integral Hall Lie layers L_i on two
generators. The preceding [class-six proof](class6-proof.md),
[homogeneous factor procedure](penultimate-target-proof.md), and
all-class degree-two procedure supply the explicitly cited ingredients
below. All rational kernel calculations are converted to integral affine
lattices before making decisions. Rational solubility alone never suffices.

## 1. A rank-two degree-six injection

Write a,b for the degree-one generators and put

    u=[b,a], v=[u,a], w=[u,b],
    s=[v,a], t=[v,b], z=[w,b].

These are bases of L2, L3 and L4 respectively. The four degree-six
tensors `[v,w], [u,s], [u,t], [u,z]` are independent. In their
associative tensor expansions, take the rows indexed by

    aaabab, aababb, aabbab, ababbb.

The resulting integer matrix is

    [ 0 -1  0  0 ]
    [-1  0  0  0 ]
    [ 2  0  1  0 ]
    [ 0  0  0  1 ],

with determinant -1. This is a finite exact expansion proof, not a
conclusion inferred from random samples. Consequently the images
`[L3,L3]` and `[L2,L4]` have zero intersection, and the map
`L2 tensor L4 -> L6` is injective in rank two.

For nonzero C in L2 and D in L3, it follows that the kernel of

    L3(Q) + L4(Q) -> L6(Q), (U,V) -> [U,D]+[C,V]

is exactly `Q*(D,0)`. Indeed the two summands must vanish separately;
the exterior-square map on L3 is injective (split tensor words into
two blocks of length three), so U is proportional to D, and the
displayed four-column injection forces V=0. Its integral kernel is
generated by `(D/content(D),0)`.

## 2. Joint linear tails

Suppose fixed lifts x,y start in weights p,q and the remaining factor
corrections start in weights s,t. Through class c these corrections
affect the commutator linearly in their integral Hall coordinates if

    2s+q>c, p+2t>c, s+t>c,

and the resulting commutator increments commute. To see this, expand
`[xc,yd]` in the truncated integral Magnus algebra. Terms using both
correction factors have weight at least s+t. Terms using a correction
factor twice have weight at least 2s+q or p+2t: a commutator vanishes
when either entire original factor is the identity, so the opposite
factor must also occur. These bounds also remove interactions between
distinct Hall coordinates within one correction. The surviving terms
are additive integer multiples of the increments obtained by inserting
one Hall generator at a time. Higher terms of the fixed x,y are retained.

Equivalently, the map from correction Hall coordinates to
`[x,y]^-1[xc,yd]` is an additive homomorphism into the relevant abelian
tail. Compute all its columns by actual group multiplication. Matching
the entire remaining target is then one integer linear system. Solving
successive layers while discarding intermediate kernels would be
incorrect; the algorithm solves these tails jointly.

The cases used below have tail increments in gamma6 or gamma7, which
are abelian in class seven. Their weights (p,q;s,t) are

    (1,2;4,5), (1,3;3,5), (2,3;4,5), (1,4;3,6).

All satisfy the strict bounds above.

## 3. Leading degrees three and four

The complete finite leading-pair enumeration from the penultimate
proof applies unchanged, including all signed divisor allocations.
For leading degree three, the only type is (1,2). Match degree four
over the integers. Its rational kernel is the Nielsen line (D,0).
The **exact** commutator-preserving move `(x,y)->(yx,y)` shifts the
integer kernel parameter by content(D). Normalize an actual solution
to one of this finite number of residues. The move may change higher
terms, which remain free for subsequent matching.

For each residue, the degree-five correction is injective by map C
in the class-six proof. If its unique rational solution is integral,
incorporate it. The corrections now start in weights (4,5); solve
degrees six and seven jointly by Section 2.

For leading degree four, type (1,3) has an injective degree-five
correction by map D in the class-six proof. Incorporate its integral
solution if present, and solve the remaining tail with weights (3,5).
Type (2,2) has zero leading bracket in rank two, since L2 has rank one,
so cannot produce a nonzero leading degree-four target.

## 4. Leading degree five: type (2,3)

After finite leading-pair enumeration, the degree-six correction is
the map of Section 1. Its integral solution set is empty or an affine
line with primitive direction `(D/content(D),0)`. The exact move
`(x,y)->(yx,y)` shifts this parameter by content(D). Enumerate its
finitely many residues, incorporate actual lifts, and solve the final
degree-seven correction with factor weights (4,5). Section 2 applies.

## 5. Leading degree five: type (1,4)

Fix nonzero leading terms C in L1 and D in L4. The first correction
map is

    L2 + L5 -> L6, (U,V) -> [U,D]+[C,V].

Its rational kernel has dimension at most one. Projection of that
kernel to L2 is injective, since ad_C on L5 is injective by the
generator-centralizer argument proved in the class-five note.
In rank two L2 has dimension one. Smith normal form thus gives
either no solution, a unique integral solution, or

    v(k)=v0+k*K, k in Z,

with K a primitive generator of the **full integral** kernel.
In the unique case incorporate it and solve the final linear layer.

In the one-parameter case choose the ordered Hall lifts of v(k) in
weights 2 and 5 and call the resulting factors x(k),y(k). Their
commutators match the target through degree six. The remaining
central discrepancy

    R(k) = [x(k),y(k)]^-1 g in L7

is an integer-valued polynomial of degree at most two. A direct
Magnus-weight count proves this assertion: two occurrences of the
weight-two correction together with the weight-four opposite factor
already have weight eight. Two weight-five occurrences have weight
at least eleven. The only possible nonlinear interaction through
degree seven uses one correction of each kind (weights 2+5=7).
The weight-two Hall layer has a single generator; binomial terms in
its powers start in weight four and cannot contribute with the
opposite base factor below weight eight. Terms involving the base
degree-one factor do not decrease any of these bounds. Thus no cubic
or higher parameter term remains. This argument concerns actual
group expansions, and does not replace group commutators by Lie
brackets prematurely.

In integral Hall coordinates write

    R(k)=A+B*k+C2*binomial(k,2).

All three coefficient vectors are integral, obtained from R(0),R(1),R(2)
by finite differences. The remaining corrections have weights (3,6).
They contribute a fixed lattice

    Lambda = [L3,D]+[C,L6] <= L7.

This lattice is independent of k: interactions with the earlier
weight-two and weight-five corrections would have weight at least
eight. Section 2 makes this final correction exactly linear. It
therefore remains to decide whether R(k) belongs to Lambda for some
integer k.

## 6. Univariate polynomial membership in an integer lattice

Let M have the lattice generators as columns. Compute integer
unimodular matrices S,T with `S M T = diag(d1,...,dh,0,...)`.
Then membership is equivalent to divisibility of the first h
coordinates of S R(k) by their nonzero diagonal entries, and
vanishing of every remaining coordinate.

If at least one of these free-coordinate polynomials is nonzero,
it has at most two integer roots (or none if constant nonzero).
They are computed exactly by the linear formula or discriminant
test after doubling the Newton-basis polynomial. Check each root
against every free equality and every divisibility condition.

If all free polynomials vanish identically, put
`e=lcm(|d1|,...,|dh|)`, with e=1 when h=0. Each remaining condition
is periodic modulo 2e: changing k to k+2e changes k by a multiple
of every di and changes binomial(k,2) by

    2e*k + e*(2e-1),

also a multiple of every di. Enumerating 0<=k<2e is therefore
complete. On a successful k, solve Mz=R(k) integrally and construct
the final corrections. This is a terminating decision procedure;
no claim of a polynomial complexity bound is made.

## 7. Completeness and limits

The identity and nonzero abelianization are immediate. Targets with
nonzero degree-two component use the existing all-class IA algorithm.
Targets starting in degree six or seven use the previous penultimate
and central algorithms. Sections 3–6 cover every remaining target.
Every branch uses finite leading-pair enumeration, finite exact
normalization, integer linear algebra and at most a univariate
quadratic lattice decision. The normalization completeness argument
is inherited explicitly from the previous proofs. Every positive
branch returns factors with the exact original target commutator.

The rank-two restriction in Section 5 is essential to this proof:
in general, injection of the correction kernel into L2 only gives
the bound binomial(r,2). Bounded rank-three probes are not a proof
of a one-dimensional kernel in all ranks. No such extension is
claimed here.
