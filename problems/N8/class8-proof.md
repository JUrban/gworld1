# Single commutators in class eight, all finite ranks

Candidate extension, 28 September 2026. The six correction-kernel
arguments were first written approximately 16:14–16:18 UTC; the finite
quadratic obstruction below was found approximately 16:23 UTC. This
is a proposed proof, awaiting independent specialist review and a
fuller novelty audit. Computations and their limits are in
`class8-audit.md`.

**Proposed theorem.** For every finite rank r, single-commutator
membership in `F_r/gamma_9(F_r)` is decidable, with factors constructed
on positive inputs. This extends the same partial N8(b) candidate.
It does not answer N8(b) in arbitrary nilpotency class.

Ranks zero and one are immediate. Put r>=2. We use the convention
`[x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y`, integral Hall coordinates in the group, and the
free Lie algebra L over Q for kernel proofs. Rational linear algebra
classifies kernels; the algorithm always retains the full integral
solution lattice. We invoke the finite leading-pair enumeration from
`penultimate-target-proof.md`, the preceding low-class kernel lemmas,
and the arbitrary-class nonzero-degree-two and penultimate procedures.

## 1. Free generators for the derived Lie algebra

Write E=L2 and B=L3. Choose homogeneous free generators for L' whose
degree-two and degree-three generators are bases of E and B, and whose
degree-four and degree-five generators span complements

    L4 = W direct-sum [E,E],
    L5 = G direct-sum [E,B].

Existence follows from the homogeneous Shirshov lemma, with the precise
argument and primary credit given in Section 1 of `class7-proof.md`.
It is a standard ingredient, not a new assertion here. Decomposition
by the types of these free generators gives

    L''5 = E tensor B,
    L''6 = (E tensor W) direct-sum Lambda^2 B direct-sum Lie_3(E),
    L''7 = (E tensor G) direct-sum (B tensor W)
             direct-sum L_(2E,1B).                         (1)

Here Lie_3(E) denotes bracket-length three in the free algebra on E,
and the last summand consists of words with two E generators and one
B generator. Tensor identifications are via the bracket. These are
direct decompositions, including injectivity, because L' is freely
generated by the chosen homogeneous set.

Fix nonzero z in L1 and put delta=ad_z. The metabelian module map mu
from the earlier proofs satisfies

    mu(delta V)=ell_z mu(V),  mu(L'')=0,

where ell_z is a nonzero linear polynomial in a polynomial domain.
The earlier proofs give ker(mu|L4)=[E,E] and ker(mu|L5)=[E,B].
The map is injective on E,B and the chosen complement W. Consequently
the following maps are injective:

    f:E -> B,       f(T)=delta T,
    g:B -> W,       g(T)=projection_W(delta T),
    h:W -> G,       h(T)=projection_G(delta T).            (2)

For example, a zero projection in the last two maps would put delta T
in the corresponding metabelian kernel; multiplication by ell_z then
forces mu(T)=0 and hence T=0.

We also require

    ker(mu|L6)=L''6.                                      (3)

The elementary polynomial-syzygy argument used in the class-six and
class-seven proofs gives

    dim mu(L6)=r*binomial(r+4,5)-binomial(r+5,6).

Indeed its image is the degree-five polynomial vectors (P_i) with
sum t_i P_i=0; the basic syzygies t_i e_j-t_j e_i and their polynomial
multiples are images of iterated degree-one brackets. Put

    e=(r^2-r)/2, b=(r^3-r)/3,
    w=(r^4-r^2)/4-binomial(e,2).

Witt's formula gives the elementary identity

    (r^6-r^3-r^2+r)/6-dim mu(L6)
       = e*w+binomial(b,2)+(e^3-e)/3.

The right side is exactly dim L''6 from (1), and L''6 is already in
the kernel. This proves (3).

## 2. Two elementary free-Lie observations

First, for nonzero c in E and b in B the map

    Lambda^2 E direct-sum E -> L_(2E,1B),
    (T,S) -> [b,T]+[c,[S,b]]                              (4)

is injective. Work in the free associative algebra on bases containing
c and b. Projection to words starting with b gives `T+S tensor c=0`
in E tensor E. An alternating tensor cannot be a nonzero pure tensor
in characteristic zero. Thus S=T=0. Distinct B directions separate.

Second, suppose V belongs to Lie_3(E) and delta V has only the B
direction f(T), for a fixed nonzero T in E. Choose T as the first
vector of a basis (e_i) of E. In delta V, the component with B letter
f(e_i) is obtained by replacing one e_i at a time by f(e_i). For i>1
this component is zero. Substituting f(e_i) back to e_i multiplies
each multihomogeneous part of V by the number of its e_i occurrences.
Characteristic zero implies V has no such occurrences. Thus

    V belongs to Lie_3(QT)=0.                             (5)

We repeatedly use the elementary generator-centralizer fact established
in the class-five proof: in a free Lie algebra, a free generator has
no commuting homogeneous element of bracket length greater than one.
Changing a rational basis makes any nonzero vector in E, B or L1 a
free generator in the appropriate freely generated subalgebra. Also,
bracketing a tensor in Lambda^2 E with a B direction is injective:
its associative words ending in B recover that tensor.

## 3. Six correction kernels

The map correcting the leading pair C in Lp, D in Lq at offset j is

    L_(p+j) direct-sum L_(q+j) -> L_(p+q+j),
    (U,V) -> [U,D]+[C,V].                                (6)

We need the following six assertions, in addition to the earlier ones.

### (a) Type (1,3), offset two

For nonzero D in B, the kernel on B+L5 is exactly Q*(D,0).
Apply mu to [U,D]+delta V=0. Then V belongs to E tensor B.
The E tensor W component of delta V is (id tensor g)V, so V=0.
The exterior-square injection Lambda^2 B into L6 now gives U in QD.

### (b) Type (2,2), offset two

For independent C,D in E, the map on L4+L4 is injective.
Project to E tensor W in (1). The equation
`-D tensor Ubar+C tensor Vbar=0` and independence give Ubar=Vbar=0.
Thus U,V belong to Lambda^2 E. Choose C=c,D=d as basis directions.
Setting c=0 gives [Ubar,d]=0 in the free algebra on the other E
generators, so Ubar=0. Hence U=[c,S] for some S in E.

In the associative expansion of [[c,S],d]+[c,V], project to words
neither starting nor ending in c. After discarding the irrelevant
c-component of S, this leaves `-S*c*d-d*c*S`. Coefficients of s*c*d
for s distinct from c,d force all those components of S to vanish;
the coefficient of d*c*d is minus twice the d-component. Thus U=0.
The generator-centralizer fact gives V=0.

### (c) Type (1,4), offset two

For nonzero D in L4, the map on B+L6 is injective. Apply mu, using
(3), to put V in L''6. Its E tensor W component must vanish by
projection of delta V to E tensor G and injection of h. The B tensor
W equation is then

    U tensor Dbar+(id tensor g)A=0,  A in Lambda^2 B.      (7)

If U and Dbar are nonzero, projection to coker(g) puts Dbar=g(T)
for a nonzero T, and (7) would make A=-U tensor T. This is impossible:
a nonzero pure tensor is not alternating. If U=0, delta V=0 implies
V=0 directly. It remains to treat D in Lambda^2 E. Equation (7)
then gives A=0, so V belongs to Lie_3(E).

If U is nonzero, separating B components outside f(E) in [U,D]
forces U in f(E). Write U=f(T). The equation delta V=-[f(T),D]
and (5) give V=0, and then [f(T),D]=0 forces D=0, a contradiction.
This proves injectivity in every case.

### (d) Type (1,5), offset one

For nonzero D in L5, the map on E+L6 is injective. Again (3) puts
V in L''6. If U=0, V=0. Assume U is nonzero. Projection to E tensor
G forces D_G=h(T) and V_EW=-U tensor T for some T in W. Projection
to B tensor W gives

    -f(U) tensor T+(id tensor g)A=0.

The same alternating/pure-tensor argument forces T=A=0. Thus
D belongs to E tensor B and V to Lie_3(E). Separating B directions
outside f(E), using (4) with its first argument zero, puts D in
E tensor f(E).

Define a derivation epsilon on the free algebra on E and f(E) by
epsilon(f(e_i))=e_i, epsilon(e_i)=0. It gives epsilon(delta V)=3V.
Therefore `3V=-[U,epsilon(D)]`, so V=[U,T] for some T in Lambda^2 E.
Substituting in the original equation gives

    [f(U),T]+[U,delta T+D]=0.

Separate the B components. For b=f(U), (4) forces T=0 and the
corresponding component of delta T+D to vanish. For every other B
direction, (4) with T=0 forces its component to vanish as well.
Thus D=0, a contradiction, proving injectivity.

### (e) Type (2,4), offset one

For nonzero C in E and D in L4, the map on B+L5 is injective.
The E tensor G component first puts V in E tensor B. If Dbar in W
is nonzero, the B tensor W component forces U=0, and then V=0 by
the generator-centralizer fact. Otherwise D belongs to Lambda^2 E.
If U is nonzero, take b=U as a B basis direction. In its component
the equation is precisely (4), with c=C and T=D. Thus D=0, a
contradiction. This also forces U=V=0.

### (f) Type (3,3), offset one

For independent C,D in B, the map on L4+L4 is injective.
The B tensor W component first gives U,V in Lambda^2 E.
The bracket map `(Lambda^2 E) tensor B -> L_(2E,1B)` is injective
by its associative words ending in B. The remaining equation is
`U tensor D-V tensor C=0`; independence of C,D gives U=V=0.

## 4. The exceptional quadratic obstruction

The first (1,4) correction, on E+L5, was classified in Section 4
of `class7-proof.md`. Its kernel is zero unless

    D=delta^2 T,  T in E nonzero,

in which case it is the line Q*(T,-[T,delta T]). We now prove that

    Q=[T,[T,delta T]]

lies outside the rational image of the *next* correction map

    A7:B+L6 -> L7, (U,V) -> [U,D]+delta V.                (8)

Suppose A7(U,V)=Q. Since mu(Q)=0, (3) puts V in L''6. The E tensor
G component kills V_EW as above. The B tensor W equation is (7).
Here Dbar=g(f(T)) is nonzero by (2). Consequently the alternating
argument gives U=A=0. We would have delta V=Q with V in Lie_3(E).
The only B direction of Q is f(T), so (5) gives V=0. But Q is a
nonzero Lie word in the two distinct free generator directions T
and f(T). This contradiction proves the assertion.

Now suppose the integral first-correction solutions are v0+kK, k in Z,
where K is a primitive generator of the full integral kernel. For
some nonzero rational rho,

    K=rho*(T,-[T,delta T]).

Use ordered integral Hall lifts of these corrections, of weights two
and five, to obtain x(k),y(k). Their commutators agree with the target
g through degree six. The degree-seven component of

    [x(k),y(k)]^-1 g

is an integer-valued quadratic polynomial R7(k). This degree bound
follows directly from group weights: two weight-two corrections with
the opposite weight-four base first contribute in weight eight;
two weight-five corrections with the degree-one base first contribute
in weight eleven. The only quadratic interaction through degree seven
is one correction of each kind, of weights two and five. Products of
different weight-two Hall powers first introduce quadratic dependence
in weight four, again too late to interact with the weight-four base
below degree eight. Thus no cubic parameter term occurs in R7.

The coefficient of k^2 in R7 is rho^2 Q (changing the convention for
the discrepancy can reverse its sign). It is nonzero in coker(A7)
by the preceding argument. Therefore at least one rational linear
functional vanishing on im(A7) sends R7(k) to a nonzero quadratic.
There are **at most two** possible integer k with

    R7(k) in im_Z(A7).                                  (9)

This is an effective and complete restriction, not a bounded search.
Integer Hermite or Smith reduction finds the free polynomial equations
and divisibility conditions for (9). Compute all integer roots of one
nonzero free equation, then test every free equation and congruence
for each root. By Section 3(c), the correction in L3+L6 is unique
over Q; retain it only when it is integral. No infinite progression
of parameters survives, and no multivariable Diophantine procedure
is invoked.

After applying this unique correction, solve the final degree-eight
integer linear system with columns [L4,D]+[z,L7]. The chosen lower
corrections affect its right side but not its columns. This completes
the exceptional branch.

## 5. All target strata and termination

Use the complete finite leading-pair enumeration already proved in
`penultimate-target-proof.md`. It retains every signed integral scale
for unequal leading weights and finite oriented-Hermite representatives
for equal weights. Exact commutator-preserving Nielsen transformations
supply the reduction to that enumeration. No rational-only leading
pair is silently promoted to an integral solution.

For fixed base weights p,q, a joint tail starting in correction weights
s,t is linear through class eight whenever

    2s+q>8, p+2t>8, s+t>8,

and its increments commute. The exact Magnus-weight argument is in
Section 2 of `class7-rank2-proof.md`. The tails below have increments
in gamma6 or deeper, so their products commute. Solve each tail as
one integer system, retaining its entire kernel; do not choose an
arbitrary solution at an intermediate noninjective layer.

For nonidentity targets with leading weight d, the procedure is:

| d / leading type | Corrections before the final joint linear tail |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Reject by abelianization. |
| 2 | Invoke the existing arbitrary-class IA procedure. |
| 3: (1,2) | Use the earlier first Nielsen line and injective second correction; tail weights (4,5). |
| 4: (1,3) | Earlier injective first correction; Section 3(a) normalizes the second correction by a finite Nielsen period; tail weights (4,6). |
| 4: (2,2) | Earlier injective first correction and Section 3(b) second correction; tail weights (5,5). |
| 5: (2,3) | Earlier first Nielsen line; tail weights (4,5). |
| 5: (1,4) | If the first kernel is zero, incorporate the unique integral solution and use tail weights (3,6). Otherwise use Section 4, then one final linear layer. |
| 6: (1,5), (2,4), (3,3) | Sections 3(d–f) give the unique first correction when integral; then one final linear layer. |
| 7,8 | Invoke the existing penultimate/central procedures. |

The Nielsen line in Section 3(a) has full integral direction
`(D/content(D),0)`. The exact transformation `(x,y)->(yx,y)` preserves
`[x,y]` and shifts its parameter by content(D). It does not change
already fixed lower coordinates, since y begins in weight three.
Thus all actual solutions can be normalized to finitely many residues.
The other two Nielsen normalizations are exactly those proved earlier.
Higher terms altered by these transformations remain free in later
corrections, so no possible solution is lost.

The identity is a positive instance. Every other possible leading type
appears in the table. All enumerations, normalizations, integer linear
systems and quadratic root lists are finite. Each accepted branch
constructs factors with the exact target commutator; a rejected target
has exhausted the complete branch list. This proves the proposed
class-eight decision theorem, with no assertion of polynomial runtime.

## 6. Limits

The structural dependency added in class seven remains the credited
homogeneous Shirshov lemma. The new kernel proofs and the quadratic
cokernel argument are written out above. Bounded computations support
the implementation and detect errors; they do not establish the
all-rank statements or novelty. There is no external proof review yet.
The arbitrary-class problem still has uncovered intermediate target
layers in classes nine and higher, and this is still one partial
candidate rather than another whole-entry solution.
