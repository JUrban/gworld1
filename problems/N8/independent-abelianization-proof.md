# N8(b): independent abelianized factors in arbitrary nilpotency class

**Draft argument, 28 September 2026, approximately 11:44–11:49 UTC. Not yet included in the claim ledger.** This arose after the class-five candidate. Its effective constructions need a separate audit and computational sanity checks; novelty is unverified.

## Proposed theorem

Given finite r,c and g in the free nilpotent group N=F_r/gamma_(c+1), with nonzero image in gamma_2/gamma_3, one can decide whether g is a single commutator, and construct factors when it is. The procedure is uniform in r and c. Inputs outside gamma_2 are rejected immediately.

This covers one target stratum in every class. It does not decide the case g in gamma_3 when c>=6. The separate class-three, class-four and class-five proofs cover all target strata in those classes.

All computations use integral Hall coordinates. Put V=N/gamma_2=Z^r and L_j=gamma_j/gamma_(j+1). Use [x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y. Ranks below two and classes below two are immediate.

## 1. The IA group and its effective generators

Let U be the group of automorphisms of N acting identically on V. For 2<=j<=c+1 put

    U_j = {alpha: alpha(X_i) = X_i mod gamma_j for every basis generator X_i}.

Then U=U_2 and U_(c+1)=1. Each U_j acts identically on every L_k, and its first correction induces

    U_j/U_(j+1) = Hom_Z(V,L_j).

Here surjectivity is constructive: for any integral D:V->L_j, choose group lifts d_i of D(e_i) and send X_i to X_i d_i. This is an automorphism. Indeed it is an endomorphism by the universal property of the free nilpotent group and induces the identity on every graded layer. Successively correcting the images of generators in gamma_2,...,gamma_c constructs an inverse. Equivalently its image generates N modulo the derived group, hence generates this nilpotent group, and injectivity follows from the graded layers.

For every Hall commutator b of weight j and generator index i, the map

    E_(i,b): X_i -> X_i b,   X_k -> X_k for k != i

is therefore an automorphism. These finitely many maps, for weights 2 through c, generate U: strip off any desired correction in U_j/U_(j+1), then proceed to the next layer. Inverses, products and evaluation on words are effective by Hall collection.

The usual substitution calculation gives

    [U_j,U_k] <= U_(j+k-1),
    alpha(x) = x mod gamma_(m+j-1) for x in gamma_m, alpha in U_j.

For completeness, modulo the first affected degree, substituting X_i times a weight-j correction replaces one letter of a weight-m commutator and raises its weight by j-1. The two orders of substitutions of weights j and k agree until their combined increase j+k-2. Inverses have the same filtration property. Induction on commutator words gives the displayed inclusions. Thus U is nilpotent of class at most c-1; its subgroups have the same upper bound.

## 2. An explicit orbit algorithm for U acting on N

We need to decide whether h=alpha(g_0) for some alpha in U. The action is trivial on V; reject unequal abelianizations. Otherwise begin with a=identity and K=U, and proceed through j=2,...,c. At the start of step j, a(g_0)=v agrees with h modulo gamma_j, and all possible automorphisms giving this agreement form the coset K a (composition acts on the left). The group K is given by finitely many explicit automorphisms.

For k in K define

    mu_j(k) = v^-1 k(v) mod gamma_(j+1) in L_j.

This is a homomorphism: each difference lies in gamma_j, which is central modulo gamma_(j+1), and U acts trivially on L_j. Let delta=v^-1 h mod gamma_(j+1). Compute mu_j on the generators of K. Integer Smith normal form decides if delta belongs to their generated lattice. If not, no orbit element exists. If it does, a product k_0 of generator powers realizes delta. Replace a by k_0 a and K by ker(mu_j).

These updates preserve exactly the solution coset for the next layer. In particular mu_j can be computed using v or h because these differ by gamma_j and U fixes that layer. At the final step a(g_0)=h exactly.

Here is a fully finite kernel construction, so no unmentioned orbit or Diophantine oracle is needed. Suppose K is generated by k_1,...,k_s, and let M be the integer matrix with columns mu_j(k_i). Compute an integral basis of ker(M:Z^s->L_j). The group ker(mu_j) is the normal closure in K of:

1. all [k_i,k_l];
2. the words k_1^(v_1)...k_s^(v_s) for those lattice-basis vectors v.

To verify this assertion, lift to the free group on the k_i. After killing its commutator subgroup, the map is precisely M on Z^s; the listed words generate its kernel normally. Taking the image in K proves the claim regardless of relations among the k_i.

Because K has nilpotency class at most c-1, this normal closure has a computable finite generating set: include every listed word and all its left-nested commutators with signed generators k_i^(+/-1), to nesting depth c-1. Commutators of greater depth vanish. Conjugating a retained generator by any signed k_i multiplies it by the next retained commutator, or by 1 at the depth bound. The generated subgroup is therefore normal and equals the required normal closure. The list can be very large, but its size is bounded and it is effective. Identity automorphisms may be discarded by testing their generator images.

This proves termination and both outcomes of the orbit procedure, with a constructive automorphism on a positive answer.

## 3. Finitely many IA orbits of pairs with fixed independent abelianizations

Fix independent u,v in V. At degree j, U_j changes the degree-j coordinates of a pair (x,y) with these abelianizations by

    D -> (D(u),D(v)),   D in Hom_Z(V,L_j).

This follows from the substitution rule in Section 1. The map has full rational rank because u,v are independent. Its integral cokernel is finite and has an effective set of representatives R_j. More explicitly, if the integer matrix [u v]^t has nonzero Smith invariant factors s_1,s_2, then this cokernel is

    (Z/s_1 Z)^(rank L_j) + (Z/s_2 Z)^(rank L_j).

Start with chosen lifts of u,v. At degree two, select corrections from R_2. At degree three, append corrections from R_3 to each previously selected pair, and continue through degree c-1. This is a finite construction of actual pairs of group words.

Every pair (x,y) with the given abelianizations is equivalent under U to one of the selected pairs modulo gamma_c in each factor. To prove this, suppose its lower coordinates already match a selected pair. Its next coordinate difference is an element of L_j^2. Choose its representative modulo the image of D->(Du,Dv), and apply an automorphism in U_j realizing the required difference. Lower coordinates stay fixed; higher ones are handled at later steps. This induction proves completeness. A gamma_c factor is central and does not affect the commutator.

Consequently the commutators of all these pairs form the union of the U orbits of the finitely many selected commutators. Membership in that union is decidable by Section 2.

One can also include j=c if representatives of pairs themselves, rather than their commutators, are desired. The shorter list above is sufficient here.

## 4. Reduction of the target to finitely many leading pairs

Let w be the nonzero image of g in L_2=Lambda^2 V. If it is not decomposable, reject. This is decidable by the rank of its alternating matrix: a nonzero decomposable exterior tensor has rank two.

Otherwise compute the primitive lattice P=(support_Q(w)) intersect V, choose its oriented basis p,q and write w=d p wedge q with d>0. The possible pairs u,v with u wedge v=w fall into finitely many SL(2,Z) classes, represented by index-d sublattices of P with Hermite bases

    u=a p,   v=b p+c q,   ac=d,   0<=b<a.

The exact group moves (x,y)->(yx,y) and (x,y)->(x,xy) preserve [x,y] and realize elementary determinant-one operations on the abelianization columns. Hence every actual solution pair can be normalized to one of these leading pairs without changing g.

For each leading pair construct Section 3's finite representatives and apply Section 2's orbit test to their commutators and g. Acceptance produces factors by applying the witnessing automorphism to the selected pair. If every orbit test fails, the preceding normalizations prove that no solution exists.
