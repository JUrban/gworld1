# GA3: discrimination by conjugating one free factor

Drafted during the active run, 29 September 2026. Candidate proof;
independent specialist review and novelty assessment remain outstanding.

## Statement and the omitted hypothesis

The frozen statement asks whether, for a group G acting freely on a
Lambda-tree, every finite subset of (G*G) minus {1} survives some
homomorphism G*G -> G. As already recorded in the earlier scope audit,
the literal statement is false for every nontrivial abelian G: the
commutator of nonidentity elements from the two factors cannot survive.
We use the usual convention that a free tree action has no inversions.

**Theorem.** Suppose G is nonabelian and acts freely without inversions
on a Lambda-tree, for an arbitrary totally ordered abelian group Lambda.
For every finite W contained in (G*G) minus {1}, there are h in G and an
integer N such that, for every n >= N, the homomorphism

    Phi_n: G*G -> G,
    Phi_n(a in the first copy) = a,
    Phi_n(b in the second copy) = h^n b h^(-n)

is nontrivial on every member of W.

Thus the intended nonabelian question has a positive answer. Together
with the elementary abelian obstruction, the property in the literal
question holds exactly when G is trivial or nonabelian. The proof does
not assume finite generation, finite presentation, or finite height of
Lambda, and makes no algorithmic claim about obtaining h or N from an
unspecified presentation of G.

## 1. A controlled real-tree action

We first prove the following reduction, including the choice of scale.

**Lemma 1.** Every finitely generated nonabelian Lambda-free group K has
an isometric action on a real tree X with the following properties:

1. some element is hyperbolic;
2. pointwise stabilizers of nondegenerate arcs are abelian;
3. pointwise stabilizers of nondegenerate tripods are trivial;
4. K preserves neither an end nor a line.

**Proof.** Let T be a free Lambda-tree for K. Extend scalars to the
divisible ordered group Lambda tensor Q. The canonical extension of
the action is still free: every nonidentity element's positive
translation length is preserved. Choose a finite symmetric generating
set S of nonidentity elements. Write ell for translation length and put

    L = max({ell(s): s in S} union {ell(st): s,t in S}) > 0.

The axis A_s of a hyperbolic element is a closed convex linear subtree.
For disjoint axes A_s,A_t, their unique bridge has length d and the
tree product formula is

    ell(st) = ell(s) + ell(t) + 2d.

These elementary tree facts follow by projecting to the axes and
concatenating the translated bridge and axis segments. In particular,
all pairwise distances between generator axes are at most L/2. Their
closed L-neighborhoods are convex, pairwise intersecting subtrees.
The finite Helly property for subtrees gives x in their intersection.
The displacement formula on a tree gives

    d(x,sx) = ell(s) + 2d(x,A_s) <= 3L.                 (1)

Let A be the principal convex subgroup generated by L:

    A = {lambda : |lambda| <= m L for some positive integer m}.

Every distance in T0 = Hull(Kx) lies in A: orbit distances are bounded
by (1) and the triangle inequality, and points in the convex hull lie
on finite segments between orbit points. Thus T0 is an A-tree with a
free K-action. Let

    I = {lambda in A : m |lambda| < L for every positive integer m}.

This is the maximal proper convex subgroup of A, and A/I is
Archimedean. Embed A/I in R, collapse the I-distances in T0, and extend
scalars to R. The resulting tree is X.

Some u in S union S^2 has ell(u)=L. Projection of x onto A_u lies on
[u^(-1)x,ux], hence belongs to T0. Its u-orbit survives the collapse
with successive distances equal to the nonzero image of L. Therefore
u acts hyperbolically on X. This step prevents a collapse to a global
fixed point; an arbitrary scale chosen from basepoint displacements
would not suffice.

Guirardel, Fact 5.1 and its explicit general-Lambda remark, supplies
abelian arc fixators and trivial tripod fixators after killing any
convex subgroup. These properties also hold after the last extension
to R. Indeed A was made divisible, so the nonzero Archimedean group
A/I is divisible and dense in R. The extension fills segments and adds
no branch points. Every nondegenerate fixed arc has an old subarc, and
every fixed tripod has an old tripod in its three directions. Thus a
violation on X would give a violation before the extension.

Recall also that K is torsion-free and CSA: its maximal abelian
subgroups are malnormal (Guirardel, Lemma 2.5). Consequently a
nonabelian K has no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup. To see this,
if 1 != B is such a subgroup and M is maximal abelian containing B,
then B is contained in M intersect kMk^(-1) for every k in K.
Malnormality forces every k into M, a contradiction.

If K preserves a line, the kernel of its action on that line is
abelian, since it fixes an arc. This abelian normal subgroup is
trivial. K therefore embeds in Isom(R). A faithful torsion-free
subgroup of Isom(R) consists of translations, since a reflection has
order two. K would be abelian.

If K fixes an end xi, use its Busemann homomorphism chi:K -> R. An
element of ker(chi) fixes a terminal ray toward xi: tree isometries
are elliptic or hyperbolic, and a hyperbolic isometry fixing xi has
nonzero Busemann displacement. Any two elements of the kernel fix a
common terminal ray, and commute by the abelian arc-fixator property.
Thus ker(chi) is abelian and normal, hence trivial. Again K embeds
in an abelian group, a contradiction. This proves the lemma. QED.

The closed-axis, projection, scalar extension and displacement facts
used above are reviewed in Guirardel, Sections 2.3--2.5. The finite
Helly argument uses only medians of three points, and does not require
metric completeness of T or X.

## 2. An element whose ends avoid finitely many constants

**Lemma 2.** Let K act on X as in Lemma 1. For every finite set
C contained in K minus {1}, there are a hyperbolic h and disjoint
nonempty open subsets U+,U- of the boundary of X, containing the
corresponding ends of h, such that

    c(U+ union U-) intersect (U+ union U-) = empty   for every c in C,
    h^n(boundary(X) minus U-) subset U+,
    h^(-n)(boundary(X) minus U+) subset U-          for every n >= N  (2)

for some N.

**Proof.** Since the action has a hyperbolic element, preserves no
line and fixes no end, it is of general type: it has two hyperbolic
elements with disjoint pairs of ends. One can also see this without
the general classification. If all translates of one hyperbolic
element's two-end set pairwise intersect, this family of two-element
sets either has a common point, consists of one invariant pair, or
is contained in the three pairs of a three-element set. The first
two cases give a fixed end or an invariant line. In the third case
a positive power of the original hyperbolic element fixes three ends,
which is impossible for a hyperbolic isometry of a real tree.

Every nonidentity c fixes at most two ends. If it fixed three, it
would fix their central branch point and all three rays pointwise,
contradicting the trivial tripod-fixator property.

Let Z be the limit set of the action in the boundary of X. It is
infinite. Choose xi in Z outside the at most 2|C| ends fixed by
members of C. Next choose eta in Z outside the same finite set and
outside {xi} union {c xi, c^(-1)xi : c in C}. Then

    c{xi,eta} intersect {xi,eta} = empty   (c in C).

The Hausdorff boundary topology and continuity give disjoint
neighborhoods V+,V- of xi,eta whose union V satisfies cV intersect V
empty for every c in C. Pairs of attracting and repelling ends of
hyperbolic elements are dense in Z times Z for an action of general
type. Choose h with its ends in V+ and V-, respectively.

For completeness, this density is the standard tree ping-pong fact:
independent hyperbolic elements supply nested attracting half-trees,
and sufficiently long products put the attracting and repelling
ends in prescribed limit-set neighborhoods. A precise version valid
even for nonproper hyperbolic spaces is Rybak, Lemma 2.9, with its
earlier references. Here only real trees are used.

Shrink to half-tree neighborhoods U+,U- of the two ends along h's
axis, contained in V+,V-. Projection to the axis shows that h^n
maps the complement of U- into U+ for all sufficiently large n;
the reverse direction is identical. This is uniform north-south
dynamics, also recorded in Rybak, Lemma 2.6. The displacement is
n ell_X(h), so a single N works for both inclusions. QED.

## 3. Simultaneous discrimination and arbitrary generation

Take a finite W as in the theorem. Cyclically reduce each word in
the free product, retaining the conjugator. A nonidentity cyclic
core in one factor survives every Phi_n, because Phi_n restricts
to the identity or an inner automorphism on each factor.

Every other cyclic core can, after a cyclic permutation, be written

    a1 b1 a2 b2 ... am bm,

where all ai are nonidentity elements of the first copy and all bi
are nonidentity elements of the second. Identify these coefficients
with elements of G and collect them, across all cores, in a finite
set C. Choose any two noncommuting elements of G, and let K be the
subgroup generated by them and C. This is a finitely generated,
nonabelian Lambda-free subgroup. Apply Lemmas 1 and 2 to K and C.

Fix n >= N and an end zeta in U-. Reading the image of a cyclic
core from right to left gives

    zeta --h^(-n)--> U-
          --bm--> outside (U+ union U-)
          --h^n--> U+
          --am--> outside (U+ union U-).

The same four steps repeat for each remaining pair of syllables;
the final image lies in a1 U+, disjoint from U-. Thus the image
moves zeta and is nonidentity. The same h and N work for every
cyclic core in W. Conjugating back preserves nonidentity.

Finally, the displayed formula for Phi_n defines a homomorphism
on all of G*G, not just on K*K: both factor maps are actual
automorphisms of G. This justifies the reduction to finite K even
when G is not finitely generated. QED.

## Dependencies and prior scope

- V. Guirardel, *Limit groups and groups acting freely on R^n-trees*,
  Geometry & Topology 8 (2004), 1427--1470,
  [arXiv:math/0306306](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306306):
  Sections 2.3--2.6, Lemma 2.5, Fact 5.1 and its general-Lambda
  remark. No finite-presentation or Rips-machine conclusion is used.
- E. Rybak, *Boundary dynamics, triple transitivity, and mixed
  identities in weakly hyperbolic groups*,
  [arXiv:2605.14159v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14159v3), July 2026:
  Lemmas 2.6 and 2.9 state the boundary facts. The proof of Theorem
  3.10 uses the same general separation mechanism for mixed words.
  This mechanism is credited prior work, not a novelty claim here.
- G. Baumslag, A. Miasnikov and V. Remeslennikov,
  [*Discriminating and co-discriminating groups*](https://web.stevens.edu/algebraic/alexeim/Publications/All_files_new/dcod_46.pdf),
  Section 1.3 and Theorem 3, distinguish the free-square hypothesis
  from CSA and credit the torsion-free hyperbolic case as prior.

The proposed contribution is the full arbitrary-Lambda application,
with a nontrivial collapse and the extension to arbitrary G made
explicit. Whether this application is already known remains to be
checked. The conclusion is not inferred from CSA alone or from an
unverified big-powers hypothesis.
