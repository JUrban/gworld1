# N5: composite congruences and integral lifting audit

Internal adversarial audit, 28 September 2026, after the original candidate.
No correction to the proof or implementation was required. This is not
independent specialist review or another proposed resolution.

## The specific concern

The finite search in Section 4.3 of `proof.md` must use reductions of
integral splittings. An arbitrary idempotent over Z/M can have different
ranks at different prime divisors of M. It then cannot come from a
direct decomposition of a free abelian group. Solving each local system
independently and combining its projections would give false positives.

For example, in F=Z^2 require both basis vectors to belong to A+2F and
to B+3F, where F=A direct-sum B. The corresponding projection P onto A
would satisfy P=I modulo 2 and P=0 modulo 3. The matrix 3I is an
idempotent modulo 6 satisfying these conditions. Nevertheless an
integral splitting is impossible: its rank would have to be both two
and zero. This remains impossible when trivial factors are allowed.

A second example prescribes P=diag(1,0) modulo 2 and P=I modulo 3.
Its unique modular projection is diag(1,4) modulo 6, again with
incompatible local ranks. Both examples are rejected by the existing
integral stabilizer search.

By contrast, P=diag(1,0) modulo 2 and P=diag(0,1) modulo 3 is possible:
the local rank is one at both primes. The implementation constructs

    P = [[9, -6], [12, -8]],

an integral idempotent with the specified reductions. Its image has
rank one, its kernel has rank one, and they sum to Z^2. Large or negative
entries in a lift are not a reason to reject it.

These controls check a real distinction in the proof. The candidate
already retains integral words in the stabilizer generators and
enumerates each allowed integral rank. It never substitutes all
idempotents over Z/M for this search.

## Separate finite-ring oracle

For five rank-two controls, a second implementation enumerates every
2-by-2 matrix over Z/4 or Z/6, checks idempotence and the congruences,
and computes the rank separately over each residue field. It has no
calls to the integral stabilizer search or Smith routine.

For these moduli, constant local rank is also sufficient for lifting.
Over each local factor Z/p^e, the image and kernel of an idempotent are
free direct summands. A constant rank r allows their bases to be joined
by the Chinese remainder theorem, giving a conjugating matrix to
diag(I_r,0). Scale one column by the inverse determinant; this preserves
the projection and makes the matrix special linear. Elementary
generation of SL_2(Z/M), or local elimination followed by the Chinese
remainder theorem, lifts this matrix to SL_2(Z). Rank zero and rank two
give the zero and identity projections directly. Thus the oracle tests
both necessity and sufficiency for these controls. It is not a proposed
replacement for the general exact-containment algorithm.

## Exact summands and mixed centres

Five further controls retain conditions which a finite-ring-only check
would omit. In Z^3, forcing e1 into A and e2 into B leaves only a
rank-zero or rank-one complement. Requiring e3 in A modulo 2 and in B
modulo 3 is impossible, whereas assigning e2+e3 to A modulo 6 has an
integral shear solution.

For Z direct-sum C6, requiring (1,1) in A and (0,2) in B has a solution
with A generated by (1,1) and B equal to C6. The torsion shear is
essential. Requiring (2,1) in A and (0,3) in B is impossible: A has free
rank one, B contains the order-two torsion subgroup, and the torsion
component of twice a free generator cannot be odd in the remaining
order-two quotient. Finally, finite torsion cannot repair incompatible
free ranks at 2 and 3.

## Relator defects checked without Smith reduction

Eight systems over C4 direct-sum C6 use negative exponent sums,
noncoprime divisibility, dependent relators and zero rows. For a fixed
central splitting, one oracle enumerates all 24^2 changes of the two
generator lifts and tests the original relator defects directly. Its
answers agree with `relation_constraints`; the four positive cases
also pass `lifting_corrections` followed by substitution into the
original equations. The other four systems are negative.

GAP separately builds the abelian groups, enumerates the generator
changes as group elements, and tests subgroup membership for the
original defects. It checks all eight positive/negative decisions and
the four supplied correction witnesses. GAP also reconstructs all four
positive splittings from the preceding ten controls, checks generation,
trivial intersection, nontriviality and each original constraint.
