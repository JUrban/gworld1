# N5 audit notes

## Statement

The full original HTML and its N5 paragraph were read. The screenshot was inspected on 28 September 2026. The statement has no torsion-free restriction, no fixed-class restriction, and no named subpart. The proposed theorem assumes a finite presentation promised to define a finitely generated nilpotent group. It decides a nontrivial binary direct decomposition, which is the requested indecomposability property.

## Internal proof audit

- Rational completion is taken after quotienting by the finite torsion subgroup. No torsion group is embedded in a torsion-free completion.
- The kernel of G/Z(G) -> R/Z(R) is exactly the finite torsion subgroup of G/Z(G), not an unbounded central subgroup. The characteristic torsion-free power subgroup proves this explicitly.
- The bound on the quotient-factor search is |T(G/Z(G))|. Actual factors have this bounded index in the prescribed preimages because their remaining component is in this finite kernel.
- Rational partitions alone are insufficient: finite-index subgroups of the preimages are explicitly enumerated. Conversely, the algorithm never assumes that every enumerated quotient decomposition lifts.
- Cross commutators of full preimages are checked before central lifting; changing a lift by a central element cannot repair a failed check.
- All presentation relators are retained. Zero rows in their Smith form are genuine exact containment conditions. Positive diagonals impose divisibility modulo a chosen central factor; neither rational solvability nor a rank count suffices.
- The finite torsion part of the centre is handled by all its direct splittings and all homomorphisms from the free part into it. Such shears cover every embedding of the free summands, not just their abstract isomorphism types.
- For the free centre, exact containments require both disjoint saturations and primitive combined span. The stabilizer fixing that span pointwise is explicitly generated; its finite reduction modulo the lcm of all congruence moduli yields an actual stopping rule.
- Nontriviality is checked on both factors, including those with trivial quotient image. Finite and abelian groups are included rather than silently omitted.

This is an internal audit by the same research agent, not independent review.

## An arbitrary-lift obstruction

One shortcut encountered while reading the preceding torsion-free argument is invalid when read literally. This is recorded separately from the new proof and is not a claim that the published main theorem cannot be repaired.

Let A be the free nilpotent group of rank two and class three, generated by x,y, and let B be the integral Heisenberg group generated by u,v. Put G=A x B, c=[x,y], and t=[u,v]. The rational factors A^*,B^* match this supplied direct decomposition. For X_1=A Z(G), the elements x,y,ct project to the usual consistent polycyclic sequence of the Heisenberg quotient A/Z(A). For X_2=B Z(G), use u,v.

But H_1=<x,y,ct> contains t, because c is already [x,y]; H_2=<u,v> also contains t. This element has infinite order and is central in both. Therefore adjoining Z(A) to H_1 and Z(B) to H_2 does not yield disjoint factors, even though the original group is the indicated direct product. Both A and B lack nontrivial abelian direct factors, so deleting abelian factors in advance does not remove this example.

This contradicts the arbitrary-lift assertion as stated in Lemma 24 on printed p.13 of the archived Baumslag–Miller–Ostheimer text; the page was checked visually and retained as `literature/figures/N5-BMO-page13.png`. The apparent problematic step is the asserted collected expression using only the selected quotient lifts and Z(G_1): quotient relators can instead contribute central terms belonging to the other factor. The new proof avoids that assertion and solves for the central changes of lifts using all relator defects. Its imported rational uniqueness ingredients precede this lemma and have been audited separately.

`scripts/check_n5_lift_obstruction.g`, recorded in `results/n5-lift-obstruction-v1`, independently constructs the direct product with GAP nq, verifies the quotient has Hirsch length three and class two, and checks the common infinite-order central element. The mathematical calculation above is the reason for the control.

## Independently known expectations for the infinite test family

For m>=2 let

    G_m=<a,b,t | [a,b]=t, t^m=1, [a,t]=[b,t]=1>.

Normal forms show T(G_m)=G_m'=C_m, Q=G_m/T=Z^2, and Z(G_m)=<a^m,b^m,t>=Z^2 x C_m. This group is directly indecomposable. A finite direct factor would be contained in the cyclic derived group T; retraction onto that abelian factor would kill T, which is impossible for a nontrivial factor. If both factors were infinite, their torsion-free quotients would each have rank one. Each factor has central torsion and cyclic torsion-free quotient, hence is abelian (choose a lift of the cyclic generator). Their direct product would then be abelian, a contradiction.

Thus G_2,G_3,G_4 are negative controls with a proof separate from the decision implementation. G_1=Z^2 is positive, as are G_2 x Z, G_2 x C_2 and G_3 x C_2 by their displayed products. The central quotients are finite, so the complete quotient-decomposition enumeration can be tested for these infinite examples as well.

## Implementation coverage

The implementation covers the full central constraint and lift-correction stages. Finite central quotients permit a complete enumeration of the preceding quotient stage in the examples. The arbitrary rational-Lie decomposition and general bounded-index enumeration are established algorithms used in the proof, but are not implemented end to end here. No bounded computation is offered as a proof for every finitely generated nilpotent group.
