# F34(a): a candidate decision procedure for potential positivity

Developed 28 September 2026, following the F38(a) polynomial-identity
argument. This is a candidate answer in every finite rank; independent
specialist and novelty review remain outstanding. Rank two and the
stability assertion F34(b) are prior work.

## The precise question

Let F_r have specified free basis A={a_1,...,a_r}. A word is positive if
its reduced spelling uses only letters of A, with no inverse letters.
It is potentially positive if some automorphism of F_r sends it to a
positive word. We give a terminating decision procedure for this property,
uniformly in r and the input word w. Under the website's “no negative
exponents” definition the identity is positive. If “positive” is instead
taken to exclude the empty word, handle the identity separately as a
negative answer; nothing else changes.

Ranks zero and one are immediate: every nontrivial rank-one element can
be made a positive power by inverting the generator if necessary. Hence
assume r>=2 and w is nontrivial.

## Positivity reflected by an embedding

**Lemma.** Let phi:F_r->F_s be an injective homomorphism. If phi(w) is
positive in a specified basis of F_s, then w is potentially positive in F_r.

Let H=phi(F_r). Its finite based Stallings graph Gamma is the folded
inverse-labelled core graph recognizing the reduced words in H. Regard
each geometric edge as oriented in the direction carrying a positive
ambient generator. This is an orientation of every geometric edge:
the opposite direction carries the inverse generator. Loops are allowed.

Choose a maximal spanning tree T of the connected graph Gamma. For each
geometric edge e outside T, retain the positive orientation and define
the based loop b_e by following T from the basepoint to the origin of e,
crossing e, and returning along T from its terminus. These loops form a
free basis B of pi_1(Gamma), identified with H by the labelling immersion.

The positive reduced word phi(w) labels a closed path at the basepoint
of Gamma. Every traversed edge has its positive orientation. Collapsing
T to a point deletes the tree edges from this path and replaces every
remaining traversal by the corresponding positive letter b_e. It produces
a word in B with no inverses. No free cancellation can introduce inverse
letters; in fact the resulting nonempty positive spelling is already
reduced. It is nonempty because an immersion represents the nontrivial
element phi(w), whereas the empty collapsed word would represent 1.

Thus phi(w) is positive in the basis B of H. Since phi:F_r->H is an
isomorphism, its inverse images of B form a free basis of F_r in which w
is positive. Mapping that basis to A gives the required automorphism.
This proves the lemma. The graph can retain a basepoint stem if present;
collapsing a maximal tree is still valid. No finite-index or free-factor
hypothesis on H is required.

More generally the same proof handles any finite list of words whose
images are simultaneously positive: the same tree basis works for every
positive closed path. This observation is not counted as another answer.
No novelty is claimed for the elementary graph lemma itself. In
particular, its application to a free-factor inclusion followed by an
automorphism recovers the already known stability implication in F34(b).

## Reduction to a polynomial inequation

For endomorphism phi:F_r->F_r let B_phi be its integer abelianization
matrix. Nonzero determinant implies injectivity: H=phi(F_r) is free,
has rank at most r because it is generated by r images, and has rank at
least r because its image in ambient abelianization has rank r. Therefore
H has rank r, and Hopficity of F_r makes the surjection F_r->H injective.

Combining this fact with the lemma gives the exact equivalence

    w is potentially positive
       iff there exists phi in End(F_r) such that
              phi(w) is positive and det(B_phi) != 0.          (1)

For the forward implication take the witnessing automorphism; its
determinant is +/-1. For the reverse implication the determinant guarantees
injectivity and the graph lemma applies. A singular map sending w to a
positive word is not enough. For example the commutator maps to the
empty positive word under the trivial map, but no automorphism can send
a nontrivial commutator to a positive word: its abelianization is zero.

Introduce freely reduced variables X_1,...,X_r,V, the group equation

    w(X_1,...,X_r) = V,       V belongs to A*.                 (2)

The constraint on V is regular and forbids all inverse letters. Every
solution corresponds to an endomorphism with positive image of w, and
every such endomorphism gives a solution. Use the explicit cancellation
triangle reduction and Diekert--Elder Theorem 4.3 described in
[F38(a), Sections 2--3](../F38/part-a-proof.md) to compute a finite union
of full EDT0L relations for the tuples (X_1,...,X_r,V). This is an
effective construction in the prescribed alphabet with regular constraints.
For positivity the finite recognizing monoid may simply be augmented by
the Boolean flag recording whether any inverse letter occurs, together
with its involutive counterpart; alternatively use its finite automaton.

If c_(j,a) denotes the count of letter a in component X_j, define

    D(c) = det( c_(j,a_i) - c_(j,a_i^-1) )_(i,j=1,...,r).      (3)

This is a degree-r integer polynomial in separate component counts.
Apply the polynomial-identity decision procedure of F38(a), Section 3,
to every solution-relation branch. Answer **no** if D vanishes on all
of them; answer **yes** if any branch has a nonzero value. By (1)--(3)
this is exactly the requested answer. The empty solution relation
satisfies the identity vacuously and correctly gives no.

For clarity, the finite procedure behind this step is as follows.
Morphism incidence matrices act linearly on the stacked letter-count
vectors of the separately designated output letters. Lift this action
to all monomials of degree at most r. In each control-automaton state,
compute the rational span of reachable lifted vectors until stable.
There are only finitely many possible dimension increases. D is a linear
functional on this lift, and it vanishes on all accepted paths exactly
when it vanishes on the final-state spans. Reversing control paths when
necessary matches function-composition order. If the language convention
requires terminal filtering, incorporate the finite support-state product.
All steps terminate and use exact finite arithmetic.

## Optional witnesses and implementation limits

A nonzero final path provides explicit X_j and V, hence an injective
endomorphism phi. Fold the finite labelled graph of the X_j and choose
the basis B in the lemma. The positive spelling of V in B is obtained
by deleting its spanning-tree traversals. Apply the same graph reading
to each X_j and call its resulting B-coordinate word Y_j. Identifying
B with the standard basis of F_r, the map a_j->Y_j is the composition
F_r->H->F_r of two isomorphisms. It is therefore an explicit automorphism
and sends w to the computed positive word. No search for inverse images
of the tree basis is necessary. One can also
enumerate Nielsen automorphisms until a positive image of w appears,
which is guaranteed after the affirmative decision.

The proof imports the full equation-solution theorem; that large
construction has not been implemented in this experiment. The polynomial
stage is implemented and independently checked for F38(a). The present
additional checks concern the graph reflection lemma and exact positive
spellings. No practical running-time bound is asserted.

## Prior work and counting

Goldstein and Lee supplied rank-two decision algorithms, subsequently
improved by O'Connor. Clark--Goldstein proved the negative answer to
F34(b) in 2005. These are credited prior results, not new counts.

Dinowitz--Koch-Hyde--O'Connor--Olive, *Growth and Language Complexity of
Potentially Positive Elements of Free Groups*, arXiv:2512.13967,
Introduction and Section 7, explicitly describe higher-rank decidability
as unknown. The June 2026 paper Koch-Hyde--O'Connor--Olive, *Residually
Solvable One-Relator Groups*, arXiv:2606.13933, Question 1.5, again asks
the recognition question and discusses the prior rank-two algorithms.
These primary scope checks and bounded searches do not certify novelty.

The potentially new scope of this candidate is F34(a) in ranks r>=3.
Count it as one named-subpart/partial candidate, with no claim to have
newly answered F34(b) or to have established a novel theorem.
