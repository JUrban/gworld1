# F38(c): the precise shortening normalization

30 September 2026, during the closing review. This supplies an explicit
comparison between the identity-based trees in
[the candidate proof](bounded-proof.md) and the minimum-displacement
normalization used in Sela's source. It strengthens that interface; the
claimed theorem, finite decision procedure and novelty status are
unchanged. No new problem is counted.

## The fixed generating set

Keep the notation G=A*<z>, P=<u,z>, with A freely generated by
a_1,...,a_k and u nontrivial and filling A as a free factor. The injective
map h_m extends f_m:A→F_r and sends z to the additional target generator
z. The word f_m(u) is cyclically reduced, and f_m was minimized over
Aut(A/u). Put

    L_m = |f_m(u)| = ||f_m(u)|| >= 1,
    M_m = max_i |f_m(a_i)|,
    M_m > 2^m(1+L_m).

Use the single fixed, possibly redundant, generating set

    S = {a_1,...,a_k,u,z}.

This includes the parameters explicitly, as in the graded setup. Every
pointwise P-fixer preserves A and restricts to an element of Aut(A/u),
as established in the candidate proof. Since M_m>L_m and M_m>1, adding
u and z leaves the based maximum equal to M_m. No such precomposition
can lower the maximum on the a_i. Thus h_m is still shortest for the
graded modular subgroup with this generating set.

The failure of the desired bound does supply this sequence: since
1+L_m<=2L_m, choose the failing ratio above 2^(m+1) before performing
the fixed conjugation that cyclically reduces f_m(u) and the subsequent
Aut(A/u) minimization. Allowing arbitrary target conjugations in the
desired conclusion only makes that failure stronger.

## A uniform comparison of basepoints and scales

Let o be the identity vertex in the target Cayley tree. For a vertex x
put

    D_m(x) = max_(s in S) d(x,h_m(s)x),
    mu_m = min_x D_m(x).

The minimum exists: this is a nonempty set of nonnegative integer
values. Choose a minimizing vertex x_m. Write t=d(x,o).

The axes of f_m(u) and z meet precisely at o. In a tree, for two lines
meeting only at o, the projection of any x to at least one of the lines
is o. Otherwise the two projection segments would force a nontrivial
common segment or a cycle. Hence one of the two axes is distance t
from x. The displacement formula for a hyperbolic tree isometry gives

    D_m(x) >= 2t.

Choose a_i with |f_m(a_i)|=M_m. The triangle inequality gives

    M_m <= 2t + d(x,h_m(a_i)x) <= 2t + D_m(x).

Consequently

    M_m/2 <= mu_m <= M_m,
    d(x_m,o)/mu_m <= 1/2.                              (1)

The upper bound uses D_m(o)=M_m. These bounds improve the earlier
M_m/(|u|+1) estimate for this augmented generating set. In particular
mu_m tends to infinity. A subsequence can be chosen with strictly
increasing mu_m, so the maps belong to distinct target-conjugacy classes
as required in the source's unbounded construction.

Conjugating h_m by x_m^-1 and rescaling by mu_m is exactly the source's
minimum-displacement normalization. Equivalently, leave h_m unchanged
and view the tree with basepoint x_m and scale mu_m. The original
identity point remains within normalized distance 1/2.

## The identity point belongs to a fixed finite orbit hull

Bounded distance alone should not be used to assume a point survives
in a chosen equivariant limit. Here there is a direct finite-hull reason.
For some s_m in {u,z}, the projection of x_m to the axis of h_m(s_m) is
o. The segment from x_m to h_m(s_m)x_m contains that projection, so

    o belongs to [x_m,h_m(s_m)x_m].                     (2)

Pass to a subsequence with s_m equal to one fixed s. In the rescaled
trees, (2) places o in the hull of two points from a fixed finite part
of the x_m orbit. Its position along the segment has a convergent
subsequence by (1). It therefore determines a point o_infinity in the
same equivariant limit used by the source. For every fixed g in G,
h_m(g)o lies in the corresponding segment with endpoints
h_m(g)x_m and h_m(gs)x_m; those points also lie in a fixed finite orbit
hull. No larger auxiliary action or extra limiting branches are assumed.

The parameter displacements at o satisfy

    d(o,h_m(u)o)/mu_m = L_m/mu_m <= 2 L_m/M_m -> 0,
    d(o,h_m(z)o)/mu_m = 1/mu_m -> 0.

Thus P fixes o_infinity in this precise normalized limit. It need not
fix the minimizing basepoint itself.

## A nondegenerate tripod in the same limit

After another subsequence, fix i with |f_m(a_i)|=M_m and let
M_m/mu_m converge to rho in [1,2]. The three points

    h_m(a_i)o,  h_m(za_i)o,  h_m(z^-1a_i)o

are all covered by the finite-hull observation above. Their unscaled
pairwise distances are

    2M_m+1,  2M_m+1,  2M_m+2.

This follows directly from free-product normal forms: the a_i image
uses only the F_r letters, whereas the separating z powers use the
additional letter. Their normalized pairwise distances all tend to
2rho, giving a tripod whose three legs have length rho>=1.

An element in the kernel of the limit action fixes this tripod.
Sela's Lemma 1.3(iii), with its elementary center-of-tripod argument,
then places it in the kernel of the conjugated h_m for all sufficiently
large m. These maps remain injective. Hence the action kernel is
trivial, and the canonical quotient is G itself.

Passing to subsequences preserves the flexible-sequence inequality:
when a retained original index m_j is relabeled j, m_j>=j and the
j-ball contains S. Thus the original bound on M_(m_j) implies the
required 2^j ball bound. The source's rigid/solid proper-quotient
conclusion now applies to exactly this normalized limit, yielding the
contradiction used in the candidate proof.

Shortness is asserted for the original h_m with its fixed parameter
images. The conjugation above is part of the limit normalization;
this argument does **not** assert that arbitrary target conjugation
preserves based graded shortness. The no-relative-free-product-quotient
and rigid/solid checks remain those in the original proof and its
earlier source audit.
