# F38(a): a candidate decision procedure in every finite rank

Argument developed 28 September 2026, before the original experiment deadline.
This is a candidate proof, awaiting independent specialist and novelty review.
It answers the named subpart (a); it does not settle (c).

## Statement and dependencies

Fix a basis A={a_1,...,a_r} of F_r. Write ||w|| for cyclically reduced
word length in this basis. The question is whether, given u,v, one can
decide

    ||alpha(u)|| = ||alpha(v)|| for every alpha in Aut(F_r).       (1)

The proposed answer is yes, uniformly in the finite rank and input words.
Rank zero is trivial; in rank one compare the absolute exponents. If
exactly one input is the identity, answer no; if both are, answer yes.
We henceforth consider r>=2 and nontrivial inputs.

We use two established results, with their precise roles separated:

1. Kapovich--Levitt--Schupp--Shpilrain, *Translation equivalence in free
   groups*, Corollary 1.4: (1) is equivalent to equality of the two cyclic
   lengths under every injective endomorphism. The archived 2005 preprint
   states this on page 4. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409284).
2. Diekert--Elder, *Solutions to twisted word equations and equations in
   virtually free groups*, Theorem 4.3, specialized to the trivial twisting
   group: the complete solution relation of a finite system of word
   equations with involution and regular constraints has an effective
   description

       { (h(d_1),...,h(d_k)) : h in R },                         (2)

   where C is a finite extended alphabet, d_i are specified letters of C,
   and R is given by a finite automaton whose edges are explicit monoid
   endomorphisms of C*. The outputs are words over the original alphabet.
   The archived arXiv v6 (26 February 2022) states the theorem on page 14
   and defines effective EDT0L relations on page 12.
   [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03297).

The full solution relation, distinct output positions and effective
finite control in (2) are essential. A description of only some solutions,
or an uncontrolled freely reduced image of a language, would not suffice.
Theorem 14.2 in the same paper provides the group-equation version. Below
we give the elementary reduction to Theorem 4.3, keeping the prescribed
basis and uniformity in rank explicit. Ciobanu--Diekert--Elder's earlier
Theorem 4 also provides the separate-output-letter representation for
unconstrained free-group equations; the constrained version above is the
dependency actually used here.

## 1. A determinant condition sufficient for injectivity

For an endomorphism phi:F_r->F_r let B_phi be its integer matrix on
abelianization, with column j the exponent sums in phi(a_j).

**Lemma.** If det(B_phi) is nonzero, phi is injective.

Let H=phi(F_r). Nielsen--Schreier makes H a free group of rank s<=r,
since it is generated by r elements. Its image in the ambient Z^r has
rank r, so its own abelianization has rank at least r. Consequently s=r.
Composing the surjection F_r->H with an isomorphism H->F_r gives a
surjective endomorphism of F_r. Finitely generated free groups are Hopfian,
so this map, and hence phi, is injective. This uses the image subgroup's
rank, not an assertion that all endomorphisms with singular abelianization
are noninjective.

It follows that (1) is equivalent to

    det(B_phi) ( ||phi(u)|| - ||phi(v)|| ) = 0
                       for every endomorphism phi:F_r->F_r.    (3)

Indeed, (1) and KLSS imply equality of lengths whenever the determinant is
nonzero; at determinant zero the product vanishes automatically. Conversely,
every automorphism has determinant +1 or -1, so (3) implies (1).
No constraint det=1, det=-1, or an infinite list of injectivity inequations
is imposed on the solution language.

## 2. Encode the quantities in a full equation-solution relation

Use variables X_1,...,X_r,P,Q,U,V in F_r and the equations

    P^-1 u(X_1,...,X_r) P = U,
    Q^-1 v(X_1,...,X_r) Q = V.                                (4)

Require the reduced words U,V to be cyclically reduced; the empty word
is permitted. This is a regular language over A union A^-1: forbid adjacent
inverse letters, and, for nonempty words, forbid the last letter from being
the inverse of the first. All X_j,P,Q are represented by reduced words.
Every tuple X_1,...,X_r extends to a solution of (4), by cyclic reduction.
For every such solution, |U|=||phi(u)|| and |V|=||phi(v)|| for the
endomorphism phi(a_j)=X_j. Thus no endomorphisms are lost, and no spurious
length values are added.

For completeness, reduce any finite system of free-group equations in
reduced-word variables to word equations with involution as follows.
Expand each equation to a product equal to 1. Introduce reduced prefix
variables Z_j, with Z_0=Z_m=1, and triangular equations Z_(j-1) T_j=Z_j,
where T_j is one original variable, its inverse, or a constant letter.
For each triangle XY=Z, introduce fresh variables S,T,R and replace it by
the three **monoid** equations

    X=S T,       Y=bar(T) R,       Z=S R.                     (5)

Here bar reverses the word and inverts each letter. Impose reduced-word
constraints on all variables. Maximal cancellation in a product of two
reduced words proves that every group solution extends to (5): T is the
suffix of X cancelled by the prefix of Y. Conversely, (5) immediately
implies XY=Z in the free group. Monoid equality with the reduced word Z
ensures the surviving concatenation S R is reduced. Constants and empty
prefixes are fixed by monoid equations. This proves equality of the
projected solution relations, not just equisatisfiability.

The regular constraints can be supplied in the exact finite-monoid form
of Theorem 4.3. Combine their finite recognizing monoids. To respect
involution, replace a recognizing morphism mu_0 by

    w |-> (mu_0(w), mu_0(bar(w))) in M x M^op,

with involution interchanging the coordinates. Each regular constraint
specifies a finite set of allowed values. Enumerate the finitely many
compatible assignments of such values to variables, apply Theorem 4.3
to each, and take the finite union. This is effective and preserves the
original alphabet. Unused X_j may be retained with tautological equations.

In this application one may use an especially small recognizing monoid:
an identity for the empty word, an absorbing zero for an unreduced word,
and a pair (first,last) for each nonempty reduced word. Multiplying two
nonidentity pairs gives zero when the adjacent boundary letters are inverse,
and otherwise retains the first and last outer letters. Reversal/inversion
sends (a,b) to (b^-1,a^-1). Reduced words are precisely the nonzero values;
cyclically reduced words additionally exclude inverse first/last letters.
Thus a concrete monoid with (2r)^2+2 elements suffices for these constraints.

Project (2) to the r+2 variables X_1,...,X_r,U,V by retaining their
designated letters; auxiliary variables need not be output. Each branch
is therefore an effective relation of exactly these tuples of reduced words.

Let x_(j,a) count occurrences of a in component X_j; use analogous counts
for U,V. On this relation, define the rational (indeed integer) polynomial

    P(x) = det( x_(j,a_i) - x_(j,a_i^-1) )_(i,j=1,...,r)
           * sum_(a in A union A^-1) ( x_(U,a) - x_(V,a) ).   (6)

It is homogeneous of degree r+1. Coordinates for extended nonterminal
letters have coefficient zero. Since accepted outputs are actual reduced
words over the original alphabet, (6) evaluates to the expression in (3).

## 3. Polynomial identities on effective EDT0L relations

**Lemma.** Given a relation (2) and a rational polynomial P in the
letter counts of its separate components, one can decide whether P
vanishes at every tuple in the relation.

Here is a finite exact algorithm and proof. For each generating
endomorphism h on an automaton edge, form the nonnegative integer incidence
matrix M_h, whose (a,b) entry counts a in h(b). Stack the count vectors
of the k output components into y in N^D, where D=k|C|. Applying h to
each component sends y to

    T_h y,       T_h = diag(M_h,...,M_h).

Initially component i is the one-letter word d_i. This stacking tracks
different components even if some d_i coincide. It requires no closure
theorem about arbitrary transductions or positions of inserted separators.

If the control automaton reads h_1,...,h_m for the composition
h_1 composed with ... composed with h_m, reverse the automaton, interchange
its initial and final states, and apply its edge matrices in traversal
order. The first applied matrix is then M_(h_m), as required. Multiple
initial states and identity/epsilon transitions cause no difficulty.

Put d=deg(P), and list all monomials in D coordinates of total degree at
most d, including 1. Write nu_d(y) for their values. Its dimension is
N=binomial(D+d,d). Expansion of products of linear forms effectively gives
a rational matrix T_h^[d] satisfying the polynomial identity

    nu_d(T_h y) = T_h^[d] nu_d(y)       for every y.            (7)

At each automaton state q maintain a rational vector subspace W_q of Q^N.
Initialize it with nu_d(y_0) at every initial state and zero elsewhere.
For every edge q --h--> q', repeatedly include T_h^[d] W_q in W_(q').
Exact rational Gaussian elimination decides whether an added vector
enlarges the subspace. There are at most N times the number of states
strict dimension increases, so this reaches a fixed point effectively.

At that fixed point W_q is **exactly** the span of nu_d(y) for paths from
initial states to q. One inclusion follows by induction on path length.
For the other, the iteration only adds spans of actual path vectors, by
(7). A basis can in fact be retained as actual path vectors, rather than
their row-reduced replacements, recording their paths for witnesses.

Write P as a linear functional ell_P on these monomial coordinates.
It vanishes on every accepted output if and only if it vanishes on W_q
for every final state. This is a finite rational linear-algebra test.
If it fails, some retained actual path vector at a final state has
nonzero evaluation; replaying that path gives a counterexample tuple.
An empty relation satisfies the identity vacuously.

Some EDT0L conventions additionally intersect generated words with the
terminal alphabet. The representation in dependency (2) already guarantees
terminal outputs on accepted paths. If an implementation instead permits
nonterminal outputs, augment control states with the finite support of y:
under a nonnegative incidence matrix, the support updates deterministically
by taking unions of letter-image supports. Accept only terminal supports.
This handles erasing morphisms and empty outputs, and prevents nonterminal
letters from being silently ignored by (6).

The lemma decides universal polynomial equality, equivalently the existence
of a **nonzero** polynomial value. It does not decide whether an arbitrary
polynomial has a zero among the outputs. In particular it does not solve
word equations with additional existential length-equality constraints.

## 4. Decision algorithm and scope

Construct the finitely many relations from (4)--(5). For each, apply the
lemma to polynomial (6). Answer yes precisely when it vanishes on every
relation. Sections 1 and 2 identify this answer with (1). All constructions
are finite and effective, so this is a terminating uniform algorithm.

A nonzero output yields an injective endomorphism distinguishing the two
cyclic lengths. It need not itself be an automorphism. If an automorphism
witness is desired, enumerate products of elementary Nielsen automorphisms
until a distinguishing one appears; KLSS guarantees termination following
a negative decision. This optional witness search is not needed for the
decision procedure.

No usable running-time bound or full implementation of the recompression
construction is claimed here. The proposed implementation checks only the
finite polynomial-identity step and elementary reductions. The general
equation-solution construction is an explicit imported theorem.

F38(b) has prior work by Donghi Lee. The rank-two instance of (a) also has
a prior algorithm by Lee; the potentially new scope here is r>=3.
Shpilrain's survey (2025 preprint, published 17 June 2026), Section 5,
still poses the general decision question. A GAGTA 2026 abstract by
Siobhan O'Connor announces a new fast rank-two algorithm; this is also
prior work, not part of the potentially new range. These dated sources
and limited current searches do not establish novelty.
F38(c), which asks for uniform two-sided bounds rather than equality,
does not follow from the polynomial-identity lemma and remains unresolved
in this investigation. Count this, if retained after audit, as one named
subpart/partial candidate, never a whole-entry F38 resolution.
