\section{Research and verification}\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{Input and working method}
The agent received the archived problem pages, their background notes
and the Hall of Fame. Preparation retained publisher bytes, per-entry
fragments, rendered statements and hashes. It recorded 49 heading stars,
11 entries with subpart stars and 69 Hall of Fame links referring to
58 distinct entries. These are historical annotations, not an exhaustive
current-status survey. For example, M0 is a cross-reference to F3 with
``free'' replaced by ``free metabelian''; both statements must be read.
The intended fixed-group quantifier in N9(a) comes from its background.

The run used a fresh Git repository and clock while retaining lessons
from Kourovka. It was not a clean-room experiment. The transfer ledger
records no imported Kourovka mathematical argument or research code;
shared software and the process-recording approach are disclosed in
the preparation provenance. The paper's layout and author footnotes are
adapted after the deadline from the Kourovka manuscript.

Work alternated between statement checks, primary-source searches,
structural arguments and exact computations. Failed strategies and
script failures were retained. The nilpotent commutator investigation
progressed from restricted classes and leading terms to the proposed
general recursion. The braid investigation produced an infinite family
but did not exhaust the four-strand case. The growth investigation
improved its lower bound through increasingly complete families of
explicit words; its upper bound came from a separate rewriting argument.

\subsection{What the checks establish}
Every counted entry has a written argument and an audit describing its
statement, dependencies, bibliographic comparisons and checks. Closing
passes reread all twelve controlling arguments. These were performed by
the same research agent. Separately written GAP reconstructions use
different arithmetic or group representations, but remain computational
checks organized by that agent; they are not an outside mathematical review.

Lean verifies delimited universal statements for F28, F34/F38, N8 and
N9. For example, the F28 formalization covers the integral-matrix
recurrence, not the Schreier and free-group bridge. The shared language
formalization covers finite-dimensional span reachability and termination,
not the full equation-language theorem. N8 has substantial formal work on
its positional separation argument, but the group-to-Lie interface and
the complete solver are outside the formal verification. No entire
candidate in Tables~\ref{tab:whole}--\ref{tab:partial} is claimed to have
been fully formalized. Appendix~\ref{app:certificates} gives representative
checks and their boundaries.

Several candidates depend on deep mathematics. The highest-priority
interfaces are the full equation-solution construction in F34/F38(a),
graded shortening in F38(c), rational support and integral lifting in N5,
the all-rank structural argument in N8, the arbitrary-definable-set input
in F41, the general ordered-tree collapse in GA3 and the fixed Diophantine
encoding in N9. Finite examples cannot verify these implications.
